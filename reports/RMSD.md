# Hard site gates beat stronger pose gradients

No published binder-design pipeline enforces a binding site by making a geometric loss term heavier, and the one controlled measurement that exists cuts hard against the idea: in the ESMFold hallucination study the RMSD term was left **10-fold dominant over pLDDT and still needed ~5× more optimisation steps** to converge, with low RMSD "not guaranteed" ([Jeliazkov et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.05.23.541774v1.full)). What working pipelines do instead is stack four mechanistically distinct layers — model **conditioning**, a weak **gradient-time loss**, mid-trajectory **geometric rejection**, and a hard site-specific **selection filter** — and both of the pipelines closest to your setup (Germinal and BindCraft2) put the decisive enforcement in the *last* layer, gating on boolean geometry *before* any confidence metric is consulted. Your configuration — site pressure only in the gradient, retention ranked on a site-agnostic confidence score — is the one combination none of the surveyed pipelines uses. That is sufficient to explain your measurements without invoking anything anomalous: ipSAE is computed over *all* binder×target residue pairs that pass a PAE cutoff, so a 36-50 residue interface anywhere on the surface saturates it, and an AF3-class predictor will supply one. Independent confirmation exists at scale: a 2026 benchmark over 3,401 SAbDab complexes plus 23,798 negatives found AF3 **"hallucinate[s] plausible antibody–target interfaces on decoy proteins, distributing predicted epitopes across much of a folded target's surface,"** with an **innate false-positive rate of ~3%** ([bioRxiv 2026.07.30.741792](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.30.741792v1)) — so your permuted-epitope decoy outscoring the real target at matched compute is a known model pathology, not a local bug. The fix the literature supports is not a bigger or scheduled pose weight. It is a 10 Å hotspot-to-CDR abort criterion during search, an epitope-masked confidence metric for ranking, and a hard geometric gate in selection — all of which exist in released code with published parameter values.

## Scope: this is a controllability question, not a design question

Read this as evidence about **optimizer controllability**, not about antibody
engineering. The project it was commissioned for is a proof of concept for
gradients ([P17_JN1.md §19](../docs/P17_JN1.md)): the question is whether a
frozen predictor's gradient with respect to binder sequence can restore that
predictor's own two readouts — interface confidence and target-aligned binder
pose — from a degraded start. Confidence recovers (0.00 → 0.77 against a
reference of 0.795–0.807); pose does not (48 → 14 Å against a reference of
2.6 Å). "Epitope" throughout this report is simply the field's name for a
*specified geometric target*, and the useful question the literature answers is
by what mechanism any pipeline makes a search converge on a specified
arrangement rather than any plausible one.

Three consequences for how to read what follows:

- **Published success rates are not the yardstick.** They measure complete
  pipelines — generation, inverse folding, filtering, wet-lab triage — under
  budget accounting that is not commensurable with a score-call count. Affinity,
  expression and developability are out of scope entirely. What transfers is
  mechanism: where in a pipeline site specification lives, and in what
  functional form.
- **The field cannot calibrate this proof of concept.** No pipeline publishes a
  site-recovery rate; fewer than ~10 designed complexes have been solved
  experimentally, all drawn post hoc from confirmed binders; there is no
  published false-positive rate or threshold for ipSAE, and no published
  distribution of it over *designed* rather than natural complexes. These are
  holes in the literature, not failures of this search, and they are stated as
  such at each point below.
- **The two findings that matter most here are negative ones.** That interface
  confidence measures the self-consistency of a packing rather than the
  nativeness of a location is what makes a confidence-ranked search unable to
  carry a pose claim at all — a statement about the metric, independent of any
  biology. And that escalating a geometric coefficient fails wherever it has
  been measured closes the "stronger weight" hypothesis directly.

A proof-of-concept-framed summary of these findings, with the implementable
items mapped to the host repository, is in
[`research_notes/Epitope targeting in binder design/README.md`](../research_notes/Epitope%20targeting%20in%20binder%20design/README.md).

## Site enforcement lives in four layers, and your pipeline occupies one

The single most useful organising fact in this literature is that "epitope targeting" names four unrelated mechanisms, and papers rarely distinguish them. **Conditioning** is an input tensor the generative model was *trained* to respond to; it creates no gradient on the design objective. **Gradient-time loss** adds a differentiable term that pulls the binder toward named residues. **In-trajectory rejection** is a mid-generation geometric check that aborts and reseeds. **Selection filtering** is a post-hoc, site-aware acceptance test on finished designs. Conflating the first two is the standard error — RFdiffusion hotspots are often described as a "constraint" when they are a learned prior — and conflating the last two is what leaves pipelines like yours exposed.

RFdiffusion sits purely in the conditioning layer: `ppi.hotspot_res=[A30,A33,A34]` writes a one-hot channel into the target's 1-D template features of a binder-finetuned checkpoint, where a hotspot was *defined* during training as a target residue within **10 Å Cβ of the binder**, and critically **only 0-20% of the true hotspots were shown to the model**, the rest masked ([RFdiffusion README](https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFdiffusion)). The model is therefore explicitly trained to expect the real interface to extend well beyond whatever you supply; 3-6 hotspots constrain the interface *centre* loosely and cannot pin its *extent*. The authors' own framing of why the feature exists is your failure mode verbatim — how do you "guarantee the binder will go to the intended interface site on the surface of the target, and not target the tantalizing hydrophobic patch you have just artificially created?" That hotspot-only conditioning is the limiting factor is best shown by its successor: adding a second, geometrically sharper conditioning channel specifying *which target β-strand to pair with* lifted in-silico success from **0.98% to 9.2%** ([Nat Commun 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67866-3)).

RFantibody strengthens the same channel and then adds the layer nobody else has. The hotspot bit enters at `features['t1d'][...,22] = self.ab_item.hotspots` with `preprocess.hotspot_dim: 23` ([model_runners.py:530](https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFantibody/blob/main/src/rfantibody/rfdiffusion/inference/model_runners.py)); the training definition is CDR-specific — a target residue whose **average Cβ distance to the closest 5 CDR residues is under 8 Å** — and **0-100% of hotspots can be shown**, versus RFdiffusion's 20% ceiling, which is why the authors warn RFantibody "is more sensitive to exactly which hotspots are selected" ([RFantibody README](https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFantibody)). The transferable mechanism is `terminate_bad_targeting`: inside the reverse-diffusion loop, on the predicted *clean* structure `px0` at a chosen timestep, the code computes Cβ distances from every hotspot to every designed loop residue, takes the per-hotspot minimum, and if the closest hotspot is further than `hotspot_termination_threshold: 10` Å from any designed loop it prints "Not targeting correctly," discards the trajectory, and after `hotspot_termination_failures_permitted: 20` failures aborts the whole job as badly specified ([rfdiffusion_inference.py:158-178](https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFantibody/blob/main/scripts/rfdiffusion_inference.py)). It is off by default (`terminate_bad_targeting: null`) and no paper states which timestep the published campaigns used — an unresolved gap — but the existence of a dedicated failure counter implies the authors saw this fail routinely. They also made their RF2 re-prediction filter epitope-aware via `--hotspot-show-prop` (default 0.1), and still conclude that **"the lack of an effective filter is the main limitation of the RFantibody pipeline at the moment."**

AlphaProteo takes the target structure plus *optional* hotspots as conditioning and describes its filter only as "a model or procedure that predicts whether a design will bind" — **no site-specificity filter is described anywhere in the accessible text**, and intended-interface verification was retrospective and experimental: "we measured binding of our top binders after mutating 1-3 residues at the target-binding interface" ([arXiv:2409.08022v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08022v1)). Per-target experimental success ranged from **88% (BHRF1, n=94) to 0% (TNFα, n=54)**. That even DeepMind resolved the epitope question by mutagenesis rather than by a computational check is a strong external signal about how much weight a confidence metric can carry.

On the loss side, EvoBind is the purest case: `--receptor_if_residues` feeds a term that is literally `loss = mean_over_peptide_atoms(min_dist_to_epitope_atoms) / mean_peptide_pLDDT`, with no weight, no schedule and no flat bottom ([mc_design.py](https://github.com/patrickbryant1/EvoBind/blob/master/src/mc_design.py)). EvoBind2's headline feature is the inverse of what you need: target residues become optional with **default = all receptor residues**, on the stated rationale that specifying a site imposes "preconceived notions" ([Commun Chem 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42004-025-01601-3)) — a published instance of exactly the degenerate objective you are optimising. EasyNano is the only pipeline with an explicit metric-space epitope term: **ℒ_epitope = (1/|𝒞|) Σ_{i∈𝒞} ELU(min_{j∈ℰ} d̄_ij − 8.0)** over CDR residues 𝒞 and epitope residues ℰ on expected Cβ distances, weight **0.2** in a six-term loss, plus a *pose prior* that pins the framework with a binary mask over a 64-bin 2-22 Å distogram at 2.5 Å tolerance, weight 0.05 ([arXiv 2606.12772](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12772v1)). Its central caveat is the one that matters most to you: a wrong starting pose is unrecoverable — anti-TNF, docked **44 Å from the crystal pose**, gained only **+0.024 ipTM** despite extensive CDR mutation. EasyNano has **no experimental validation** and reports only self-scored ipTM deltas on six target–framework pairs.

BindEnergyCraft deserves explicit mention because it is the nearest thing to an "improve the gradient" fix and it does not address your problem. It replaces ipTM with **E_pTMEnergy = −(1/|ℐ|) Σ_{(i,j)∈ℐ} log Σ_b g(d_b)·exp ℓ_ijb**, a LogSumExp over PAE logits that "preserves gradients across the interface" where ipTM "zeroes out the gradient from all but one position," at the same 0.05 weight BindCraft gives ipTM ([arXiv 2505.21241](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21241v1)). Its benchmarks were run explicitly **"without applying any hotspot constraints,"** and it is computational-only. A denser interface-confidence gradient makes it *easier* to find a high-confidence interface anywhere, so adding pTMEnergy without a site term should be expected to worsen off-epitope docking. Its one gift is that ℐ is an explicit inter-chain pair set, so defining ℐ as binder×epitope rather than binder×target yields a site-specific pTMEnergy in one line — an unexploited and directly implementable modification.

The two pipelines in the gradient-through-predictor family that actually hit their epitopes share a structure worth stating plainly: **continuous, weak site pressure in the gradient; discrete, hard site gates in selection.** Germinal's gradient term is nothing more than AfDesign's hotspot-masked `i_con` at weight **0.2** with `inter_contact_number` raised to **10** at a **20 Å cutoff** ([configs/run/vhh.yaml](https://github.com/SantiagoMille/germinal/blob/main/configs/run/vhh.yaml)) — the same weak pressure you already have, if not weaker. What differs is `configs/filter/initial/vhh.yaml`, which contains exactly five filters: `clashes < 1`, **`binder_near_hotspot == true`**, **`cdr3_hotspot_contacts > 0`**, **`percent_interface_cdr > 0.5`**, and `interface_shape_comp > 0.6` — three of five are site- or paratope-specific and **there is no confidence metric at all** ([initial/vhh.yaml](https://github.com/SantiagoMille/germinal/blob/main/configs/filter/initial/vhh.yaml)). The booleans come from `compute_hotspot_proximity()` with `distance_threshold = 5.3`, `contact_distance = 6.0`, `min_hotspot_contacts = 3` ([filter_utils.py](https://github.com/SantiagoMille/germinal/blob/main/germinal/filters/filter_utils.py)). A design 14-27 Å off-epitope dies there, instantly, regardless of ipTM or ipSAE. And Germinal *computes* `ipsae` in `build_filter_metrics()` yet does **not** list it among the VHH thresholds — the authors treated confidence and site as separate, non-substitutable axes. Germinal also carries the only *negative* site term at gradient time, **L_paratope ∝ L_CDR − λ·L_framework**, in which framework-to-target proximity is actively subtracted ([Germinal preprint v3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.19.677421v3.full)); the analogous construction for your problem — `L_contact(binder, epitope) − λ·L_contact(binder, non-epitope surface)` — is implemented by no pipeline at gradient time, only as a BindCraft2 filter.

BindCraft deserves a specific warning because its one filter whose name suggests a site check is not one. `Hotspot_RMSD` is computed as `unaligned_rmsd(trajectory_pdb, mpnn_design_pdb, binder_chain, binder_chain)` — a superposition-free PyRosetta `RMSDMetric` between the MPNN-redesigned binder and *its own hallucinated trajectory*, both predicted on the same target template ([bindcraft.py:264](https://github.com/martinpacesa/BindCraft/blob/main/bindcraft.py), [pyrosetta_utils.py:176-206](https://github.com/martinpacesa/BindCraft/blob/main/functions/pyrosetta_utils.py)). With a threshold of **6 Å** ([default_filters.json](https://github.com/martinpacesa/BindCraft/blob/main/settings_filters/default_filters.json)) it measures trajectory→MPNN self-consistency; if the trajectory docked off-epitope, the filter *rewards* reproducing the wrong site faithfully. BindCraft 1's post-trajectory gate is no better — `len(hotspot_residues(pdb)) >= 3` where `hotspot_residues(pdb, binder_chain="B", atom_distance_cutoff=4.0)` counts *any* binder residue within 4 Å of *any* chain-A atom. Nothing in BindCraft 1's selection stage references the user's epitope. BindCraft2 closes exactly this gap, adding `Hotspot_Contact_Fraction`, `Epitope_Residues_Contacted`, **`Off_Epitope_Contact_Fraction`**, `Coldspot_Contact_Fraction`, plus `Off_Paratope_Contact_Fraction` and `Framework_Packing_Fraction` for antibodies ([BindCraft2 reference.md](https://github.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/blob/main/docs/source/reference.md)). Their default numeric thresholds were not recovered and several off-target filters are explicitly unset by default — verify before relying on them.

One further intervention class is worth separating out because it costs no loss engineering at all: **input-space manipulation**. BindCraft2's `forced_targeting` "replaces exposed residues outside the protected hotspot shell with lysine, then restores the original target before hardening and validation," with `forced_targeting_shell` default **10 Å**; `lysinate_target()` mutates every surface-exposed residue outside `epitope_residues(atoms, atom_mask, hotspot_residues, epitope_cutoff)` to lysine ([epitope_targeting.py:21-83](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/bindcraft/epitope_targeting.py)). Separately, trimming the target to **discontinuous epitope fragments only** reportedly "significantly accelerated sampling and improved design quality while preserving validation performance," with success improving as the epitope was narrowed ([arXiv 2509.25479](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25479)) — though this claim comes from a search snippet and was **not verified against the full text**. Both make off-epitope poses unattractive or geometrically unavailable to the frozen predictor without touching a single loss weight, which makes them unusually well suited to a sequence-gradient pipeline: they are interventions on the model's input, where your pipeline is already free to act.

## ipSAE's mask, not its reduction, is why 0.77 is meaningless

Your retention metric is doing something structurally different from what you need, and the reason is in the formula rather than in a threshold. ipSAE applies `f(PAE, d0) = 1/(1 + (PAE/d0)²)` with the Yang–Skolnick form `d0 = max(1.0, 1.24·(L−15)^(1/3) − 1.8)`, over a pair set defined as `valid_pairs_matrix = np.outer(chains == chain1, chains == chain2) & (pae_matrix < pae_cutoff)`, then — in the headline `d0res` variant — averages `f` across each residue's valid partners, takes `argmax` over all chain-1 residues to get `asym`, and takes the **maximum of the two chain directions** ([ipsae.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DunbrackLab/IPSAE/main/ipsae.py)). The published implementation has a strictly four-positional-argument CLI with **no chain-selection flag, no residue-list flag and no epitope file**, and no `min` variant exists.

This matters because you have already applied the two obvious hardenings. Taking the *mean* instead of `max_i`, and the *minimum* instead of the maximum over directions, are both stricter than published ipSAE — and they changed nothing, because the pair set is still every binder residue against every target residue. **The decisive object is the mask, not the reduction.** The `valid_pairs_matrix` never references the intended site, so a confident, self-consistent 36-50 residue interface built anywhere on the surface passes through it untouched. Two independent mechanistic accounts converge on this. The CAPRI 47-55 assessment finds that for antibody–antigen targets "the confidence score for multimers is calculated 80% between chains, heavily biasing it toward the antibody chains rather than antibody-antigen relative positions" ([Proteins 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/prot.26801), reported via search summary). And the ipSAE paper itself is explicit that its claim is about separating **true from false complexes** more efficiently than ipTM — a direct full-text read found it **does not address whether correct interface geometry accompanies a high ipSAE, reports no AUC, proposes no numerical interaction threshold, and quantifies no false-positive rate** ([ipSAE preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.10.637595v2.full)). Your 0.77 versus a native 0.795 is not a contradiction of that paper; it is outside its validated claim, and a 0.025 gap is well inside the noise of a metric with no published null distribution — which your permuted-epitope control is in fact supplying.

Note also a smaller calibration detail: the ipSAE README uses PAE cutoffs of **15 Å for AF2 and 10 Å for AF3/Boltz**. Your 12 Å sits above the AF3-class convention, admitting more pairs into an already site-agnostic set.

The literature contains no epitope-restricted confidence metric under any name — **this is a genuine gap, not something I failed to find a citation for** — but every component exists and three routes are one line of code each. The best starting point is BindCraft2's **i_pDAE**, which is already the pipeline's ranking metric (`RANKING_METRIC = 'i_pDAE'`) and is ipSAE with the confidence-based mask replaced by a *geometric* one:

```python
def tm_score_distance_scale(partner_count):
    return jnp.maximum(1.24 * (jnp.maximum(partner_count, 19.0) - 15.0) ** (1/3) - 1.8, 1.0)

def anchored_interface_tm_scores(pae, contact):
    partner_counts = contact.sum(-1).astype(jnp.float32)
    tm_scores = 1.0 / (1.0 + (pae / tm_score_distance_scale(partner_counts)[:, None]) ** 2)
    return jnp.where(contact, tm_scores, 0.0).sum(-1) / jnp.maximum(partner_counts, 1.0)
```

with `contact = (CA-CA distance ≤ 8.0) & resolved` ([BindCraft2 filters.py:118-169](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/bindcraft/filters.py)). Because the mask is geometric it *cannot* be satisfied by confident-but-distant pairs, it is passed as an explicit boolean matrix so `contact & epitope_mask[None, :]` is a legitimate restriction, and a per-residue track already exists (`tracks['i_pDAE'] = interface`). Its `.max()` over both directions has ipSAE's site-agnosticism problem, so restrict the mask *and* replace the max with a mean over epitope residues plus a minimum-contacting-epitope-residue requirement. The cheapest alternative is **LIS** — keep interchain PAE entries under 12, map each to `(12 − PAE)/12`, mean over survivors only — which has *no length-dependent normalisation at all*, so `LIS@epitope` stays on the same 0-1 scale as unrestricted LIS and the two are directly comparable ([ipsae.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DunbrackLab/IPSAE/main/ipsae.py)). The third route, if continuity with ipTM-family numbers matters, is **actifpTM**, which is literally a residue-subset-restricted ipTM: ColabFold's `predicted_tm_score_modified(..., pair_residue_weights=...)` accepts an arbitrary `[num_res, num_res]` weight matrix, and `get_actifptm_contacts` currently picks its subset from `cmap >= 0.6` ([extra_ptm.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sokrypton/ColabFold/main/colabfold/alphafold/extra_ptm.py), [Bioinformatics 2025](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/41/3/btaf107/8075121)). Swapping that for your epitope index list, or setting `pair_residue_weights = cmap ⊙ (binder_mask ⊗ epitope_mask)`, gives epitope-restricted ipTM with zero new mathematics — and because its `d0` derives from the *full* array length rather than the subset, the restricted and unrestricted values stay on one scale. Stock actifpTM, by contrast, would make your problem *worse*: it restricts to whatever interface formed, discarding the non-interface residues that would otherwise dilute the score.

One calibration warning applies to every restricted variant that renormalises `d0` by surviving partner count — ipSAE's `d0res`/`d0dom` and i_pDAE's `tm_score_distance_scale` both do. Restriction shrinks `d0`, which *hardens* the score, so **your 0.795 native reference will not transfer**; re-derive it by computing the restricted metric on the true complex with the same epitope definition. If a stable scale matters more than strictness, freeze `partner_counts` at the unrestricted value and restrict only the summation.

Finally, do not try to make a single number carry both jobs. The right shape is a **two-factor retention score**: one factor says the interface is confident (`i_pDAE@epitope` or `LIS@epitope`), the other says the interface is *here* — `EpiRecall = |P ∩ E|/|E|`, `EpiPrecision = |P ∩ E|/|P|`, or the differentiable soft form `EpiCoverageFraction = (Σ_{j∈E} c_j)/(Σ_{j∈target} c_j)` with `c_j = Σ_i cmap[i,j]`. The set-overlap family is published precedent: **Epitope Recall (EpiRec)**, "the fraction of true epitope residues that the design contacts," is defined in CHIMERA-Bench ([arXiv:2603.13431](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13431), **definition taken from a search summary; the primary PDF was not read**), and `Fnat` is DockQ's "fraction of retrieved native contacts (same as Recall or TPR)" ([DockQ README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bjornwallner/DockQ/master/README.md)). The Jaccard form is a reasonable construction but I found no paper reporting it under that name — present it as your own. Whatever you choose, state the cutoff: the codebases use **4.0 Å atom-atom** (BindCraft2 `Interface_Residues`), **8.0 Å CA-CA** (i_pDAE) and **8.0 Å CB-CB** (pDockQ, actifpTM), and results are not comparable across the three. DockQ itself is unavailable to you — it needs a reference complex and its `--mapping` is chain-level only — and pDockQ is restrictable but ill-advised, since `x = mean_plddt · log₁₀(npairs)` confounds "wrong site" with "small site."

## Inter-chain pose RMSD has no precedent, and escalating it fails everywhere measured

Your third question has a clean answer: **no published work uses an inter-chain, align-on-target / measure-on-binder reference-pose RMSD as a differentiable loss through a structure predictor to optimise sequence.** The machinery exists — ColabDesign's `_get_rmsd_loss(true, pred, weights, L, include_L=True, copies=1)` performs weighted Kabsch via `_np_kabsch()` and explicitly "aligns on first L positions, optionally computes error on remaining positions when `include_L=False`," which is precisely the align-on-subset/score-the-complement pattern — but it is wired into the **`partial` (partial hallucination) protocol**, not the binder protocol, and the binder loss set contains **no coordinate RMSD term at all** ([ColabDesign af/loss.py](https://github.com/sokrypton/ColabDesign/blob/main/colabdesign/af/loss.py)). Every epitope-targeting binder method located specifies placement through distogram-space terms on named residue pairs and reserves RMSD for post-hoc evaluation: Germinal's paratope BCE, TorchCraft's paratope and hotspot terms, the fold-conditioned contact-map RMSE, EasyNano's CDR-to-epitope distance. The revealed preference is unanimous.

The mechanistic reason is gradient density rather than correctness. A distogram loss over an |epitope|×|CDR| pair set supplies gradient to every pair directly from a network output head; a global pose RMSD supplies **one scalar per step**, and the Kabsch step deliberately removes exactly the rigid-body component that constitutes most of a 14-27 Å placement error, leaving a residual diluted across all binder CA atoms. A hinge on that scalar is worse still: `relu(rmsd − tol)` has gradient magnitude exactly 1 when active and 0 otherwise, so it **carries no information about how far off the pose is**. And under Adam, which normalises per-parameter by a running second moment, a uniform rescaling of one loss term is largely absorbed once the second-moment estimate re-adapts — a strong candidate explanation for your coefficient-independence, and one that predicts larger weights will keep not helping. (That argument is my inference, not a cited finding; no source analysed optimiser normalisation for a pose-RMSD term.)

Two tempting diagnoses should be set aside. The Kabsch/SVD gradient pathology is real but narrow: the exact gradient carries `F_{i,j} = 1/(s_i² − s_j²)` and is "undefined whenever two singular values are both zero and large when their sum is very near zero," yet the original authors report "no instabilities due to large gradients" in practice ([Levinson et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.14616)). For a Kabsch fit over a folded, three-dimensionally extended target domain the cross-covariance is well conditioned, and the predicted failure is gradient *blow-up*, not vanishing — so it poorly explains a term that does almost nothing. It would become relevant only with a small, collinear or planar alignment set, such as a short helical epitope. FAPE is not a drop-in fix either: ColabDesign's `get_fape_loss(..., clamp=10.0)` takes `clip(norm(true_vector − pred_vector), 0, clamp)/10.0`, so at a 14-27 Å misplacement **every term clips to a constant 1.0 with identically zero gradient**, and sidechain FAPE raises `"ERROR: 'sc_fape' not currently supported for 'multimer' mode"` — unavailable for complexes.

On whether escalation or scheduling helps, the evidence is consistent and negative. Classical penalty continuation does work — TrajOpt minimises `f̃(x) + μ Σ|g̃_i|₊ + μ Σ|h̃_i|` and sets `μ ← k·μ` whenever constraints miss tolerance ([TrajOpt, RSS 2013](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss09/p31.pdf)) — but it depends on each inner problem being solved to convergence inside a trust region, plus the l1 exact-penalty theorem guaranteeing a finite μ\* exists. An AF-backprop loop satisfies none of these: the "inner solve" is a fixed number of Adam steps on a non-convex predictor with a discrete end-target. The structure-refinement community's practice is the *opposite* of escalation: Rosetta's default FastRelax ramps coordinate constraints **down**, `coord_cst_weight 1.0 → 0.5 → 0.0 → 0.0` across each repeat's four steps, with `-relax:ramp_constraints` true by default, explicitly because strong restraints against a possibly inaccurate reference create artifacts ([Rosetta Relax docs](https://docs.rosettacommons.org/docs/latest/application_documentation/structure_prediction/relax)). What gets ramped *up* is van der Waals repulsion.

The sharpest measurement comes from constrained diffusion, which states flatly that **"increased weight on guidance terms tends to disrupt the diffusion trajectory"** and that soft constraints on discrete sequence prediction "fail to consistently provide constraint adherent outputs." On a PDZ hydrogen-bond-geometry task, standard RFdiffusion achieved **0% constraint satisfaction**; "Recenter Mass Guidance" **0% across 31,000 samples**; SMC-based constraint-guided diffusion 0% on the angle requirements — **zero usable samples across ~100k total** — while a consensus-ADMM dual method reached **100% satisfaction (21.0% usable after realism filtering)** ([arXiv 2510.14989](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14989)). Their Theorem 6.2 formalises when escalation is safe: the schedule **λ_t = c_t/η_t** with increasing c_t makes feasibility dominant *only once the model's prediction is accurate*, whereas a uniform increase causes early-step over-constraint that disrupts the trajectory. Among AF-design pipelines, what is actually scheduled is the **sequence parameterisation**, not loss weights — BindCraft's four stages run logit-space `(1−λ)·logits + λ·softmax(logits/T)`, then softmax with temperature lowered over 45 iterations, then a five-iteration straight-through estimator, then one-hot greedy ([BindCraft, Nature 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09429-6)) — and TorchCraft reports explicitly that **"No universal annealing schedule for individual loss weights is explicitly reported; weights appear task-configured statically"** ([arXiv 2609.19770](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19770)). The two exceptions are instructive: Germinal anneals a *prior* weight, `ablm_scale = [0.2, 0.4, 0.4, 1.0]` on the IgLM term, and reports that "this annealing schedule empirically provided more robust performance than fixed IgLM weights"; TorchCraft ramps `w_interface(t)` for small-molecule design and reports it **without ablation**.

So: **a stronger fixed pose weight is not the fix, and ramping it from step 0 is the specific thing the one relevant theorem warns against.** If hard satisfaction is genuinely required, the supported mechanisms are a dual/augmented-Lagrangian update on the *observed* violation (`w ← w + η·violation`, which escalates only while violated and relaxes once met), per-constraint rather than global coefficients, and **late activation** — the geometric term off during the logits/soft phases and on only after the parameterisation has annealed. Note too that your weight-0 ablation result (22.9 vs 25.3 Å at matched compute) is, as far as the notes could establish, **a genuinely unreported negative result**: no paper backpropagates an inter-chain reference-pose RMSD through a predictor and reports its effectiveness either way, so there is no published baseline to compare against.

## "Right fold, wrong epitope" is unnamed, unquantified, and almost never controlled

There is **no accepted term** for high interface confidence at the wrong site on the right target. The field's vocabulary covers wrong *target* — mBER's "on-design" versus "off-design hits" and "sticky targets" that absorb disproportionate non-cognate binding ([mBER](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.26.678877v1.full)) — wrong *pose*, and wrong *confidence* ("hallucination," "adversarial example," "confidence failures"). The closest formal metric is hotspot conservation, reported as **HC-precision 0.813 → 0.889 and HC-recall 0.878 → 0.935** for a time-dependent-guidance method over its baseline ([Bioinformatics 2026](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/42/Supplement_2/btag455/8767312)). Your phenomenon is best described as an **epitope-recall failure under an unconstrained interface objective**: the design loss was satisfied by *any* interface rather than the intended one. In most published pipelines it would not be detected as a failure at all — it passes every ipTM/ipSAE/PAE filter and is scored a success until a structure is solved.

Experimentally confirmed **wrong-epitope** instances are essentially absent, and the reason is survivorship rather than rarity. Across the flagship papers, fewer than roughly ten designed complexes have been solved experimentally, all drawn from *confirmed binders*, all reported as matching the intended site: BindCraft's three crystal structures (BBF-14/binder4 **1.7 Å** backbone RMSD; Der f7/binder2 1.7 Å; Der f21/binder10 3.1 Å, from an alternative interface-tyrosine rotamer) out of ~150 designs tested across ten targets ([BindCraft preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full)); RFantibody's influenza VHH cryo-EM at 3.0 Å with **1.45 Å backbone and 0.8 Å CDR3 RMSD**, from 28 SPR-confirmed binders out of ~9,000 designs per target ([bioRxiv 2024.03.14.585103v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.14.585103v2.full)); Germinal's anti-PD-L1 scFv H5 at **1.25 Å global Cα RMSD** with contacts to I37/Y39/E41 maintained ([Germinal preprint v3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.19.677421v3.full)). **The field therefore has no measured rate of intended-epitope engagement and no published counterexample — and the absence of published off-epitope failures is not evidence that they are rare.** Germinal, the closest methodological precedent to your pipeline, has *no experimental structures at all* for epitope verification: "AF3-predicted structures showed designed binders positioned at intended epitopes," i.e. the predictor family is both designer and verifier, a circularity you can legitimately point at.

Two leads probably contain on-point material and **could not be verified**. A search-level summary of the published RFantibody paper describes one VHH that "bound the correct RBD epitope but its binding mode deviated significantly from the design model, adopting a framework-mediated interaction instead, which was classified as a design failure" ([Nature 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09721-5)) — but the full text was paywalled and a direct read of the 2024 preprint found no such VHH, so treat as unverified. And a summary of a 2025 epitope-specific antibody workflow states that "there have been cases where the highest-affinity candidates bind outside the intended epitope" ([Nat Commun 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67361-9)) — the single most promising lead for a documented instance, with the wording, detection method and counts all unconfirmed behind a paywall. Both should be read in full before any novelty claim.

What *is* abundantly documented is that confidence scores do not discriminate. RFdiffusion's own antibody work reports that **"design success rates were not significantly higher for filtered designs vs unfiltered designs with the RF2 settings we used"** and that "AlphaFold2 fails to routinely predict antibody-antigen structures accurately, preventing its use as a filter in an antibody design pipeline." mBER's large-scale measurement is subtler and more damning: ipTM filtering genuinely enriches on-target binding — median hit rate ~0.4% baseline, ipTM > 0.7 moving hit rates "into the double-digit percent range," best epitopes reaching 38% — yet **off-design hit rates were "relatively flat across ipTM bins."** Confidence enriches for *some* binding without discriminating *where*. BindCraft reports no ROC curves or binder/non-binder separation statistics at all. On the prediction side, AF3 picks the most accurate structure roughly **25%** of the time (AF2 at most 35%), with selection errors costing up to **0.40 DockQ** — a qualitatively different docking — and predicted structures remain invariant to mutation of up to 40% of residues, with mean ranking score still **0.62** at 20% mutation, "barely below the 0.6 high-confidence threshold" ([bioRxiv 2026.02.25.708002](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.25.708002v1)). Taken with the AF3 antibody benchmark's finding that **~34% of false negatives retained the correct epitope location despite poor structural alignment**, the decoupling is bidirectional: confidence, pose accuracy and epitope correctness vary independently, so an overlap metric and an RMSD metric are **not substitutes** and you should carry both.

Your permuted-epitope decoy is the most methodologically significant thing in your results, because **almost nobody runs it.** Negative controls in this field are negative-control *binders* (Germinal used anti-TNFα nanobodies against PD-L1 and IL3 screens, anti-PD-L1 against IL20 and BHRF1) or negative-control *surfaces* (BindCraft used lysozyme; mBER uses empty wells to exclude plastic binders) — not negative-control *targets*. No design paper was found that permutes or scrambles the target epitope and re-runs the pipeline at matched compute; consequently **there is no published baseline against which your ~2× figure can be compared.** The PPI-screening community has internalised this discipline — "negative controls are important to identify an expected rate of false positives," with false positives arising "where domains may be inaccurately swapped or the structures may be predicted to bind somewhere where they are known not to interact" ([Plant J 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/tpj.16969)) — and the design community has not imported it. The right framing is that your control is an **adversarial-robustness probe of the design objective**, in the same family as the AF3 mutation-invariance experiments, which connects it to a real citable literature rather than to the absent off-epitope-design literature.

Detection methods that are reported to work are all experimental, and two have numbers. Germinal's epitope-directed alanine scanning reduced binding in **17 of 26 designs**, with at least one mutation abrogating binding entirely, while control binders aimed at different epitopes retained wild-type-like affinity; on PD-L1, alanine substitutions at I54, Y56 and E58 each "reduced binding signal and weakened affinity by >10-fold," and crucially "nanobodies with distinct binding modes were differentially affected, with no single mutation abolishing binding across all designs" — **the differential pattern is the evidence.** Competition assays serve the same role in BindCraft (PD-L1, IFNAR2, Bet v1), AlphaProteo and RFantibody, where for SARS-CoV-2 RBD "binding was to the expected epitope, confirmed by competition." On the computational side, the only mitigation with a quantified improvement on the antibody–antigen docking problem is physics-based rescoring: AlphaRED lifts success from AF-Multimer's **20% to 43%** ([eLife reviewed preprint 94029](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/94029)). **No benchmarked numbers exist for "re-prediction with a held-out predictor improves design success by X%"** — the idea is universally advocated and, as far as the notes could establish, never quantified; note that BindCraft's own re-prediction stays *inside* the AF2 family, which cannot address predictor exploitation. Also unquantified: MM-GBSA or Rosetta ddG rescoring improving *epitope* accuracy as opposed to binding success.

## A 10 Å abort and a two-factor retention score are the cheapest fixes

Your open question was whether a stronger or dynamically scheduled pose weight, or a larger edit budget, is the fix. On the evidence assembled here the answer is no to all three, and the ordering of what to do instead follows directly from the taxonomy. Note one general observation that reframes the problem: across every pipeline examined, the recurring restraint form is **a soft flat-bottom on min-distance, reduced per *epitope* residue rather than per binder residue.** RFantibody takes the min over CDR-loop residues for each hotspot; ColabDesign with a hotspot sets `mask_1d = target_id` so that *every hotspot residue* must contact *something* on the binder, instead of the default *every binder residue* must contact *something* on the target. Same function, opposite quantifier. A site-agnostic confidence score cannot answer the hotspot-side question at all.

| Layer | Intervention | Exact parameters, from released code |
|---|---|---|
| **Selection** (do first) | Hard geometric site gate before any confidence metric | Germinal: `binder_near_hotspot == true`, `cdr3_hotspot_contacts > 0`, `percent_interface_cdr > 0.5`; 5.3 Å proximity, 6.0 Å contacts, ≥3 contacts |
| **Selection** | Two-factor retention score replacing bare ipSAE | `i_pDAE@epitope` (contact = CA-CA ≤ 8 Å, masked `& epitope_mask`, mean not max) × `EpiRecall`/`EpiJaccard` at a stated 4 Å atom-atom cutoff |
| **In-trajectory** | Abort and reseed on bad targeting | RFantibody: min hotspot-Cβ to designed-loop-Cβ > **10 Å** → discard; **20** failures → abort the input spec |
| **Conditioning / input** | Make off-epitope poses unattractive to the frozen predictor | BindCraft2 `forced_targeting`, shell **10 Å**, lysinate exposed non-epitope surface, restore before hardening; or epitope-fragment-only target template |
| **Gradient** | Replace the pose hinge with a distogram-space epitope term | EasyNano ELU hinge, **8 Å** set-point, weight 0.2; and/or Germinal's `L_CDR − λ·L_framework`, generalised to off-epitope target surface |
| **Gradient** | Site-restrict what you already have | ColabDesign `i_pae` with `hotspot` set is **already** an epitope-restricted differentiable PAE loss (`mask_1b = target_id`); BindEnergyCraft's ℐ restricted to binder×epitope |

Two of these deserve emphasis because they are free. First, the gate: a 14-27 Å displacement is outside **every** published epitope-contact threshold — 4 Å heavy-atom (BindCraft interface enumeration), 5 Å atom / 15 Å token (BoltzGen), 6 Å (Boltz-2 `pocket: max_distance`), 8 Å Cβ (RFantibody's training definition), 10 Å Cβ (RFdiffusion's definition and RFantibody's abort threshold). Even the loosest criterion in the literature rejects your designs. Second, the gradient term you may already own: if your pipeline inherits ColabDesign's `_loss_binder`, setting `opt["hotspot"]` rebinds `target_id` to the hotspot mask, which makes `i_pae` an epitope-restricted interface-PAE loss as a *side effect of variable reuse* — undocumented, but production-tested and differentiable. Watch `num_pos`, the most overlooked knob in that loss: at its default `inf` the reduction averages over **all** hotspot rows, and setting `num_pos = k < |hotspot|` makes the term **looser**, not stricter, which is the opposite of the natural intuition. And watch the cutoff scale: at ColabDesign's `i_con` default of **21.6875 Å** (the last distogram bin edge, i.e. effectively no distance restriction) or BindCraft/Germinal's 20 Å, a binder 14-27 Å away is already partially inside the cutoff for some epitope residues, so the term sits near its floor and supplies almost no gradient toward the correct site. EasyNano's 8 Å set-point and Germinal's 5.3 Å filter bracket the scale at which "on-epitope" is actually judged. **The weakest link in your loss is likely the cutoff, not the weight.**

Two notes on your other results. The 5-edit control — three of four seeds localising the single most-damaged position but none selecting the correct residue there, and zero damaged residues restored — reads as a credit-assignment failure at amino-acid *identity*, not a budget failure: the gradient carries positional information and not identity information. That is consistent with the documented mutation insensitivity of these predictors, which BindCraft itself concedes ("AF2 has also been shown to be insensitive to the predicted effects of point mutations") and which the AF3 adversarial study quantifies at structural invariance under 40% mutation. A larger edit budget enlarges the set of edits that satisfy a site-agnostic objective faster than it enlarges the set that satisfies the intended one, so absent a site gate it should be expected to make off-epitope docking *more* likely, not less — that inference is mine, not a cited finding, but it follows from the objective's degeneracy. Relatedly, and against your stored preference on this one axis: the AF3 adversarial study finds **ESMFold *more* mutation-sensitive than AF3** ("greater sensitivity to point mutations," "higher sensitivity to mutations known to induce conformational transitions"). That does not undercut OpenDDE's validated advantage as a *classifier* on your P17 system, but mutation sensitivity is precisely what a 5-edit search needs from its gradient, so the two criteria may not point the same way.

## Conclusion

The deepest implication of this synthesis is that interface confidence measures the **self-consistency of a packing, not the nativeness of a location** — and once that is accepted, no reweighting, rescaling or rescheduling of any function of confidence can become site-aware. That is why the two pipelines that work put a boolean in front of the confidence check rather than trying to build a better confidence score, and why Germinal computes `ipsae` but declines to filter on it. It also explains why your hardened aggregation (mean instead of `max_i`, min instead of max over directions) bought nothing: you tightened the reduction while leaving the pair set global, and the pair set is where the epitope information would have to enter. The corollary is mildly uncomfortable: an epitope-restricted metric built on ipSAE or i_pDAE will *harden* through `d0` shrinkage, so a restricted score of 0.77 is not comparable to an unrestricted 0.795 and you will need to recompute the native reference under the restricted mask before any comparison means anything.

The second implication is that you have, more or less by accident, built the control the field is missing. There is no published design paper that permutes the target epitope and re-runs at matched compute; there is no published false-positive rate for ipSAE at any threshold; there is no published distribution of ipSAE for *designed* as opposed to natural complexes; and there is no published fraction of gradient-converged designs that dock off-epitope before filtering, even though Germinal's and BindCraft2's filters imply the number is non-trivial. Those are four genuine holes, not gaps in the search. A permuted-epitope null distribution plus an epitope-restricted confidence metric is a small, self-contained contribution that would make the existing hotspot filters interpretable rather than merely conservative — and it is worth more than another sweep of the pose coefficient, which the ESMFold 10×/5× result, Rosetta's 1.0 → 0.5 → 0.0 ramp-down, and the constrained-diffusion 0%-across-100k-samples baseline all independently predict will keep not working.
