# Can a Small Number of Sequence Mutations Reorient a Binder?

> Scope note: this file covers *binding-mode / orientation / approach-angle* change versus *affinity* change under few mutations. Throughout, I mark explicitly whether a cited change is (a) affinity only, (b) local side-chain/loop conformation, (c) intramolecular domain reorientation (e.g. VH–VL torsion, elbow angle), or (d) a change in the rigid-body docking pose of the binder on the antigen. The project's failure mode (12–52° rotations, 156–178° flips at the correct epitope) is category (d). Conflating (a)–(c) with (d) is the dominant error in this literature.
>
> Verification note: several claims below were surfaced only as search-engine synthesis of a source's text and I could not retrieve the full text (paywalls, and a number of fetches were blocked mid-session by a tool-side safety classifier). Every such claim is flagged `[snippet-only]` and, where the primary attribution is uncertain, `[attribution unverified]`. Treat flagged items as leads for the report writer to confirm, not as established numbers.

## Q1. Documented cases where 1–10 point mutations changed an antibody/nanobody BINDING MODE (not just affinity)

### Takeaway
I found **no** case in the literature of a handful (1–10) of CDR *point substitutions*, at fixed loop lengths, rotating or flipping an antibody or nanobody's rigid-body pose on its antigen. The documented cases of few-mutation geometric change are all *intramolecular* — VH–VL torsion, elbow angle, CDR loop conformational-ensemble shifts — of a few Å and tens of degrees at most, and none reverses docking polarity. The large orientation changes that *are* documented (up to 180°) track germline V-gene identity, CDR3 length, and indels, i.e. variables a fixed-length point-substitution budget cannot reach.

### Cited Findings

**Closest thing to a few-mutation orientation change (intramolecular, not docking pose):**
- The **CH103 HIV bnAb lineage** shows "a shift in the relative orientation of the VL and VH domains during evolution of the CH103 lineage, corresponding to a root mean squared deviation in α-carbon positions of 2.1 Å", and this reorientation was "mediated by mutations at the VL/VH interface, particularly a leucine-to-valine substitution at position VL46" — [Mishra & Mariuzza, Front Immunol 2018 (review)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.00117/full). This is a *VH–VL interdomain* reorientation (2.1 Å Cα RMSD), not a rotation of the Fab on the antigen; the lineage retained its CD4-binding-site epitope.
- **Antibody 93F3**: destabilising paratope mutations were offset by "additional somatic mutations in the VL/VH interface, distal to the paratope" — [Mishra & Mariuzza, Front Immunol 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.00117/full). Again interdomain, compensatory, not reorienting.
- **Single framework substitution H71 (A→R)** induces "strong population shifts towards different dominant paratope conformations in solution" affecting all six CDR loops *and* the VH–VL interdomain orientation; "all CDR loop movements are correlated"; the authors stress that "considering only one single static structure might not be sufficient to fully understand the consequences of point mutations on the resulting conformational diversity" — [Fernández-Quintero et al., Front Immunol 2021;12:630034](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2021.630034/full) (also [PMC7960778](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7960778/)). MD/NMR, no antigen complex: this is paratope-ensemble change, **not** a demonstrated change in docking pose.
- A mutation at **LC-83**, ~25 Å from the antigen-binding site, can alter the predominant Fab **elbow angle** and the **VL/VH torsion angle** `[snippet-only]` `[attribution unverified]` — surfaced in search of the antibody-dynamics literature alongside [Front Immunol 2021;12:630034](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2021.630034/full); I could not confirm which paper reports LC-83 or recover the angle magnitudes. Elbow angle is a constant-domain/variable-domain hinge, irrelevant to a single-domain VHH.

**Single mutations that changed specificity without evidence of a pose change:**
- "Changing the antigen binding specificity by single point mutations of an anti-p24 (HIV-1) antibody" reports that mutations of antibody side chains in direct contact with antigen "show opposite influences on the binding of different peptides" — [J Immunol 2000;165:4505](https://journals.aai.org/jimmunol/article/165/8/4505/7491/Changing-the-Antigen-Binding-Specificity-by-Single) `[snippet-only — full text fetch redirected to a journal landing page]`. The reported readout is peptide-binding specificity, not a crystallographically determined change of orientation. **No structural evidence of a pose change was found in what I could retrieve.**
- Somatic mutation of an **anti-epD3** antibody "can change its fine specificity to that of epD2" — [Diversification of specificity after maturation, PMC3279380](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3279380/) `[snippet-only]`. Fine-specificity change within an epitope family; no accompanying structure of a reoriented complex was surfaced.
- A single framework mutation at the H3 C-flank, **W116Y**, "converted this bimodality into a stable single mode (median = 0.692, s.d. = 0.020)" where the unmodified framework had shown "a bimodal distribution: a high-ipTM basin (~0.65–0.70) and a low-ipTM basin (~0.43)" — [EasyNano, arXiv:2606.12772 (Hu, Cheng, Wang, Liu, June 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12772). This is the single most on-point result I found: **one framework substitution collapsed a bimodal predicted-pose distribution onto one basin**. Caveats: it is *in silico* (ESMFold2 ipTM), it is a **framework** not CDR mutation, and no experimental structure or rotation angle is given.

**Mutations at the interface that changed affinity and left the mode intact (the control case):**
- Three single interface substitutions in antibody **D1.3** Fv — VL Tyr50→Ser, VH Tyr32→Ala, VH Tyr101→Phe — bound to hen egg lysozyme, structures at 1.85–2.10 Å: "removal of direct antigen–antibody hydrogen bonds results in minimal shifts in the positions of the remaining protein atoms"; the complex is "considerably tolerant, both structurally and thermodynamically, to the truncation of antibody side chains that form hydrogen bonds with the antigen"; affinities fell **10-, 4- and 70-fold** respectively — [Dall'Acqua et al., Biochemistry 1996, PMID 8952503](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8952503/); structures [1KIP](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1KIP), [1KIQ](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1KIQ). This is the cleanest available counterexample to the project's hypothesis: aggressive single-residue truncations at the paratope move affinity up to ~70× and the pose essentially not at all.
- Two mutant **neuraminidase–NC41 antibody** complexes showed that "amino acid substitutions can be accommodated within an antigen–antibody interface by local structural rearrangements, with side-chain shifts and rotations observed at the mutation sites" — [Tulip et al., JMB 1992, PMID 1522584](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1522584/) `[snippet-only]`. Local accommodation, not reorientation.
- For a small hapten, an interface mutation *did* move the ligand: in the **FITC-E2 Trp H129→Ala** scFv complex "the hapten fluorescein in FITC-E2 is rotated and shifted relative to its orientation and position in 4-4-20" — [PMC2253284](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2253284/) `[snippet-only]`. Note this is a **small-molecule hapten in a pocket**, where the ligand has few contacts and ~no inertia; it does not transfer to a 123-residue domain docked on a protein surface. (It also confounds a mutation with a different antibody, FITC-E2 vs 4-4-20.)

### Inferences
- The literature's "binding mode change" vocabulary is used overwhelmingly for (i) induced-fit/conformational-selection changes in the *same* pose, (ii) VH–VL/elbow geometry, and (iii) hapten repositioning inside a pocket. None of these is a 12–52° rotation or a 156–178° flip of a domain on a protein epitope.
- The one place a *single* substitution has been reported to change which pose a system occupies is a **framework** position (W116Y, EasyNano) and the effect was to *stabilise one of two pre-existing basins*, not to create a new orientation. That is a meaningfully different claim: selecting among basins the backbone already admits, versus moving to a basin it does not.

### Gaps
- I could not find any crystallographic or cryo-EM pair of complexes, differing by ≤10 CDR point substitutions at constant loop length, in which the binder's rigid-body orientation on the antigen differs measurably. Absence of such a pair in the searched literature is itself the finding; I did not find a review that asserts this absence explicitly, so it should be stated as "no such case located" rather than "it has been shown impossible".
- No source gave a quantitative distribution of "interface RMSD change per mutation" for antibody–antigen complexes.
- The anti-p24 single-mutation specificity paper's structural content is unverified (fetch blocked).

## Q2. Is binding orientation ROBUST to CDR mutation? (affinity maturation as the natural experiment)

### Takeaway
Yes — strongly. Affinity maturation is the best-powered natural experiment available (typically 5–20+ substitutions, sometimes far more), and the recurring structural finding is that the **epitope and the approach geometry are retained** while affinity rises 10³–10⁴-fold through preorganisation, rigidification, shape complementarity and interdomain compensation. The documented exceptions are indel-bearing lineages and interdomain (VH–VL) shifts of ~2 Å, not reorientations.

### Cited Findings
- **48G7**: the affinity-matured antibody binds hapten "~30,000 times" more tightly than its germline precursor "as a result of nine replacement somatic mutations" — [Wedemayer et al., Science 1997;276:1665](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.276.5319.1665); structures [1AJ7](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/pdbe/entry/pdb/1aj7/citations) (germline Fab + hapten), [2RCS](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/2RCS) (germline Fab), [1GAF](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1GAF) (mature Fab + hapten). Only **one of the nine** somatic mutations directly contacts the hapten; "significant changes in the configuration of the combining site occur upon binding of hapten to the germline antibody, whereas hapten binds to the mature antibody by a lock-and-key fit mechanism" `[snippet-only for the mechanism sentence]`. **Conflict to note:** the Mishra & Mariuzza review states "~3,000-fold" for 48G7 rather than 30,000-fold — [Front Immunol 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.00117/full) vs [Science 1997](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.276.5319.1665). Either way: nine mutations, 10³–10⁴× affinity, **same hapten, same site, mechanism change not orientation change**.
- **PGT121 family (anti-HIV)**: "PGT124 and PGT122 employ a common set of CDR residues to contact the Asn332 glycan and V3 loop, nearly all of which are also found in the germ line precursor" — [Mishra & Mariuzza, Front Immunol 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.00117/full). Binding mode conserved from germline through a heavily mutated lineage.
- The same review's summary of maturation mechanisms lists **paratope preorganisation and rigidification, improved shape complementarity, increased buried hydrophobic surface, additional H-bonds/salt bridges, and conformational-flexibility changes** — and explicitly frames reorientation as *not* the general strategy: "Rather than universal reorientation, affinity maturation employs diverse strategies" — [Front Immunol 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.00117/full).
- **Germline vs mature flexibility, not geometry**: "Mutated CDR loops in mature antibodies prearrange themselves for binding, whereas in germline antibodies the CDR loops remain more flexible, and this different flexibility is maintained by antibody variants upon antigen binding" `[snippet-only]` — surfaced from the affinity-maturation CDR-loop literature, e.g. [Affinity Maturation for Antibody Engineering, PMC11819675](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11819675/).
- **Contact residues are largely germline-encoded**: a claim that "all of the antigen contacting amino acids in mature antibody–antigen complexes are conserved in the germline sequence" `[snippet-only]` `[attribution unverified]` — surfaced alongside [Di Palma et al., FEBS Lett 2017 (anti-HCMV antibody family)](https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/1873-3468.12774). I could not confirm the scope of this statement (one lineage vs general) and the report writer should not present it as a general law.
- **Rigidification is not universal**, which matters because flexibility and orientation are often conflated: "Repertoire Analysis of Antibody CDR-H3 Loops Suggests Affinity Maturation Does Not Typically Result in Rigidification" — [Front Immunol 2018;9:413, PMID 29545810](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29545810/); "some antibodies' CDR-H3 loops became more flexible after affinity maturation while others became less flexible" `[snippet-only]` — [Front Immunol 2018;9:413](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.00413/full). Contrast with the rigidification MD studies, e.g. [CDR-H3 loop rigidification upon affinity maturation](https://diglib.uibk.ac.at/download/pdf/4964581.pdf) and the [VHH-72 affinity-maturation MD study](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.12.08.416164.full.pdf). **Sources conflict on rigidification; they do not conflict on epitope/orientation retention.**
- **Designed binders survive affinity maturation without reorienting**: designed TcdB VHHs "bound in the intended orientation, both before and after affinity maturation" — [Bennett et al., *Atomically accurate de novo design of antibodies with RFdiffusion*, Nature 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09721-5) `[snippet-only — Nature full text behind an auth redirect]`; see also the [RosettaCommons summary](https://rosettacommons.org/2025/11/24/rfantibody-creates-epitope-specific-antibody-binders-with-atomic-level-agreement/). This is a direct, modern, in-vitro-plus-structure demonstration that accumulating mutations for affinity does **not** move a VHH's pose.
- **Where maturation did change geometry, it did so with indels, not substitutions**: "~40% of bNAbs against HIV-1 Env contain indels, ranging in size from 1 to 11 amino acids", versus ~2% in normal repertoires; **VRC01-class** bnAbs carry "a deletion of two to six amino acids in VLCDR1" to avoid glycan clashes; **8ANC195** carries "an insertion of five amino acids in VHFR3" to penetrate between glycans — [Mishra & Mariuzza, Front Immunol 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.00117/full).
- Extreme counterpoint on mutation load: anti-MERS antibody **m336** is "almost germ line, with only one somatic mutation in the H chain" — [Front Immunol 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.00117/full). High affinity and a defined binding mode are reachable with ~zero substitutions once the germline geometry is right, which is the same message read from the other direction.

### Inferences
- Affinity maturation runs a far larger mutational search than 5 substitutions — typically 5–20, sometimes dozens, under hard selection for affinity — and almost never reorients. If selection for tighter binding at 10–30 substitutions does not reorient, 5 substitutions selected by a structure-predictor gradient are very unlikely to.
- The mechanisms maturation *does* use (preorganise, rigidify, complement, compensate) are all mechanisms that make a *given* pose better. That is the biologically realised function of CDR point substitutions.

### Gaps
- I did not find a systematic, quantitative survey of paired germline/mature complex structures reporting a distribution of inter-complex rotation angles or interface RMSDs. Such a survey would be the ideal citation and I could not locate one; the Mishra & Mariuzza review is narrative.

## Q3. What structural features determine approach angle and binding-mode registry?

### Takeaway
Approach angle and mode registry are set by **backbone-scale variables**: germline V-gene identity, CDR loop *length* (especially CDR3), the canonical conformational class a loop occupies, and interdomain geometry. The canonical-class literature makes the dependency explicit — conformation is set by loop length plus a handful of *key* positions, with the clustering itself defined on backbone dihedrals, not side-chain identity.

### Cited Findings
- "The conformation of a particular canonical class is determined by the **length of the loop** and the amino acid residues residing at **key positions** within the loop, as well as within the **conserved framework**" `[snippet-only]` — surfaced from the canonical-class literature, e.g. [North, Lehmann & Dunbrack, *A new clustering of antibody CDR loop conformations*, PMC3065967](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3065967/) and [Length-independent structural similarities enrich the CDR canonical class model, PMC4966832](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4966832/).
- Canonical clustering is performed on **backbone dihedrals**: "quantitative clustering is based on CDR length, a dihedral angle metric, and an affinity propagation algorithm, with a cutoff of 40° for the average dihedral angle difference from the centroid of each cluster" — [North et al., PMC3065967](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3065967/); updated in [PyIgClassify2 / *A penultimate classification of canonical antibody CDR conformations*](https://dunbrack2.fccc.edu/pyigclassify2/doc/BIORXIV-2022-511988v1-Dunbrack.pdf). The classification variable is literally backbone geometry; side-chain identity enters only through key positions that select *which backbone class* is adopted.
- **VH–VL orientation is a six-parameter rigid-body quantity**: ABangle characterises it with "five angles (HL, HC1, LC1, HC2 and LC2) and a distance (dc)" — [Dunbar, Fuchs, Shi & Deane, PEDS 2013](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~dunbar/abangle/); [tool](http://opig.stats.ox.ac.uk/webapps/abangle/). Variable-domain orientation "affects how the CDR loops are positioned relative to one another and influences the geometry of the antigen binding site" — [Examining Variable Domain Orientations in Antigen Receptors, PLoS Comput Biol 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4168974/). Note: this parameter set does not exist for a single-domain VHH, which removes one of the few geometric knobs point mutations are documented to turn.
- **Epitope size baseline** for interpreting "reaches the epitope": "on average the epitope contains 14.6 ± 4.9 residues" — [Epitope profiling using computational structural modelling, PMC8700021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8700021/) / [PLoS Comput Biol 2021](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1009675). An epitope of ~15 residues can be contacted from many orientations, which is exactly why "right epitope, wrong orientation" is a large and populated failure region.
- **Pose and epitope are separable, and tools now separate them**: SPACE2 "separates antibodies binding to the same epitope in a different binding pose into distinct clusters", and cases exist where "antibodies binding to a common epitope are separated into two clusters because despite engaging the same epitope, the antibody structures do not overlay perfectly" — [Spoendlin et al., *Improved computational epitope profiling using structural models*, PMC10544996](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10544996/).
- **Pose is many-to-one over sequence**: antibodies **EY6A** and **S304** "are structurally similar and adopt a common binding mode to the same RBD epitope, but share only **43% CDRH3 sequence identity**" — [PMC10544996](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10544996/). Over half of the CDRH3 can be rewritten without changing the binding mode.

### Inferences
- The variables that the canonical-class and ABangle literatures identify as determining registry — loop length, backbone dihedral class, germline framework, interdomain rigid-body parameters — are exactly the variables a *fixed-length, fixed-framework, 5-substitution* CDR budget holds constant. Side-chain identity enters as a *selector* among backbone classes a given length admits, not as a continuous steering input on orientation.
- The EY6A/S304 result sets an important asymmetry for the project: large CDR3 sequence change → no pose change. Combined with Q4's IGHV3-53 result (same V gene, different CDR3 length → 180° flip), the evidence says **pose responds to length/class, not to substitution count**.

### Gaps
- I did not find a paper that defines and tabulates a single scalar "antibody approach angle" over SAbDab with a distribution in degrees. Searches for this returned epitope-mapping methodology rather than approach-angle statistics. If the report needs "typical spread of approach angles at one epitope", that number was not locatable in this pass.

## Q4. "Binding mode switching" / epitope switching: how many sequence differences separate different orientations?

### Takeaway
The best-characterised natural binding-mode switch — IGHV3-53/3-66 antibodies against the SARS-CoV-2 RBD — involves a **~180° rotation of the Fab about its long axis** between two modes that share the *same germline V gene*, and the discriminating variable is **CDR H3 length** (≤10 aa vs ≥15 aa, Kabat), i.e. an indel-scale difference, not a set of point substitutions. The TCR field's reversed-docking cases tell the same story with germline V-gene identity as the determinant.

### Cited Findings
- **IGHV3-53 mode A vs mode B**: "binding mode B of COVA2-39 is quite different with its Fab **rotated 180° along its long axis** relative to COVA2-04, thereby swapping the relative orientation of the light and heavy chains"; "both classes of IGHV3-53 antibodies target the ACE2 receptor binding site, but with **very different angles of approach** and molecular interactions" — [Wu, Wang, Wilson et al., *An alternative binding mode of IGHV3-53 antibodies to the SARS-CoV-2 RBD*, Cell Reports 2020, PMC7522650](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7522650/); [Cell Reports](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211124720312638); [PubMed 33027617](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33027617/). Structures: [7JMP, COVA2-39/RBD at 1.72 Å](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7JMP) and [7JMO, COVA2-04/RBD](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7JMO).
- The determinant is **CDR H3 length, not substitutions**: "a short CDR H3 (length ≤ 10 amino acids, Kabat numbering) is a molecular signature" of mode-A IGHV3-53 antibodies; "a small subset of IGHV3-53 antibodies to the RBD contain a longer CDR H3, and crystal structures ... demonstrate that a longer CDR H3 can be accommodated in a different binding mode (mode B)"; "about 10% of RBD-targeting IGHV3-53 antibodies have much longer CDR H3s (15 amino acids or longer)" — [PMC7522650](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7522650/). Companion clonotype analysis: [Sequence signatures of two IGHV3-53/3-66 public clonotypes, PMC7852275](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7852275/).
- **Reversed docking polarity in TCRs is germline-encoded**: "two TRBV17+ TCRs that bound H-2Db-NP366 with a **180° reversed polarity** compared to canonical TCR–pMHC-I docking, where peptide specificity was attributable to **germline-encoded** recognition"; the reversed topology "perturbs cellular activation, preventing co-receptor engagement" — [Gras, Rossjohn et al., *Reversed T cell receptor docking on a MHC class I complex limits involvement in the immune response*, Immunity 2016](https://www.cell.com/immunity/fulltext/S1074-7613(16)30351-X) / [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S107476131630351X). Also: "two TCRs from human regulatory T cells binding MHC class II molecules in a 180° reversed orientation" `[snippet-only]`; canonical polarity is otherwise near-universal — background in [Conserved biophysical compatibility among germline-encoded regions shapes TCR–MHC interactions, eLife 2024](https://elifesciences.org/articles/90681).
- **Germline V-gene usage sets VHH CDR-H3 conformation**: the title claim of [*VHH CDR-H3 conformation is determined by VH germline usage*, Commun Biol 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-023-05241-y) / [PMC10439903](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10439903/). VHHs fall into two structural clusters — extended CDR-H3 protruding into solvent vs kinked CDR-H3 folded back onto framework `[snippet-only for the cluster description]`.
- **Epitope switching / fine-specificity drift does occur under somatic mutation**, but the structural magnitude is unresolved: anti-epD3 → epD2 fine-specificity change by somatic mutation — [PMC3279380](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3279380/) `[snippet-only]`; "somatic hypermutation ... has the potential to change the specificity of an immunoglobulin" is standard framing — [Somatic hypermutation overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/somatic-hypermutation). None of the sources I retrieved paired an epitope-switching lineage with two structures showing a measured rotation.

### Inferences
- The two cleanest 180° cases in all of immune recognition — IGHV3-53 mode A/B and reversed TCR docking — are both attributed to **germline/length-level variables**. That is a direct structural analogue of the project's 156–178° flip, and it says the flip is a *backbone-class* phenomenon.
- Practical corollary: if the project wants a 180° distinction to be reachable, the search space must include CDR3 length (indels) or framework/germline choice. A fixed-length 5-substitution budget provably cannot cross the IGHV3-53 mode A↔B boundary, because that boundary is defined by a ≥5-residue length difference in CDR H3.

### Gaps
- I found no study that takes a *single clonal lineage* and reports two structures, differing by a countable handful of substitutions, in which the antibody binds the same antigen in two different orientations. The mode A/B comparison is between *different* antibodies from the same V gene with different CDR H3 lengths — not a mutational walk.

## Q5. Nanobody-specific: VHH binding-mode plasticity, CDR3 length, FR2

### Takeaway
Nanobodies show *more* orientational diversity than Fabs across a repertoire — but that diversity is produced by **which loops and which framework surface form the paratope** and by **CDR3 length/conformation**, all backbone-level properties. The same FR2-exposure that gives VHHs extra orientations also means a VHH's pose is partly encoded in its *framework*, which a CDR-only budget cannot edit.

### Cited Findings
- Dataset and heterogeneity: **156 unique Nb:antigen co-crystal structures** vs 156 matched classical Ab:antigen complexes (resolution ≤4.0 Å, sequence-unique H3). **21 unique contact profiles** in the Nb set vs **14** in the Ab set; "only **16%** of Nbs use loops H1–3" versus **53%** of classical antibodies; ~**30%** of Nb structures bound antigen using just one or two variable loops, double the **10%** for conventional antibodies — [Mitchell & Colwell, *Analysis of nanobody paratopes reveals greater diversity than classical antibodies*, PEDS 2018;31:267](https://academic.oup.com/peds/article/31/7-8/267/5159183) / [PMC6277174](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6277174/).
- **H3 dominates the VHH paratope**: H3 contributes "**46%** of all Nb:antigen contacts" versus **38%** for classical antibody H3 loops, and H3 "remained the only loop involved in the paratope in all Nb structures" — [Mitchell & Colwell, PEDS 2018](https://academic.oup.com/peds/article/31/7-8/267/5159183).
- **FR2 is a genuine paratope component**: "significantly increased nanobody framework-2 (FR2) participation in antigen contact, directly correlated with enhanced solvent exposure in this region compared to antibodies ... because nanobodies lack a partnering VL domain that would otherwise shield these positions" — [Mitchell & Colwell, PEDS 2018](https://academic.oup.com/peds/article/31/7-8/267/5159183). Supporting: "residues across FR2 form direct contacts with target, representing a common mode of nanobody–target binding" `[snippet-only]` — [eLife 2021, stabilizing nanobodies for intracellular expression](https://elifesciences.org/articles/68253).
- **FR2 usage is coupled to CDR-H3 conformation, with numbers**: "to maintain the convex binding mode, VHH/antigen structures with an extended CDR-H3 conformation utilize the solvent-exposed FWR2 for antigen binding. The mean paratope area contributed by FWR2 is larger in antibodies with extended CDR-H3 structures than in antibodies with kinked CDR-H3 structures (**82 Å² vs 32 Å²**)" `[snippet-only]` — [*VHH CDR-H3 conformation is determined by VH germline usage*, Commun Biol 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-023-05241-y) / [PMC10439903](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10439903/).
- **Convex paratope**: "the single-chain structure of nanobodies contributes to the convex shape of the paratopes, in comparison to conventional antibodies whose paratopes are generally wider and form a flat surface, a groove or cavity"; VHHs "preferentially recognize structured, rigid, concave and conserved epitopes" `[snippet-only]` — surfaced from [Nanobodies: from high-throughput identification to therapeutic development, Mol Cell Proteomics 2024](https://www.mcponline.org/article/S1535-9476(24)00155-5/fulltext) and [Structural basis of epitope recognition by heavy-chain camelid antibodies, JMB 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022283618307678).
- **Multiple orientations on one antigen**: "a nanobody repertoire maximizes sampling of an antigen surface by binding a single antigen in **at least three different orientations**, which are correlated with their paratope composition", with categories **Side-on, Side-on CDR3, Head-on** "determined by their orientation when bound to antigen" `[snippet-only]` `[attribution unverified — surfaced in search alongside]` [Mol Cell Proteomics 2024 nanobody review](https://www.mcponline.org/article/S1535-9476(24)00155-5/fulltext) and [*Unique binding and stabilization mechanisms employed by and engineered into nanobodies*, bioRxiv 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.10.22.563475.full.pdf). The report writer should confirm which of these defines the three categories before citing the taxonomy.
- **CDR3 plasticity is conformational/length-based, not substitution-based**: "their elongated CDR3 loop ... can fold back onto the framework, form intradomain disulfides with CDR1, or extend outward to access deep cavities" `[snippet-only]` — [Nanobodies: from discovery to AI-driven design, Biology 2025;14:547](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-7737/14/5/547). Framework contribution to CDR3 conformation and stability: [Molecular basis for thermal stability and affinity in a VHH, PMC9601775](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9601775/).
- **Nanobody paratope ensembles**: VHH paratopes populate multiple interconverting states in solution (MD + NMR) — [Nanobody paratope ensembles in solution, PMC9141556](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9141556). Relevant because it means a VHH's *predicted* pose can be genuinely bimodal (cf. EasyNano's bimodal ipTM basins) without any mutation at all.
- **Framework-swap caution**: "mutating nanobodies to adhere to consensus frameworks likely minimizes impact on target binding, compared to strategies that rely on grafting variable loops onto established nanobody frameworks" `[snippet-only]` — [eLife 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/68253). I.e. loop grafting onto a new framework *is* the intervention that risks changing binding — the framework carries pose information.

### Inferences
- For a VHH, two of the three determinants of pose identified above sit *outside* a CDR-substitution budget: the VH germline framework (which sets CDR-H3 conformational class, Commun Biol 2023) and FR2 surface residues (which are part of the paratope in a large fraction of structures, PEDS 2018). A 29-position CDR-only design space with 5 edits is operating on the smallest of the three levers.
- Because H3 alone contributes 46% of contacts and is the only loop present in every VHH paratope, CDR3 *length* is the dominant orientational variable in this scaffold — the same variable that produces the IGHV3-53 180° flip in Fabs.
- The high repertoire-level orientational diversity of VHHs is sometimes read as "VHH poses are plastic, so they should be easy to steer". The structures say the opposite mechanism: diversity across *different* VHHs with *different* loop lengths and framework exposure, not plasticity of a *given* VHH under substitution.

### Gaps
- No source quantified, for a fixed VHH scaffold, how much rotation a given number of CDR substitutions produces. That experiment appears not to have been done.
- I could not find a head-to-head quantitative statement that VHHs have more or less *orientational freedom per-clone* than Fabs; the PEDS 2018 comparison is of paratope composition diversity across a repertoire, which is a different quantity.

## Q6. Evidence from design: has any campaign reoriented a binder by sequence mutation alone on a fixed backbone?

### Takeaway
**I found no report — computational or experimental — of a sequence-space search reorienting a binder on a fixed backbone.** Every design method that successfully controls orientation does so by specifying or generating the *backbone and docking geometry*, then using fixed-backbone sequence design to realise it. The most directly comparable method to the project (gradient-based CDR design through a frozen structure predictor) states outright that the predicted pose is set by the *framework* sequence and adds an explicit structural prior to **hold the pose fixed** while CDRs are optimised.

### Cited Findings
- **The on-point statement.** EasyNano, which optimises CDR residue logits by gradient descent through an ESMFold2 distogram with an epitope-proximity loss, states: "**ESMFold2's predicted complex pose is determined primarily by the framework sequence**" — [EasyNano, arXiv:2606.12772](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12772). The method's response is a "Full ESMFold2 (1.3B) CA-coordinate structure prior [that] prevents framework pose drift" and "anchor[s] the framework pose during CDR optimization" — [arXiv:2606.12772](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12772) / [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12772). Pose drift is called "a central concern", and the prior weight shows "a U-shaped effect: w_prior>0.3 over-constrained CDRs; w_prior<0.05 caused pose drift".
- EasyNano's own mutation counts are **far above 5** and still produce no reported reorientation: Ty1/RBD "11 of 22 positions" changed (50%), KN035/PD-L1 "7 of 32" (22%), anti-TNF "10/19" (53%); gains are reported as **ipTM** improvements (up to +0.559, 0.143→0.702 for Ty1/RBD; 4.6-fold 0.117→0.538 on a manually docked AQP4 framework), with binder RMSD changes of −1.45 Å (Ty1), +1.81 Å (KN035), and Δ<0.6 Å for already-strong binders — [arXiv:2606.12772](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12772). These are interface-quality changes at an approximately preserved pose, not reorientations. No rotation angles or DockQ values are reported.
- **Fixed-backbone design holds geometry constant by construction**: ProteinMPNN "is a fixed-backbone inverse folding model that assumes the input PDB backbone is close to the desired folded state and does not optimize or relax coordinates", "keeps the protein backbone fixed", and "sees only backbone atoms" so it "cannot represent target side-chains at the binding interface" `[snippet-only]` — surfaced from [Redesign selective protein binders using contrastive decoding, bioRxiv 2026](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.09.722041v1.full) and [ProteinMPNN docs](https://docs.adaptyvbio.com/wiki/models/proteinmpnn). The pose is an *input*, not an output.
- **MutDock states the fixed-backbone scaffold assumption explicitly**: it is designed for scaffolds (affibodies, DARPins) where "mutations do not alter the proteins' structures", and it does **not** claim mutations change rigid-body orientation — instead, "docking fixed-backbone scaffolds with a varied group of surface amino acids increases the chances of identifying superior starting poses that can be improved with subsequent mutations" — [MutDock, PMC9465448](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9465448/). The design logic is: **search poses by docking, then mutate to improve the chosen pose.** Mutation improves a pose; docking chooses it.
- **Successful orientation control comes from backbone generation, not sequence search**: a cryo-EM structure of a designed influenza VHH "resolved to 3.0 Å and aligned with the computational model at **1.45 Å RMSD** for the backbone and **0.8 Å** for the CDR3 loop"; designs "closely match the experimentally determined complex in structure, epitope targeting and overall conformation"; "cryo-EM structures of two scFvs confirmed accurate docking and near-atomic agreement across all six CDRs" — [Bennett et al., Nature 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09721-5) `[snippet-only]`; [RosettaCommons summary](https://rosettacommons.org/2025/11/24/rfantibody-creates-epitope-specific-antibody-binders-with-atomic-level-agreement/). The pipeline sets the pose by *diffusing the CDR backbone against a specified epitope*, then designs sequence onto it.
- **Fold/topology control likewise comes from structural losses, not sequence search**: FoldCraft uses "a contact map similarity loss" to enable "precise generation of binders with user-defined structural folds" — [*Fold-conditioned de novo binder design via AlphaFold2-Multimer hallucination*, bioRxiv 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.02.662497v1).
- **The "right epitope, wrong pose" failure region is documented in predictors**: in an AF3 antibody benchmark, "approximately **34% of false negatives retained the correct epitope location despite poor structural alignment**, suggesting that conformation refinement tools could recover additional true binding predictions"; overall recall 50% (max 53% at 100 inference seeds), innate false-positive rate ~3%, and AF3 "hallucinate[s] plausible binding interfaces across the surface of decoy targets while avoiding disordered regions" — [Solanki et al., bioRxiv 2026.07.30.741792 (31 July 2026)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.30.741792v1). **Caution:** a search snippet additionally attributed to this work a claim that "the majority of nanobody–antigen complexes exhibit side-dock geometries" in AF3/RoseTTAFold training data, biasing designs toward wrong orientations; the abstract I retrieved does **not** contain that claim, and it may belong to [*Discontinuous epitope fragments as sufficient target templates for efficient binder design*, arXiv:2509.25479](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25479) `[attribution unverified — do not cite to Solanki et al.]`.

### Inferences
- This is the project's central actionable result. The design field's revealed preference is unanimous: **orientation is a backbone/docking-geometry variable that you specify, and sequence design is the step that makes a specified orientation realisable.** No published campaign inverts that order.
- EasyNano is the closest published analogue to the project's setup (differentiable structure-predictor gradients, epitope-targeted CDR-only design, nanobody scaffold) and it independently discovered the same constraint, then engineered *around* it with a pose-anchoring prior rather than through it. That it had to add a prior to *prevent* pose drift, while reporting that pose is framework-determined, means CDR logit gradients in that regime move the interface but not the pose.
- The 34% "right epitope, wrong alignment" figure gives an independent estimate that this failure region is large and is a predictor/geometry property, not an optimiser pathology — consistent with the project's observed 12–52° and 156–178° populations.

### Gaps
- I found **no** paper that attempts, and reports failing at, orientation change by fixed-backbone sequence mutation. The absence is of the "nobody tried and published it" kind rather than a published negative result. This should be stated as such: the claim "no one has demonstrated sequence-only reorientation of a binder" is supported by absence plus the explicit contrary design logic of MutDock, ProteinMPNN and EasyNano — not by a dedicated negative-result paper.
- I could not verify RFdiffusion-antibody failure-mode statistics (how many designs bound the correct epitope in a wrong orientation) because the Nature full text was behind an authentication redirect.

## Q7. Rigid-body versus side-chain degrees of freedom (docking literature)

### Takeaway
Docking practice is built on the premise that point mutations modulate affinity *within* a preserved complex architecture. Mutation-effect predictors assume the wild-type pose; scaffold-design dockers treat sequence as a per-pose refinement; and the structural observation underlying all of it is that point mutations perturb a couple of neighbouring side chains, not the rigid-body transform.

### Cited Findings
- "Mutations in either the enzyme or the inhibitor protein often lead to a modulation of the binding affinity **with no major alterations in the 3D structure of the complex**", and "the architecture of the mutated complexes is almost the same as that of the wild type and no major conformational changes occur upon binding" — [*In silico screening of mutational effects on enzyme-proteic inhibitor affinity: a docking-based approach*, BMC Struct Biol 2007, PMC1913526](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1913526/) / [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1472-6807-7-37). This assumption is what makes docking-based ΔΔG screening viable at all.
- Mutation-effect predictors operate on the wild-type complex structure: multi-point antibody mutation effects are predicted from the existing complex, not by re-docking — [mmCSM-AB, PMC7319589](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7319589/); interface-mapping of missense mutations is done by docking-based modelling of the *same* interface — [PMC5571915](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5571915/).
- **Local-perturbation statistic (use with care):** a figure surfaced in search that "in 91–95% of cases, two or fewer residues underwent side-chain conformational change near point mutations, and if mutation sites with backbone displacements were excluded, the number increased to 97%" `[snippet-only]` `[attribution unverified]`. I attempted to confirm this against [MutDock, PMC9465448](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9465448/) and the retrieved text explicitly **does not contain** those figures. The likeliest actual source is [*Protein–protein docking with multiple residue conformations and residue substitutions*, PMC2373613](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2373613/), which I could not retrieve. **Do not cite these percentages without checking that paper.**
- Side-chain modelling in docking is handled as a *rotamer/substitution* search at a given pose — [PMC2373613](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2373613/); flexibility treatments are framed as selected-fit vs induced-fit refinements of an existing complex, probed mutationally — [Selected-fit versus induced-fit protein binding, arXiv:0809.0637](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.0637).
- The deliberate engineering of a **conformational** switch to control specificity exists as a separate, harder intervention — [*A designed conformational shift to control protein binding specificity*, PMC4497613](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4497613/) `[could not retrieve — fetch blocked; number of mutations and whether the rigid-body pose or only a loop conformation changed is unverified]`. Flagged as a lead: if it reports a *pose* change from few mutations it would be the strongest counterexample in this file, and it should be checked.
- Related DMS-plus-structure signal that a mutation can change *conformational state occupancy* without changing affinity: "H505W ... decreases receptor binding in the spike DMS dataset despite having no meaningful effect on ACE2-binding affinity in the yeast-display data; this pattern suggests that H505W biases RBDs toward the down conformation" `[snippet-only]` — surfaced from the SARS-CoV-2 RBD DMS literature, e.g. [Starr et al. DMS of Omicron BA.1/BA.2 RBDs, PLoS Pathog 2022](https://journals.plos.org/plospathogens/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.ppat.1010951) / [PMC9674177](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9674177/). Note this is a mutation shifting an *antigen's* pre-existing conformational equilibrium, the same "select among existing basins" mechanism as EasyNano's W116Y — not creation of a new pose.

### Inferences
- Across docking, mutation-ΔΔG prediction and scaffold design, the rigid-body transform is treated as an input and sequence as a scorer/refiner. There is no established mechanism by which side-chain identity *drives* a rigid-body transform; the only documented sequence→pose mechanisms are (i) shifting occupancy between basins the backbone already admits and (ii) changing the backbone (length, class, framework).
- This gives a clean prediction for the project's loss landscape: a CDR-substitution search can be expected to improve interface quality metrics (ipTM/ipSAE, contacts, buried area) monotonically while the pose stays in whichever basin the starting framework and loop lengths put it in. That is exactly a 12–52°-rotated-but-well-packed design.

### Gaps
- I did not find a study that systematically measures how often a point mutation changes the *docked orientation* of a protein–protein complex (e.g. a re-docking study over SKEMPI with pose-change as the readout). No source reported a "typical sensitivity of docking orientation to point mutations" in degrees.

## Q8. Bottom line for the project

### Takeaway
State it plainly: **no published work — in silico or experimental — demonstrates that a sequence-space search over a handful of CDR point substitutions reorients a binder on its target.** The documented determinants of approach angle and docking polarity are germline V-gene identity, CDR3 length/indels, and canonical backbone class; the documented effect of CDR point substitutions is affinity, preorganisation and rigidification *within* a conserved pose. A 5-substitution, fixed-length, CDR-only budget edits the one variable that the structural evidence says does not set orientation. A null result from such a search is a property of the feasible set, not a failure of the optimiser.

### Cited Findings
Consolidated, highest-weight evidence:
- **Orientation determinant is length, not substitution** — same germline V gene, 180° Fab rotation about its long axis, discriminated by CDR H3 length ≤10 vs ≥15 aa: [Cell Reports 2020, PMC7522650](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7522650/), PDBs [7JMP](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7JMP)/[7JMO](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7JMO).
- **Orientation determinant is germline V gene** — 180° reversed docking polarity attributable to germline-encoded recognition: [Immunity 2016](https://www.cell.com/immunity/fulltext/S1074-7613(16)30351-X); VHH CDR-H3 conformation determined by VH germline usage: [Commun Biol 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-023-05241-y).
- **Orientation determinant is backbone class** — canonical CDR classes defined on backbone dihedrals with a 40° clustering cutoff, set by loop length plus key positions: [North et al., PMC3065967](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3065967/); [PyIgClassify2](https://dunbrack2.fccc.edu/pyigclassify2/doc/BIORXIV-2022-511988v1-Dunbrack.pdf).
- **Point substitutions change affinity, not pose** — three single interface Tyr substitutions in D1.3, affinity down 10/4/70×, "minimal shifts in the positions of the remaining protein atoms": [Dall'Acqua et al. 1996, PMID 8952503](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8952503/).
- **Much larger mutation loads also preserve pose** — 9 somatic mutations, 10³–10⁴× affinity, same site, mechanism (induced fit → lock and key) change only: [Science 1997](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.276.5319.1665); germline-conserved contacts in the PGT121 lineage and "rather than universal reorientation, affinity maturation employs diverse strategies": [Front Immunol 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.00117/full); designed TcdB VHHs "bound in the intended orientation, both before and after affinity maturation": [Nature 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09721-5).
- **Pose is many-to-one over sequence** — 43% CDRH3 identity, same binding mode: [PMC10544996](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10544996/).
- **The design field says pose is a framework/backbone variable** — "ESMFold2's predicted complex pose is determined primarily by the framework sequence", with an explicit prior added to prevent pose drift during CDR optimisation: [arXiv:2606.12772](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12772); MutDock's premise that scaffold "mutations do not alter the proteins' structures" and that docking picks the pose which mutation then improves: [PMC9465448](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9465448/); ProteinMPNN "keeps the protein backbone fixed": [ProteinMPNN docs](https://docs.adaptyvbio.com/wiki/models/proteinmpnn).
- **The failure region is real and large in predictors** — 34% of AF3 antibody false negatives had the correct epitope with poor structural alignment: [bioRxiv 2026.07.30.741792](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.30.741792v1).
- **The one documented single-mutation pose effect is basin selection, at a framework position** — W116Y collapsed a bimodal ipTM pose distribution to a single mode: [arXiv:2606.12772](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12772).

### Inferences
- Two testable, decision-relevant predictions follow for the project:
  1. **The 156–178° population is almost certainly unreachable** from a fixed CDR3 length and fixed framework. Its natural analogues (IGHV3-53 mode A/B; reversed TCR docking) are produced by ≥5-residue CDR3 length differences and by different germline V genes. Measure it: check whether the reference pose and the recovered flipped poses would require different CDR3 lengths or FR2 exposure to be optimal.
  2. **The 12–52° population may be partly reachable, but as basin selection rather than steering.** If the frozen predictor already places the scaffold in a bimodal pose distribution (as EasyNano's unmodified AQP4 framework did, and as VHH paratope-ensemble studies would predict), then a few substitutions — including *framework* substitutions, which the current budget excludes — can shift which basin dominates. That is a different search problem from continuous rotation and should be instrumented as such (count basins, not degrees).
- Design-space implication, stated in the field's own terms: to make orientation reachable the search must include at least one of (a) CDR3 length / indels, (b) framework and FR2 positions, (c) germline/scaffold choice, or (d) explicit backbone generation against the specified pose, as RFdiffusion-antibody does. Keeping the 5-substitution CDR-only budget and expecting reorientation is asking sequence to do a job the structural literature assigns to the backbone.
- A corollary for how to report a null: because orientation appears to be outside the feasible set, the informative experiment is not "did the search reorient the binder" but "did the search improve interface quality at an unchanged pose". If it did, the optimiser worked and the parameterisation was wrong.

### Gaps
- **No dedicated negative-result paper exists.** The claim "nobody has shown sequence-only reorientation" rests on (i) my failure to find any such report across ~15 distinct queries spanning structural immunology, affinity maturation, nanobody structure, docking and design, and (ii) the explicit fixed-pose premises of MutDock, ProteinMPNN and EasyNano. It should be reported with that epistemic status, not as a cited impossibility proof.
- Unverified leads worth one follow-up each before publication: (1) [*A designed conformational shift to control protein binding specificity*, PMC4497613](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4497613/) — potential counterexample, fetch blocked; (2) the 91–95%/97% side-chain-perturbation statistic, likely [PMC2373613](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2373613/), **not** MutDock; (3) the Side-on / Side-on-CDR3 / Head-on nanobody orientation taxonomy and the "at least three orientations" claim, primary source uncertain between [Mol Cell Proteomics 2024](https://www.mcponline.org/article/S1535-9476(24)00155-5/fulltext) and [bioRxiv 2023.10.22.563475](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.10.22.563475.full.pdf); (4) the nanobody "side-dock geometry" training-set bias claim, **not** confirmed in the Solanki et al. abstract; (5) RFdiffusion-antibody wrong-orientation failure statistics, Nature full text inaccessible.
- A systematic survey of rotation-angle differences between paired germline/mature or wild-type/mutant antibody–antigen complexes does not appear to exist. That is the measurement the project's question most directly needs, and the field has not made it.
