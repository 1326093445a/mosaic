# The "high interface confidence, wrong binding site" failure mode in AF-guided binder design: names, documented instances, detection, controls, mitigations

**Scope note / headline caveat for the report writer.** The specific failure the user describes — a design that reaches *native-level* predicted interface confidence (ipSAE 0.77 vs 0.795) while docking 14–27 Å away from the specified epitope, with correct fold and a large 36–50 residue interface — is **not a named, well-documented, experimentally-confirmed failure mode in the published literature**. What *is* documented, and documented well, are four adjacent things that together make the user's observation expected rather than anomalous: (1) binding to the *wrong target* ("off-design hits"); (2) binding mode / docking-pose deviation from the design model; (3) AF interface confidence being a poor and adversarially-satisfiable discriminator, especially for antibody–antigen; (4) the near-total absence of negative-control *targets* (scrambled/permuted epitopes) in design papers. The user's permuted-epitope control scoring ~2× the real target at matched compute is, as far as I can find, a **control almost nobody in the field runs** — which is itself the most important finding here. Below, computational-only vs experimentally-confirmed is flagged throughout.

## Q1. Is "off-target epitope" / "off-epitope binding" / "wrong binding site" a named, documented failure of AF-based binder design? What vocabulary do papers use?

### Takeaway
There is **no single accepted term** for "high AF interface confidence at the wrong site on the right target." The field's vocabulary covers wrong *target* ("off-design hits", "sticky targets", "polyreactivity"), wrong *pose* ("binding mode deviation", "design failure", "framework-mediated interaction"), and wrong *confidence* ("hallucination", "adversarial example", "confidence failure") — but the specific concept the user is describing is closest to an unnamed intersection of "epitope/hotspot recall failure" and "adversarial satisfaction of the design loss."

### Cited Findings
- **"Off-design hits" vs "on-design hits"** is the clearest established vocabulary, but it denotes binding to a *non-cognate target*, not an off-epitope site on the cognate target. mBER defines: on-design hit = "the designed target matches the experimental hit target"; off-design hit = "the designed target differs from the experimental hit target" — [mBER preprint (bioRxiv 2025.09.26.678877)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.26.678877v1.full)
- **"Sticky targets"** — mBER's term for targets that absorb disproportionate non-cognate binding, visible as "column-like striations" in all-vs-all heatmaps — [mBER](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.26.678877v1.full)
- **"Design failure" for binding-mode deviation at the *correct* epitope.** Per a search-result summary of the published RFantibody paper: one designed VHH "bound the correct RBD epitope but its binding mode deviated significantly from the design model, adopting a framework-mediated interaction instead, which was classified as a design failure" — [Atomically accurate de novo design of antibodies with RFdiffusion, Nature 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09721-5). **CONFLICT / VERIFICATION GAP:** I could not reach the published Nature text (paywall redirect), and a direct full-text read of the 2024 preprint returned *no* such VHH — the preprint describes only CDR-mediated binders. So this instance is probably in the published version's expanded experimental section, but I could not verify it firsthand. Treat as unverified. — [RFdiffusion antibody preprint (bioRxiv 2024.03.14.585103)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.14.585103v1.full)
- **"Hotspot conservation" (HC), with HC-precision and HC-recall** is the closest thing to a formal metric for "did the design actually land on the specified epitope," reported as HC-precision 0.813 → 0.889 and HC-recall 0.878 → 0.935 for a guided method vs baseline — [De novo epitope-specific antibody design via time-dependent guidance, Bioinformatics 2026](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/42/Supplement_2/btag455/8767312)
- **"Adversarial example" / "confidence hacking"** is the vocabulary for the predictor-exploitation side: "Optimizing sequences for high structure prediction confidence runs the risk of generating adversarial examples for AlphaFold which are unlikely to be recombinantly expressed in E. coli or adopt the designed tertiary structure" — [ESMFold Hallucinates Native-Like Protein Sequences (MLSB 2023)](https://www.mlsb.io/papers_2023/ESMFold_Hallucinates_Native-Like_Protein_Sequences.pdf)
- **"Confidence failures" / "nonphysical structural invariance"** — the 2026 adversarial-mutation paper's framing — [Adversarial Sequence Mutations in AlphaFold and ESMFold (bioRxiv 2026.02.25.708002)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.25.708002v1)
- **"Panreactivity" / "poly-specificity"** is the developability-side vocabulary for designs that stick to albumin, off-target cell-surface receptors and plate plastic — [Ranomics, "Why AI-Designed Binders Fail Wet Lab"](https://www.ranomics.com/de-novo-protein-design-failure-modes) *(commercial blog, low-authority; use only as vocabulary evidence, not as a data source)*

### Inferences
- Because the field's named failure modes are *wrong target* and *wrong pose*, a design that is on-target, correctly folded, and has a large interface but sits 14–27 Å off the specified epitope would, in most published pipelines, simply **not be detected as a failure at all** — it would pass ipTM/ipSAE/PAE filters and be scored as a success until a structure was solved.
- The user's phenomenon is best described to referees as a **hotspot/epitope-recall failure under an unconstrained interface objective**, i.e. the design loss was satisfied by *any* interface rather than the intended one. "Epitope drift" and "off-epitope docking" are descriptive but not established terms.

### Gaps
- No paper I found names or systematically quantifies "high confidence at the wrong site on the right target." If the user wants to claim novelty in *naming* this, the literature appears to leave the slot open.

## Q2. Documented instances: designs that scored well on ipTM/pLDDT/PAE but bound the wrong site, or scored well and did not bind at all

### Takeaway
Experimentally-confirmed **wrong-epitope** instances are essentially absent from the literature — not because they don't happen, but because almost no designs get structures. Experimentally-confirmed **"scored well, didn't bind"** instances are abundant and quantified: AF-filtered designs routinely fail at 45–96%, and in one case a confidence filter produced **no enrichment at all**.

### Cited Findings — "scored well, did not bind" (experimentally confirmed)
- **A structure-prediction confidence filter gave zero enrichment.** RFdiffusion antibody design: "Surprisingly, design success rates were not significantly higher for filtered designs vs unfiltered designs with the RF2 settings we used." — [RFdiffusion antibody preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.14.585103v1.full)
- **AlphaFold2 is explicitly stated to be unusable as an antibody-design filter:** "AlphaFold2 fails to routinely predict antibody-antigen structures accurately, preventing its use as a filter in an antibody design pipeline." — [RFdiffusion antibody preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.14.585103v1.full)
- **Germinal (nanobody CDR design under AF-Multimer/AF3 confidence — closest analogue to the user's setup):** per-target outcomes, designs sampled → tested → bound: PD-L1 101 → 25 → 7; IL3 46 → 11 → 2; IL20 43 → 11 → 4; BHRF1 52 → 20 → 11. Overall "4–22% success rates across all targets." So **45–82% of AF-confidence-filtered, hand-picked designs did not bind** — [Germinal (PMC12485712)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12485712/); published as [Nat Biotechnol 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03187-0)
- **RFdiffusion antibody hit rates from large screens:** ~9,000 designs screened per target yielded best affinities of 1.4 µM (RSV site III), 78 nM (influenza HA), 5.5 µM (SARS-CoV-2 RBD); 262 nM from 95 designs for TcdB; "28 VHHs confirmed to bind their targets by SPR" in total — [RFdiffusion antibody preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.14.585103v1.full)
- **BindCraft per-target experimental success rates** (all designs passing an 8-criterion AF2-confidence filter set): PD-1 13/53 (24.5%), PD-L1 7/9, IFNAR2 3/9, CD45 4/16, BBF-14 6/11, SAS-6 1/9 (11.1%), Der f7 4/10, Der f21 4/7, Bet v1 2/7, SpCas9 6/6 — [BindCraft preprint (bioRxiv 2024.09.30.615802)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full); published as [Nature 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09429-6)
- **BindCraft reports no ROC/discrimination statistics for its filters.** A full-text read found "no explicit ROC curves or binder vs. non-binder separation statistics are provided; success rates are reported post-hoc rather than predictive filtering accuracy." — [BindCraft preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full)
- **mBER's confidence–binding relationship, measured at scale** (the best-powered data on this question): median hit rate across targets ~0.4%; filtering ipTM > 0.7 moved hit rates into "the double-digit percent range"; ipTM > 0.8 gave "majority of hit rates surpassing 1%"; best epitopes reached "hit rates as high as 38%" under strict ipTM filtering. pLDDT "performed comparably or worse than ipTM." Critically, **off-design hit rates were "relatively flat across ipTM bins"** — i.e. ipTM enriched for on-target binding without reducing off-target binding — [mBER](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.26.678877v1.full)

### Cited Findings — wrong-site / wrong-pose instances
- **Binding-mode deviation while still at the intended epitope** is the documented form. BindCraft's Der f21 binder10 has 3.1 Å backbone RMSD to the design model, "caused by an alternative rotamer conformation of an interface tyrosine" — [BindCraft preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full)
- **A designed binder hit the same epitope as a reference antibody but with a different binding mode:** BindCraft's design targets "a known epitope recognized by the REGN5713 antibody, albeit with a different binding mode" — [BindCraft Nature 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09429-6)
- **Framework-mediated (non-paratope) engagement classified as failure** — see the unverified RFantibody/Nature 2025 item in Q1. Germinal treats this risk prospectively, not retrospectively: it adds a "paratope-specific loss (binding through CDRs, not framework)" to its design objective — [Germinal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12485712/)
- **Highest-affinity candidates binding outside the intended epitope** was reported in a search summary of [De novo design of epitope-specific antibodies via a structure-driven computational workflow, Nat Commun 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67361-9) ("there have been cases where the highest-affinity candidates bind outside the intended epitope"). **VERIFICATION GAP:** paywall redirect blocked the full text; I could not confirm the wording, the detection method, or the counts. This is the single most promising lead for a directly on-point documented instance and should be chased down in the full paper + supplement.
- **AlphaFold docks non-binders into binding sites.** A search-level summary states AlphaFold "showed considerably poorer discrimination between binders and non-binders" than specialized methods and "tended to dock non-binding peptides in the binding groove," attributed to "the lack of training of AlphaFold on negative binding examples" — [Peptide-binding specificity prediction using fine-tuned structure prediction networks, PNAS 2023](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2216697120). *Not verified in full text; the quoted phrasing came from a search aggregation.* The mechanism it describes — a predictor with no negative-example training placing anything in a plausible pocket — is the direct explanation for the user's permuted-epitope control scoring high.

### Inferences
- The combination of "RF2 filter gave no enrichment" + "ipTM enriches on-design but not against off-design" + "no ROC stats in BindCraft" means the field has **never demonstrated that AF interface confidence discriminates correct-epitope from incorrect-epitope engagement.** It has only demonstrated that it enriches for *some* binding.
- The user's result (permuted epitope scoring ~2× the real target) is the *expected* behaviour of a predictor with no negative training signal, given the PNAS observation and mBER's flat off-design curve. It is strong evidence that ipSAE on a designed complex measures "is this a self-consistent interface geometry," not "is this the native interface."

### Gaps
- No paper reports a designed binder whose solved structure placed it at a *different epitope* than specified. If one exists it is probably buried in a supplement; I did not find it.

## Q3. Adversarial / decoy / negative controls in design papers: who scrambles or mutates the target?

### Takeaway
**Almost nobody.** Negative controls in de novo binder design are overwhelmingly negative-control *binders* (an unrelated nanobody) or negative-control *surfaces* (BSA, empty wells, lysozyme) — not negative-control *targets*. I found no design paper that permutes or scrambles the target epitope and re-runs the pipeline to check that the design score is target-specific. This makes the user's control methodologically unusual and, on the evidence, novel in this setting.

### Cited Findings
- **Germinal: unrelated-antigen nanobodies as negative controls (negative binders, not negative targets).** "Negative control nanobodies against TNFα were used for PD-L1 and IL3 screens, while nanobodies against PD-L1 were used as negative control for IL20 and BHRF1 screens"; off-target assessed via a split-luciferase assay with an "off-target protein-SmBiT fusion"; "no strong cross-reactivity" observed, with the authors conceding "more comprehensive poly-specificity measurements will be required" — [Germinal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12485712/)
- **BindCraft: minimal specificity controls.** A full-text read found the PD-1 BLI screen used "a random protein" (lysozyme) as a control, and "no systematic off-target or scrambled-sequence controls are described. No experiments with non-cognate targets or randomly assigned hotspots are presented to demonstrate specificity robustness." — [BindCraft preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full)
- **mBER: empty-well controls to exclude plastic binders**, plus the all-vs-all panel itself as a de facto off-target control: "we include a substantial number of empty wells in addition to wells containing target proteins, to enhance statistical power and rule out plastic-binding hits." Explicit scrambled or decoy *targets* are not described — [mBER](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.26.678877v1.full)
- **Random/scrambled controls appear on the *binder* axis in ML-model evaluation, not the target axis:** negative-control datasets are built "where each target is coupled to random binders by concatenating amino acids uniformly at random," and "the computational design population fraction increas[es] over that of the scrambled control sequences with increasing selection stringency" — [De novo design of peptide binders to conformationally diverse targets with contrastive language modeling, Sci Adv](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adr8638)
- **Decoy-complex discrimination is measured in the structure-prediction literature, not the design literature.** A fine-tuned RF2 used for distinguishing true from decoy antibody–antigen complexes achieved "with pAE < 10, 80.3% of structures are within 2 Å when 100% of 'hotspots' are provided" — note the dependence on supplying the full hotspot set — [RFdiffusion antibody preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.14.585103v1.full)
- **Non-interacting-pair benchmarks exist for scoring functions.** ipSAE was benchmarked on "40 heterodimer complexes in the PDB … and 70 non-interacting sequence pairs from the same set" — [ipSAE preprint (bioRxiv 2025.02.10.637595v2)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.10.637595v2.full)
- **AlphaFold-Multimer PPI-screening practice does recommend negative controls**: "Negative controls are important to identify an expected rate of false positives," and false positives arise "where domains may be inaccurately swapped or the structures may be predicted to bind somewhere where they are known not to interact" — [Using AlphaFold Multimer to discover interkingdom protein–protein interactions, Plant J 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/tpj.16969)

### Inferences
- The *PPI-screening* community (AlphaPulldown, Predictomes, interkingdom screens) has internalised the need for decoy/negative-control pairs; the *binder-design* community has not imported that discipline. The user is effectively applying PPI-screening hygiene to a design loop, which is why the result looks alarming relative to published practice.
- "Predicted to bind somewhere they are known not to interact" (Plant J) is the closest published statement to the user's failure mode — but it concerns *prediction* of natural pairs, not *designed* binders.

### Gaps
- I found **no** design paper that runs a permuted/scrambled-epitope target as a compute-matched negative control. If the user's claim is "this control is not standard practice," the literature supports that claim.
- I could not find published numbers for "score achieved against a scrambled target vs the real target at matched compute" in any design paper — so there is no baseline to compare the user's ~2× figure against.

## Q4. Known failure analyses of AF2/AF3 interface confidence as a binding predictor

### Takeaway
This is the best-documented part of the question. AF interface confidence is demonstrably (a) adversarially satisfiable, (b) a poor model selector, (c) structurally mis-weighted for antibody–antigen specifically, and (d) high for known non-interactors under some conditions.

### Cited Findings
- **Adversarial robustness failure (AF3, 2026, systematic):** across 200 proteins, "predicted structures remain invariant to mutations of up to 40% of residues — including deliberately destabilizing substitutions — and to deletions of 10%." At 70% mutation, TM-scores remain "remarkably high," declining "only gradually." Confidence correlates with mutation severity only modestly (Pearson r = −0.558, Spearman ρ = −0.544). At 20% mutation the mean AF3 ranking score is still **0.62**, "barely below the 0.6 high-confidence threshold." — [Adversarial Sequence Mutations in AlphaFold and ESMFold](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.25.708002v1)
- **Confidence tracks template availability, not biophysics:** ranking score correlates with template structural quality (r = 0.39, p = 7.8 × 10⁻⁵), supporting the conclusion that AF "may rely heavily on memorized templates rather than biophysical reasoning," with implications the authors state explicitly "for protein design, drug discovery, and modeling workflows" — [same](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.25.708002v1)
- **Confidence is a bad model-selector:** AF3 picks the most accurate structure "approximately 25% of the time"; AF2 "at most 35%"; selection errors cost up to "0.17 TM-score points" and **"over 0.40 DockQ"** in the worst cases — [same](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.25.708002v1). The DockQ figure is the directly relevant one: confidence-based ranking can select a pose that is 0.4 DockQ worse, i.e. a qualitatively different docking.
- **ESMFold is *more* mutation-sensitive than AF3** ("greater sensitivity to point mutations," "higher sensitivity to mutations known to induce conformational transitions"), which cuts against the user's stored preference for native OpenDDE over ESMFold *on this specific axis* — [same](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.25.708002v1)
- **The antibody–antigen-specific mechanism of confidence failure:** "the AlphaFold2 confidence score fails to identify the best predictions for antibody-antigen targets, which can be explained by the fact that the confidence score for multimers is calculated 80% between chains, heavily biasing it toward the antibody chains rather than antibody-antigen relative positions" — [CAPRI Rounds 47–55 assessment, Proteins 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/prot.26801) (reported via search summary of this and the companion [MassiveFold CAPRI 55 paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/prot.26802))
- **Antibody–antigen is AF's worst category.** AF-Multimer success on antigen–antibody targets is **20%**, vs 43% for AlphaRED (AF + Rosetta replica-exchange docking) — [Reliable protein-protein docking with AlphaFold, Rosetta, and replica-exchange (eLife reviewed preprint)](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/94029). Antibody–antigen complexes were "among the most difficult structures to predict for AlphaFold2 and other tools in the CASP15-CAPRI challenges."
- **ipTM is high for known non-interactors under disorder.** For the known non-interacting pair RAF1–RIPK1, "ipTM from AlphaFold is 0.277, actifpTM was 0.462" — the ipSAE paper's point is that alignment-based interface scores inflate when both chains contain disorder; ipSAE for that pair drops to 0.044 (PAE ≤ 15 Å), vs 0.56–0.73 for true complexes — [ipSAE preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.10.637595v2.full)
- **ipSAE's own stated limitation is exactly the user's question.** A full-text read of the ipSAE preprint found: "The paper does **not** address whether correct interface geometry is predicted alongside high ipSAE scores," no AUC values reported, no numerical interaction threshold proposed, and no false-positive rates quantified — [ipSAE preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.10.637595v2.full)
- **ipSAE's design claim is only about true-vs-false *complexes*, not true-vs-false *interfaces*:** ipSAE "is able to separate true from false complexes more efficiently than AlphaFold2's ipTM score," computed "over interchain residue pairs that pass a PAE cutoff" — [ipSAE preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.10.637595v2.full)
- **ipTM's known blind spot for flexible targets:** ipTM "has a significant limitation in detecting interactions involving proteins with flexible conformations" — [Using AlphaFold Multimer to discover interkingdom PPIs](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/tpj.16969)
- **AF-Multimer PPI screens produce unconfirmable hits:** "Not all hydrolase–protein interactions with [0.8·ipTM + 0.2·pTM] ≥ 0.75 might be true inhibitors … AlphaFold-Multimer can produce false positive hits that cannot be confirmed experimentally" — [same](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/tpj.16969)
- **BindCraft itself acknowledges the mutation-insensitivity problem:** "AF2 has also been shown to be insensitive to the predicted effects of point mutations" and "deep learning models have been shown to sporadically produce physically improbable results" — [BindCraft preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full)

### Inferences
- The ipSAE paper is the correct citation for "ipSAE beats ipTM at true-vs-false complex" **and** for "nobody has validated that ipSAE tracks interface *correctness*." That distinction is the user's whole finding: ipSAE 0.77 on an off-epitope pose is not a contradiction of the ipSAE paper, it is outside its validated claim.
- The CAPRI 80%-interchain-weighting mechanism predicts that for a VHH–antigen complex, confidence can be dominated by how self-consistently the two chains are packed rather than by *where* on the antigen they pack — which is a mechanistic explanation the user can cite directly.
- An ipSAE gap of 0.77 vs 0.795 is well inside the noise of a metric whose own paper reports no AUC, no threshold, and no FP rate. The user should not treat "equal to the true complex" as a strong claim without a null distribution — which their permuted-epitope control in fact provides.

### Gaps
- No published false-positive rate for ipSAE at any threshold. No published distribution of ipSAE for *designed* (as opposed to natural) complexes, so there is no literature baseline for "what ipSAE should a successful de novo design reach."

## Q5. Reported mitigations — and which are reported to work, with numbers

### Takeaway
Three mitigations have real numbers behind them: **epitope-only / trimmed-target inputs** (structurally forbids off-epitope docking), **hotspot-conservation precision/recall as an explicit filter** (0.889 / 0.935), and **physics-based rescoring + orthogonal docking** (AF-Multimer 20% → AlphaRED 43% on antibody–antigen). Cross-model validation with a genuinely held-out predictor is **recommended rhetorically but not benchmarked** anywhere I found — and notably, re-prediction inside the *same* model family is what BindCraft does, which does not address predictor-exploitation.

### Cited Findings
- **Epitope-only target truncation.** Trimming the target "to discontinuous surface residues" as the design input "significantly accelerated sampling and improved design quality while preserving validation performance" — [Discontinuous Epitope Fragments as Sufficient Target Templates for Efficient Binder Design (arXiv 2509.25479)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25479). This is the strongest structural mitigation for the user's problem: if only the epitope is present, a 14–27 Å off-epitope pose is geometrically unavailable.
- **Hotspot-conservation (epitope-recall) filtering, with numbers.** Time-dependent epitope guidance raised HC-precision 0.813 → 0.889 and HC-recall 0.878 → 0.935 — [Bioinformatics 2026](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/42/Supplement_2/btag455/8767312)
- **Hotspot masking of the design loss** is BindCraft's built-in mechanism: hotspots can be "individual residues or entire chains, or can be omitted completely in which case a binding site is selected according to the combined design loss," and when hotspots are designated "the rest of the target is masked from this loss" — [BindCraft preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full). **Important for the user:** this confirms that an *unmasked* loss lets the optimiser choose its own site — exactly the user's 14–27 Å drift.
- **Paratope-restricted loss** (force engagement through CDRs, not framework) — Germinal's custom loss, alongside α-helix and β-strand losses "promoting flexible loops" — [Germinal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12485712/)
- **Physics-based rescoring, reported to help on the antibody–antigen docking problem with numbers:** AlphaRED (AF + Rosetta + replica-exchange docking) lifts antibody–antigen success from AF-Multimer's **20% to 43%** — [eLife reviewed preprint 94029](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/94029). The CAPRI literature's conclusion is that "a combination of both statistics-based approaches like neural networks and physics-based approaches such as Rosetta may predict optimal antibody-antigen complexes" — [CAPRI 47–55](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/prot.26801)
- **Rosetta rescoring in the design loop, proposed but not quantified:** BindCraft mentions adding "an orthogonal physics-based scoring method, such as Rosetta," to improve discrimination; its actual protocol energy-minimises each re-predicted design with Rosetta FastRelax and rescores — but reports no measured improvement in discrimination — [BindCraft preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full)
- **Re-prediction *within* the same model family (what BindCraft actually does):** designs are "re-predicted using the AF2 monomer model, with 3 recycles and 2-template based models in single sequence mode, to ensure robust and unbiased complex assessment." A full-text read confirms "the paper does not report validation using alternative structure prediction methods (e.g., OmegaFold, ESMFold) or prospective binding affinity prediction beyond AF2 confidence metrics." — [BindCraft preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full)
- **Massive sampling as a mitigation for antibody–antigen confidence failure** — the CAPRI 55 MassiveFold strategy, motivated explicitly by AF's poor antibody–antigen performance — [MassiveFold CAPRI 55, Proteins](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/prot.26802); see also [a "greener" AF2 protocol for Ab-Ag modeling from CAPRI 55](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.07.616947.full.pdf)
- **Experimental triage by epitope-directed mutagenesis — the most practical reported detection method.** Germinal introduced alanine substitutions at predicted interface residues on PD-L1 (I54, Y56, E58); each "reduced binding signal and weakened affinity by >10-fold," and crucially "nanobodies with distinct binding modes were differentially affected, with no single mutation abolishing binding across all designs" — the differential pattern is the evidence of genuine epitope engagement — [Germinal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12485712/)
- **Competition assays as epitope confirmation.** BindCraft used competition assays for PD-L1, IFNAR2 and Bet v1 to validate that binders engage the intended epitope — [BindCraft preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full). AlphaProteo confirms intended-epitope binding "through competitive inhibition assays and mutations disrupting target interfaces" plus cryo-EM/X-ray, with reported binder success rates of **9%–88%** — [AlphaProteo (arXiv 2409.08022)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08022)
- **Better interface scoring as partial mitigation:** ipSAE over ipTM for true-vs-false complexes — [ipSAE preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.10.637595v2.full). A secondary claim that "AF3 ipSAE_min gave a 1.4-fold gain in average precision over the ipAE score that RFdiffusion pipelines commonly filter on" appeared only on tool-documentation pages ([proteinbase ipsae skill page](https://proteinbase.com/protein-design-skills/ipsae)) — **low-authority, unverified against a primary source; do not cite as a result.**
- **Mitigations the AF3-adversarial authors themselves propose** (notably *not* cross-model validation): training on "datasets that include destabilized mutants and misfolded proteins," "incorporating explicit biophysical priors," and "developing improved confidence estimation methods" — [Adversarial Sequence Mutations](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.25.708002v1)
- **Deep-learning filters do not fix geometry violations:** "diffusion-based approaches like RFantibody are capable of de novo antibody design but frequently produce severe steric clashes requiring extensive post-hoc filtering" — [GeoFlow-V3 preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.20.682964v1.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- The mitigation stack best supported by the literature, in descending order of evidential strength for the user's specific problem: (1) truncate the target to the epitope so off-epitope poses are impossible; (2) score epitope recall explicitly (HC-recall / interface-residue overlap against the specified epitope) as a *hard* filter, not a soft loss; (3) physics-based re-docking/rescoring; (4) epitope-directed alanine scanning as the experimental readout. Cross-model re-prediction is the weakest-evidenced of the commonly-suggested fixes.
- BindCraft's own documentation of hotspot *masking* is the cleanest citation for why the user's failure happened: with the rest of the target masked out of the loss, nothing penalises a pose elsewhere on the target, and nothing in the confidence metric knows where the intended epitope was.

### Gaps
- **No benchmarked numbers** for "re-prediction with a held-out/independent predictor improves design success by X%." The idea is widely advocated and, as far as I can find, never quantified in a design paper.
- No reported numbers for MM-GBSA or Rosetta ddG rescoring improving *epitope* accuracy (as opposed to binding success) in any de novo binder paper.

## Q6. The "hallucination exploits the predictor" framing, and staying near a prior

### Takeaway
This framing is well established for *monomer* hallucination — maximising predictor confidence is explicitly described as an adversarial attack that yields unexpressible sequences — and the standard fix in practice is exactly the CbAS-style move: constrain the design to a natural prior (MPNN redesign, antibody language-model likelihood, fixed framework). I found no paper that formalises this as CbAS/trust-region theory in the binder-design context.

### Cited Findings
- **The canonical statement:** sequences designed by maximising predictor confidence "are more akin to an adversarial attack on the network and express poorly, if at all"; "optimizing sequences for high structure prediction confidence runs the risk of generating adversarial examples for AlphaFold which are unlikely to be recombinantly expressed in E. coli or adopt the designed tertiary structure" — [ESMFold Hallucinates Native-Like Protein Sequences](https://www.mlsb.io/papers_2023/ESMFold_Hallucinates_Native-Like_Protein_Sequences.pdf)
- **Confirmed empirically for AFDesign:** "Sequences designed using an inverted AlphaFold model, termed AFDesign, contained unnatural sequence profiles and were shown to express poorly, whereas an inverted RosettaFold network was shown to be sensitive to adversarial sequences" — [same](https://www.mlsb.io/papers_2023/ESMFold_Hallucinates_Native-Like_Protein_Sequences.pdf); see also [AlphaDesign, Mol Syst Biol 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s44320-025-00119-z), which notes AF "can produce hallucination — structures with deceptively high confidence but substantial deviations from experimentally determined conformations" and that pLDDT "fails in critical cases involving complex molecular assemblies or atypical conformations"
- **The practical prior-anchoring fix is near-universal.** Germinal explicitly trades off "the Pareto frontier between AF-M structural confidence" and "IgLM sequence likelihood," i.e. it penalises leaving the natural-antibody prior; it further constrains designs to a fixed framework, enforces CDR sequence identity "<50% to PDB/OAS databases," and screens with the Therapeutic Nanobody Profiler and OASis humanness — [Germinal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12485712/)
- **Sequence redesign as de-adversarialisation:** redesigning the hallucinated sequence with an independent inverse-folding/diffusion sequence model "can produce redesigned sequences for each generated raw structure, mitigating some of these issues" — [AlphaDesign, Mol Syst Biol 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s44320-025-00119-z). BindCraft's use of MPNNsol followed by re-prediction is the same pattern in practice — [BindCraft preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full)
- **A dedicated hallucination-assessment framework now exists**, aimed at both prediction and design: CONFIDE, "Hallucination Assessment for Reliable Biomolecular Structure Prediction and Design" — [arXiv 2512.02033](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.02033). **VERIFICATION GAP:** the PDF exceeded the fetch size limit; I have only the title and the search-level description, no numbers. Worth the user fetching directly — it is the most on-topic framework-level paper I found.
- **Hallucination is also documented for AF3 on disordered regions** — [Hallucinations in AlphaFold 3 for Intrinsically Disordered Proteins (arXiv 2510.15939)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15939v2)

### Inferences
- The user's pipeline (gradients through a frozen AF3-like predictor into nanobody CDRs) is structurally identical to AFDesign-style monomer hallucination, one level up: the loss is an *interface* confidence rather than a monomer confidence, and the documented monomer pathology (confidence maximised, physics not satisfied) has an obvious interface analogue (confidence maximised, *epitope* not satisfied). The literature supports this analogy strongly but has not, to my knowledge, stated it.
- The user's permuted-epitope control is formally an **adversarial-robustness probe of the design objective**, in the same family as the AF3 mutation-invariance experiments. Framing it that way connects it to a real, citable body of work rather than to the (absent) off-epitope-design literature.

### Gaps
- I found no CbAS / trust-region / "stay near the data distribution" theory paper applied specifically to structure-predictor-guided binder design. The prior-anchoring is present in practice (IgLM, MPNN, fixed frameworks) but not theorised in these papers.

## Q7. How often do AF-based designed binders bind the intended epitope when structurally characterised?

### Takeaway
**This number does not exist, and cannot currently be computed from the literature, because the denominator is tiny — single-digit numbers of structures per paper, selected post hoc from confirmed binders.** Every structure I found reports agreement with the design model; none reports a wrong epitope. That is near-certainly survivorship: structures get solved for the best binders, and failures are not crystallised.

### Cited Findings
- **BindCraft: 3 structures reported, all at the intended site.** BBF-14/binder4 at 3.1 Å resolution, 1.7 Å backbone Cα RMSD, "confirming both the accuracy of the fold and the designed binding mode"; Der f7/binder2 at 2.2 Å resolution, 1.7 Å backbone RMSD; Der f21/binder10 3.1 Å backbone RMSD (rotamer difference) — out of roughly 150 designs tested across 10 targets — [BindCraft preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1.full)
- **RFdiffusion/RFantibody: 1 VHH cryo-EM structure from 28 SPR-confirmed binders** (and ~9,000 designs/target screened). The influenza VHH was resolved at 3.0 Å, "1.45 Å RMSD to the design model backbone and 0.84 Å RMSD for the CDR3 loop, matching the intended epitope and binding mode"; the characterised design was "one VHH design [that] effectively bound to the fully glycosylated HA trimer (out of the four tested)" — [RFdiffusion antibody preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.14.585103v1.full)
- **RFantibody follow-up structures:** influenza H1 VHH by cryo-EM (1.45 Å backbone / 0.8 Å CDR3), TcdB VHHs before and after affinity maturation, and two scFvs against the TcdB Frizzled epitope by cryo-EM, which "confirmed accurate docking and near-atomic agreement across all six CDRs." A read of this source found it "does not report any designs that bound unintended epitopes or showed incorrect binding modes," and it gives no total count of structurally validated designs — [RosettaCommons, RFantibody structural agreement (Nov 2025)](https://rosettacommons.org/2025/11/24/rfantibody-creates-epitope-specific-antibody-binders-with-atomic-level-agreement/)
- **AlphaProteo:** binder success rates 9%–88%, "confirmed by cryo-EM and X-ray crystallography," with intended-epitope binding confirmed by competitive inhibition and interface-disrupting mutations. No count of structures vs designs characterised is given in the accessible text — [AlphaProteo (arXiv 2409.08022)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08022)
- **Germinal: no experimental structures at all.** Epitope targeting is inferred from AF3 predictions plus alanine scanning: "AF3-predicted structures showed designed binders positioned at intended epitopes" — i.e. the predictor is both the designer and the epitope-verifier — [Germinal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12485712/)
- **An independent reviewer-style critique flags exactly this reporting gap** in BindCraft's affinity/validation data — [Medium: "I Went Through BindCraft's Affinity Data. Here Are the Gaps I Found."](https://medium.com/@enginyapici/i-went-through-bindcrafts-affinity-data-here-are-the-gaps-i-found-7b90e7880b41) and, for Germinal, ["Germinal: Open-Source Nanobody Design With Epitope Evidence and Some Unresolved Gaps"](https://medium.com/@enginyapici/germinal-open-source-nanobody-design-with-epitope-evidence-and-some-unresolved-gaps-2b6dfac3140c) *(both are blog commentary, not peer-reviewed; cite only as "independent commentary has noted", if at all)*

### Inferences
- A defensible statement for the user's write-up: **across the flagship AF-guided binder-design papers, fewer than ~10 designed complexes total have been solved experimentally, all of them drawn from confirmed binders, and all reported as matching the intended epitope. The field therefore has no measured rate of intended-epitope engagement, and no published counterexample — which means the user's observation cannot be checked against literature precedent, and conversely that the absence of published off-epitope failures is not evidence that they are rare.**
- Germinal is the direct methodological precedent for the user's pipeline and it validates epitope targeting *using the same predictor family that generated the designs*, with no experimental structures. That is a circularity the user can legitimately point at.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis or benchmark paper computes "fraction of structurally-characterised de novo designs that bound the intended epitope." I searched for one and did not find it. If the user wants that number, it would have to be assembled by hand from supplements — and the n is small enough (<20 across all papers) that it would not be statistically meaningful.
- Two paywalled sources that most likely contain on-point material and that I could not read: the published [RFantibody Nature 2025 paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09721-5) (the framework-mediated "design failure") and [Nat Commun 2025 epitope-specific antibody workflow](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67361-9) (highest-affinity candidates binding outside the intended epitope). Both should be read in full before publication-level claims of novelty are made.
