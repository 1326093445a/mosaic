# P17 → JN.1: a proof of concept for OpenDDE sequence gradients

**Scope, restated 2026-10-04; reworded 2026-10-06.** This project is a **proof
of concept for gradients**. It is not a campaign to produce a molecule, and no
section below should be read as one. The question is narrow and mechanical:

> OpenDDE separates P17+Alpha (high interface confidence, near-reference pose)
> from P17+JN.1 (neither) on its own metrics. **Does OpenDDE's gradient with
> respect to designed-chain sequence carry enough information to move the
> low-confidence case toward the high-confidence case on those same two
> readouts?**

Every quantity in this document is the predictor's own output. Nothing here is
a claim about measured function, and no experiment below is designed to support
one. P17/JN.1 is the test system because the predictor's separation on it is
large, reproducible and already measured — not because a sequence for JN.1 is
the deliverable.

**Current state in one paragraph (2026-10-06).** The gradient moves both of
OpenDDE's readouts a long way on valid geometry — ipSAE 0.00 → 0.77 against the
Alpha reference level of 0.795, pose 48 → 14 Å — reproducibly across four search
seeds. Three controls then qualify that heavily. A decoy target whose specified
contact site is permuted, and which clears the declared fold gate, reaches **twice**
the confidence of the real target at matched compute (§21.2) — so high
confidence is obtainable without the specified site, though what share of the
real target's gain is nonspecific is not established. The first-order
proposal shows no discrimination within the moves it selects: P(ipSAE improves |
the gradient predicted an improvement) equals the base rate in every seed, while
the gradient consumes 45% of wall-clock (§21.5). And on the standard docking
measure the output is uniformly wrong: **all 165 structures in the latest screen
are CAPRI `incorrect`, best DockQ 0.185 against an `acceptable` threshold of
0.23, mean `fnat` 0.021** (§27.4). The pose gain survives removal
of the pose term (§21.3), and the population maintains diverse parents (§21.6).
**Causal attribution to the gradient remains unresolved in both directions**, and
the random-proposal arm is what would settle it (§21.8).

**Three design questions closed negatively on 2026-10-06 (§27).** A 10-seed
factorial with exploration settings pinned for the first time found the
**registry restraint null on confidence** (+0.072 / −0.020 ipSAE at the two
budgets, inside its own ~0.11 detection threshold), the **population-versus-
independent choice null** (−0.059 ipSAE, −3.0 Å), and **budget 7 worse than 5**
on orientation — flipped poses rose to 26–27 of 33 against 18–23 at budget 5.
⚠️ The registry null is **uninterpretable as it stands**: §26.5 required
per-term gradient norms before any weight comparison, precisely so an absorbed
gradient could be distinguished from an ineffective term, and the screen ran
the comparison without them (§27.5). That measurement — a few gradient calls —
is now the highest-value unrun item in the project, and the large run should not
launch ahead of it.

**And the remaining failure is specifically one of placement (§§22, 25).**
Across every arm the designed chain folds correctly (independently aligned
RMSD 1.4–3.7 Å), the target folds and superimposes (target fit 1.8 Å), and a
large interface forms (34–50 residues per side against 2/1 at WT). §25 then
localized that placement failure precisely, and it is **not** a migration to a
different location: the designs reach the specified site at a mean closest
approach of 2.7 Å, and the residual error decomposes into a ~170° flip that
engages the site with the fixed region rather than the contact face, plus a
12–21° rotation with a 13–19 Å offset in the correctly oriented half. **Right
site, right face, wrong registry.** On the Alpha control, where a solution provably sits five edits
away inside the budget, the search recovered 58% of the confidence gap and only
19% of the pose gap, so this is a search limit rather than a feasible-set
limit. No stage of the pipeline currently selects for *site*: retention ranks
on site-agnostic ipSAE, and pose enters only through a proposal that does not
discriminate. [§26](#26-the-registry-restraint-a-gradient-term-that-can-see-a-flip--2026-10-05) adds the gradient term that
addresses it: a named-pair registry restraint, 3–47× more sensitive than the
existing aggregate contact term to a flip that keeps the designable regions on the specified site,
off unless `--weight-registry` is set and untested against a real model.
§22.3 argues that makes site-specific retention the next change,
ahead of a larger edit budget or a heavier pose weight, and
[§24](#24-should-the-search-policy-be-polished-against-germinal--2026-10-05)
argues it also comes ahead of polishing the search policy — Germinal's
gradient-time site term is *weaker* than ours (weight 0.2 at a 20 Å cutoff
against our 0.5 at 8 Å); what differs is that its selection filter gates on
site booleans and carries no confidence metric at all.

**The sharpest single observation is in §22.5.** On the Alpha control the
search restored **not one** of the five damaged residues, and three of four
seeds ended *further* from the known answer than they started — while three of
four correctly identified position 108 as the one to fix and none chose the
residue that fixes it. Positional signal is present and correct; residue
identity, at this sample size, mostly is not. ⚠️ Read §22.5's own correction
with it: that table is computed from **final winners**, the 5-edit rung's move
trajectory has never been read, and on the 2-edit rung — where the trajectory
*was* read — one run of four proposed the correct residue three times and
accepted it every time. So the asymmetry is a measured *rate*, not an absence
of residue information. [§23](#23-scale-up-readiness-what-the-large-run-needs-and-why-it-targets-jn1--2026-10-05)
sets out what the first large-scale run needs before it is worth committing to,
and records the decision that it targets JN.1 with a small Alpha arm carried
alongside as the interpretability anchor.

## 1. Goal

**The proof-of-concept question.** Run from sequence alone — no MSA, no template
— OpenDDE gives P17+Alpha high interface confidence and a near-reference designed chain
pose, and gives P17+JN.1 neither (§3). The hypothesis under test is that the same
frozen model's gradient, taken with respect to the designed-chain sequence, carries
enough information that following it moves the JN.1 case toward the Alpha case.

Two readouts, both the predictor's own:

1. **Interface confidence** — ipSAE, ipTM, cross-chain gPDE.
2. **Target-aligned designed-chain pose RMSD** against the reference arrangement.

Recovery means **both** move toward the high-confidence regime. The numbers that define
the two regimes, and therefore what counts as a pass, are in §19.1.

What this is **not**: a claim that any recovered sequence has any measured
property, or a sequence anyone intends to make. OpenDDE's opinion is the object
of study here, not a stand-in for an experiment.

**Historical framing, retained for the record.** The project was originally
scoped as *constrained scaffold repair*: the rest of the chain held fixed, only
the 29 designable positions free, edit budget 5–7, AbLang2 sequence naturalness in the loss. Those
constraints follow from the optimization-campaign framing, not from the gradient
question — and §19.3 argues the proof of concept should first be run **without**
them, because they are what make a negative result uninterpretable. Sections 5,
7, 8, 10 and 11 are written under the original framing; read them as history.

## 2. Why the two cases separate

> **Scope note (2026-10-04).** Under the proof-of-concept framing this section
> describes a *property of the test system*, not a problem to solve. What
> matters is that the two targets differ enough at the interface that OpenDDE
> separates them decisively (§3); the per-residue differences below explain why
> the separation is large, and are not themselves a repair target.

Target identity between the two target domains is **82.1%**, but the
differences are concentrated exactly at the interface. All **five** of the
specified-site anchor residues differ:

| Position | Alpha | JN.1 | Change |
|---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| 115 | N | D | charge introduced |
| 117 | L | W | large aromatic substitution |
| 146 | N | K | charge introduced |
| 148 | VE | K | **indel** |
| 150 | F | P | aromatic → proline |

Losing 5/5 anchor residues — including an indel — is the structural argument
that a 5-edit budget may be too tight, and that 7 is defensible. See §7.

### Structural inputs

| File | What it is | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| `P17_Alpha.pdb` | P17 + Alpha target domain | **PDB 8GZ5**, X-ray, 1.70 Å, real B-factors (19.26–122.92) |
| `P17_JN1.pdb` | P17 + JN.1 target domain | **Model.** Alpha complex with the JN.1 target domain swapped in, PyRosetta-relaxed. All B-factors exactly 0.00 |

`P17_JN1.pdb` was verified to be a faithful transplant of the crystallographic
arrangement, not an arbitrary pose:

- the two target domains superimpose at **1.71 Å** over 160 aligned residues
- carrying P17's crystal pose across onto the JN.1 frame lands **2.22 Å** from
  the modeled designed-chain pose

So it is a legitimate target geometry: *where P17 would sit on JN.1 if it engaged
the way it engages Alpha.*

binding from non-binding** on this system rather than confidently folding
everything.

### 3.3 What the numbers say

**The chains fold fine in both cases.** plDDT is 94.2 (Alpha) vs 88.3–89.8 (JN.1)
— high in both. The model is not confused about either protein. Only the
*interface* fails.

**The target realigns in both cases** — 0.66–0.74 Å vs 1.84–1.87 Å. This isolates
the entire gap to **designed-chain placement**, which is the single most important control
in the whole comparison.

**Cross-chain gPDE is the cleanest discriminator** — 0.69–0.70 vs 4.50–12.47, a
~17× separation at its widest, wider and more monotone than ipTM. Worth
considering as a selection metric alongside ipSAE.

**JN.1 seed 0 found literally no interface** — ipSAE exactly 0.0 with **0/0**
interface residues at the cutoff. Not a weak interface; none.

**JN.1 variance is itself informative.** Seed 2 is a partial outlier (ipTM 0.579,
ipSAE 0.234, 32/25 interface residues) while seeds 0–1 are decisive. Alpha by
contrast is reproducible to ±0.001 ipTM. A designed chain that does not bind produces an
*unstable* answer, which argues for multi-seed scoring of every candidate and for
ranking on cross-seed variance, not just the best value (§8.5).

**No clashes anywhere**, so the JN.1 failures are not steric-rejection artifacts.

### 3.4 What the model actually sees

Critically, the model is fed **sequences only**:

```python
features, _ = opendde.binder_features(len(binder_seq), [TargetChain(target_seq, use_msa=False)])
```

No template, no MSA. `P17_JN1.pdb` is used only to (a) extract the two sequences,
(b) compute `reference_distances` for the pose-drift loss term, and (c) score
RMSD after the fact.

This matters for interpretation: the 22–58 Å result is **not** the model failing
to reproduce a pose it was shown. It is a de novo prediction from sequence that
lands nowhere near the built pose, and an ipSAE of ~0.0 is the model reporting no
confidence in any interface at that site.

## 4. Infrastructure

### The OOM problem and its resolution

mosaic's JAX path (via `jopendde`) originally needed **29.79 GiB** and could not
run on the 24 GB RTX 4090, while native torch could. Root cause, after
investigation: **not** tiled/flash attention kernels — torch with `-d fp32` also
OOMs (~26.6 GiB). Same weights, same architecture, comparable fp32 memory. The
real difference is that torch exposes `-d bf16` (`torch.autocast`, i.e.
mixed precision — matmuls in bf16, weights and reductions in fp32) and the JAX
port never implemented it.

Two fixes, both landed:

1. **Autocast-style bf16 attention**, now the **default**, with a
   `JOPENDDE_ATTENTION_DTYPE=fp32` escape hatch — `patches/patch_jopendde_bf16_dtype.py`
2. **`jax.lax.scan` over the role-pair projection** — replaced a `jnp.stack` of
   49 projections (`54 × f32[621,621,384]` = 29.79 GiB) with a scan accumulator.
   Verified **bit-exact** (0.0 max abs diff in float64). Native torch chunks this
   path (`pair_chunk_size: 128`); the JAX port dropped that.

Result: **29.79 GiB → 15.37 GiB peak**, ipTM ~0.885–0.892 vs torch's 0.9006.

Note: run-to-run non-determinism of ~0.007 ipTM was observed across processes
(XLA autotuning); two runs within one process agree exactly.

### N = 621 explained

Structural tokens are a second token stream (~2 subtokens/residue; glycine gets
1), present in **both** repos. Combined with mosaic featurizing the designed chain as
**poly-Trp** — so any residue can be substituted mid-search — this gives 3273
atoms vs torch's 2475.

### What runs where

| Workload | Local RTX 4090 (23.51 GiB) |

| Forward-only structure prediction (JAX) | ✅ 15.37 GiB |
| Native torch, `-d bf16` | ✅ ~10 s/seed |

## 5. The design pipeline

> **Scope note (2026-10-04).** Written under the optimization-campaign framing. The
> edit budget, designable-only mask and AbLang2 naturalness term are campaign
> constraints, not requirements of the gradient question — §19.3 argues for
> running the proof of concept without them first. The loss-term table and the
> MCMC mechanics remain accurate as a description of the implemented prototype.

`examples/p17_hallucination_search.py`

### Designable space

designable ranges from ANARCI (IMGT) on P17's own sequence — designable region 1 26–33, designable region 2 51–58,
designable region 3 97–109 (13 residues; **not** VHH72's 97–114). **29 designable positions.**

### Two stages

1. **Continuous relaxation** — `simplex_APGM`, 200 steps, stepsize 0.05, scale 1.2
2. **Discrete budgeted search** — `edit_budgeted_gradient_mcmc` (default), 100
   steps; or `edit_budgeted_greedy_descent`, 200 steps

Stage 2 is seeded from stage 1's result via `--apgm-seed-mode argmax`, **not**
from WT. (`--apgm-steps 0` skips to a WT start.)

### Loss terms

| Term | Weight | Anchored to | Encodes |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `BinderTargetContact` | 0.5 | 5 specified-site anchor residues | **direction** — make these contacts |
| `BinderPoseDistogramDrift` | — | `P17_JN1.pdb` geometry | **endpoint** — the real binding pose |
| `EditBudget` | 5.0 | WT P17 sequence | **origin** — soft hinge `relu(E − budget)` |
| AbLang2 | 0.10 | — | sequence naturalness |

Pose tolerance is **calibrated at runtime**: `wt_pose + POSE_DRIFT_MARGIN` (3.0 Å),
where `wt_pose` is OpenDDE's own measured drift on the WT sequence.

All three anchors are present — so the search does know where it starts, where it
should end, and which direction to move. This holds regardless of whether stage 2
starts from WT or from the APGM rounding; the endpoint lives in the loss, not the
initialization.

### How MCMC decides what to keep

- **Proposal:** `delta = g − g[current]` (first-order Δloss per (pos, aa)), mask
  non-designable and budget-violating entries to `inf`, `softmax(−delta/proposal_temp)`,
  sample; repeat 1–2×
- **Acceptance:** `log_accept = min(0, (v − v_prop)/temp)` on the **true** loss
- Defaults: `temp=0.02`, `proposal_temp=0.01`. Cold — Δ=0.02 → 37% accept,
  Δ=0.1 → 0.7%
- Output: a **Pareto dict** `{edit_count: (loss, seq)}`

Three known issues:

1. The proposal log-prob is computed then **discarded**
   (`proposal, mutation, _ = sampled`), so there is no q-ratio. This is **not**
   true Metropolis-Hastings. It is an MCMC-inspired stochastic optimizer;
   the acceptance rule does not establish sampling from a Boltzmann target.
2. `proposal_temp=0.01` may concentrate proposals strongly, depending on gradient
   scale. Proposal entropy has not established how deterministic this is in P17.
3. `delta` is a long linear extrapolation across a non-linear loss, and
   concentrated proposals may overcommit to that approximate ranking. `_value_scores` already
   exists and could screen top-k against the true loss.

**Neither `temp` nor `proposal_temp` is passed at the call site or exposed on the
CLI** — both sit at library defaults and have never been swept.

## 6. The core caveat

Every in-loop term is a **geometric proxy** — predicted contacts and a distance
map. None measures whether OpenDDE *believes the complex forms*. That is
ipSAE/ipTM from the full path, deliberately excluded because the hard
`pae < cutoff` mask is non-smooth and useless for gradients.

Consequence: the cheap in-loop loss can keep improving while the real structural
signal does not follow — which has already been observed on this project. The
search optimizes the **predictor's opinion**, so final candidates require scoring
outside the loop that drove them.

`p17_hallucination_mcmc_with_full_opendde_rescoring.py` already computes the real

### Edit budget 5 → 7

**Against:** search space grows ~4,800× — C(29,5)·19⁵ ≈ 2.9e11 → C(29,7)·19⁷ ≈ 1.4e15.

**Recommendation:** run at 7 **in addition to**, not instead of, budget 5.

⚠️ **Corrected.** An earlier revision claimed the Pareto front "subsumes" the
budget-5 result because it returns 5/6/7 in one run. That is wrong: enlarging a

smaller-budget run's result. A budget-7 chain explores a different trajectory, and

P17**. P17's own `results/p17_sweep/combined.csv` is on the cluster.

Corrected earlier claim: "every run spends its full budget, 0/110 overshoot" is
**not** evidence for raising the budget — `num_mutations_from_wt == edit_count`
is structurally guaranteed by the Pareto output format.

### Proposal temperature annealing

Concentrated proposals could make an unfavorable APGM→discrete rounding difficult
to revise. The prototype permits stochastic moves and reversions, so it is
incorrect to say that it has no escape mechanism; practical exploration needs
measurement.

**Distinct from a prior negative result:** `--apgm-seed-mode sample/topk` was
tested on VHH72 and hurt — but that randomizes the *starting point*. Annealing
`proposal_temp` keeps the good seed and allows escape *during* the chain. Not the
same experiment.

**Proposal:** expose exploration controls and compare fixed versus adaptive
proposal entropy. Keep the existing settings as a prototype reference. Earlier
significance testing on another task does not establish a validated P17 baseline.
No numerical temperature schedule is selected by this document.

This compounds with the budget question: a bigger budget with near-greedy
proposals walks *further down the same path* rather than exploring more of it.

### AbLang2 in bf16 — **no**

44.8M params = 171 MiB fp32. bf16 saves 86 MiB, ~0.5% of peak, on a
gradient-supplying term already down-weighted to 0.10. Not worth the numerical
risk.

> **Scope note (2026-10-04).** Off the proof-of-concept critical path (§19.4).

> The proof of concept asks the prior question — whether the gradient moves the
> predictor's own metrics at all — and a single-arm experiment answers it.
> Retained in full: the corrections in §§8.9–8.10 and the gradient-objective
> findings in §11.1 stay relevant whenever search policy becomes live again.

A companion survey — `docs/protein_search_policy_review.md` (Codex, 2026-09-21) —
covers the literature more rigorously than the first pass in this document and
**corrected several claims here**. Corrections are marked ⚠️ below and in §8.9.

### 8.0 Framing: five composable decisions

Following the companion review, "search policy" is not one choice but five:

1. **Representation** — discrete sequences, relaxed probabilities, learned latents, or partially denoised structures
2. **Proposal** — random edits, LM suggestions, gradient-informed edits, recombination, generative sampling
3. **Search memory** — one chain, many chains, a population, a beam, or a learned policy
4. **Evaluation allocation** — which proposals get cheap scoring, expensive prediction, or repeat seeds
5. **Feedback** — whether results update an archive, a population, a surrogate, or a generative policy

The reviewed prototype is: relaxed→discrete / gradient-informed / **single active
sequence** / cheap evaluation / **cheap-score feedback, without full-confidence
selection feedback**. Its best-per-edit-count archive is not an active population.
Search memory and feedback from the expensive evaluator are the main proposed changes.

### 8.1 Already implemented in `src/mosaic/optimizers.py`

Nine optimizers exist. The P17 script uses **two**.

| `edit_budgeted_gradient_mcmc` | discrete, budgeted | `steps=100`, `batch_size=3`, `temp=0.02`, `proposal_temp=0.01`, `max_path_length=2` | ✅ stage 2 |

Dormant machinery that addresses problems flagged elsewhere in this document:

- **`colabdesign_stage` anneals** soft/temp across a stage — the hot→cold schedule the discrete search lacks, already written, on the continuous side
- **`biohub_optimizer` has tail-select** via a separate `tail_loss_function` — a ready-made hook for scoring with the expensive full-path metric without paying per step (§6)

### 8.2 Gradient-informed sampling: scale and detailed balance

[EvoProtGrad / PPDE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09925) is a close algorithmic
relative: discrete gradient proposals, multistep paths, and forward/reverse
proposal probabilities in acceptance. The budgeted mosaic prototype discards its
proposal log probability and does not include the reverse-proposal correction.
It should be described as an MCMC-inspired optimization heuristic, not exact MH.

**Correction:** the earlier claim that `0.01` is "200× colder than the principled
value 2" was not justified. PPDE's factor of two applies to its specified log-target
scale; mosaic's weighted loss has a different scale. If a target distribution is
defined by `exp(-loss/T)`, that choice of `T` also scales its gradients. Proper MH
does not eliminate the choice of target distribution or its temperature.

Measure proposal entropy, accepted moves, and sequence diversity before drawing
conclusions from the raw temperature. Adaptive entropy is a candidate control
(§10), not an established fix. Correct sampling and effective optimization under
limited compute are distinct objectives.

### 8.3 ⭐ Position selection may matter more than the sampler (LaMBO-2)

LaMBO-2 (NeurIPS 2023) is the closest *problem* match: sequence optimization under
locality and developability constraints, with real in vitro validation (99%
expression, 40% binding). Its headline mechanism is for exactly our regime —
"stronger performance with **limited edits** through a novel application of
saliency maps."

The mechanism: take the gradient of the value function w.r.t. embeddings to find
**which positions** most affect the objective, and concentrate edits there. Their
Figure 3 saliency map on an antibody VH concentrates on **CDRH3** — the region
experts pick manually — while still allocating some budget to **framework and
other designable regions, "since these positions may also affect binding."**

Implications for P17: the designable mask defines eligibility, but the current gradient
softmax already assigns nonuniform probabilities to position/substitution pairs.
Explicit position selection would change that policy; it is not the first use of
positional information. The useful question is which allowed designable positions to
spend the small edit budget on, and when to reconsider them. **Fixed positions
remain fixed by the project constraint.** The paper's broader allowed space does
not justify expanding ours. Embedding saliency and reductions of mosaic's
amino-acid gradients are related ideas, not identical implementations.

### 8.4 ⭐ The low-edit regime is its own regime (PEX)

PEX (ICML 2022) maintains a **proximal frontier** — non-dominated solutions trading
predicted fitness against Hamming distance from wild type — and explores near that
frontier rather than optimizing either axis alone. It reports outperforming
AdaLead, genetic algorithms, BO and MCMC, with advantages concentrated under low
edit counts, and argues the low-edit regime needs a *different policy* than
unconstrained search.

Relevance: continuous relaxation uses a soft `EditBudget` hinge, while the
discrete stage also enforces a hard WT-relative cap. PEX-style parent selection
across distance/fitness tradeoffs is a separate idea. The existing best-per-edit-
count dictionary is bookkeeping, not frontier-driven exploration; adopting that
exploration would not require removing the hard cap.

⚠️ PEX is benchmarked on learned fitness models over measured landscapes, not a
structural oracle. A hard mutation cap does not reproduce its exploration policy.

### 8.5 ⭐ Adaptive parent selection (AdaLead)

AdaLead's selection rule is `S = {x | φ(x) ≥ max(y)·(1−κ)}` — a threshold relative
to the best score so far. The behavior this produces is the interesting part, in
the authors' own framing: when the landscape is **flat**, many sequences clear the
filter and the algorithm **explores**; when there is a **prominent peak**, it
climbs rapidly.

That adapts **parent selection** to landscape shape. Proposal temperature controls
a different decision, so the two mechanisms can coexist. The threshold parameter
still needs choosing; relative thresholds also need care when ipSAE values are
zero or tied. This is a source of design ideas, not a parameter-free solution.

Two further findings worth keeping:

- AdaLead "out-competes more complex" BO/RL/CbAS/DbAS methods across FLEXS
  landscapes and is **robust even with an uninformative model** — directly relevant
  given §6, where our in-loop objective is a known-imperfect proxy.
- "Some algorithms are faster to climb in the first couple of batches, but none
  outperform AdaLead in the longer horizon" — a caution against judging policies on
  early progress.
- Recombination contributes only a **small** share of its gain, so most of the
  benefit comes from the adaptive threshold and rollouts, not crossover.

⚠️ Benchmarked on RNA landscapes with ground-truth simulators plus less-accurate
protein simulators — not structure-prediction oracles.

### 8.6 Expensive-evaluation allocation (BO-EVO / BoGA / LaMBO-2)

BO-EVO uses GP uncertainty and an acquisition function to prioritize evolutionary
proposals. BoGA puts evolutionary proposals inside an online surrogate loop and is
closer to our setting because it targets *computational* structure objectives
rather than requiring an assay per round.

⚠️ **Corrected.** This document previously reported "+11% over MCMC, +21% over pure
EA." Accurately: **11% and 21% increases in round-five success ratio against its
MCMC and AdaLead baselines respectively.** The second baseline is AdaLead, not
generic evolution, and this is one benchmark result — not an expected improvement
over mosaic's particular gradient-guided constrained implementation.

Relevance is real regardless: a surrogate absorbs expensive true-objective calls,
which is our stated bottleneck (§6). But a surrogate trained on OpenDDE confidence
learns *computational confidence*, not any measured property — it does not escape §6, it only
makes the proxy cheaper to query.

### 8.10 On ranking by cross-seed variance

§3.3 observes empirically that JN.1 gives 0.22/0.30/0.58 across seeds while Alpha
reproduces to ±0.001 — the non-designed chain's signature is an *unstable* answer. That
observation stands on our own data.

What does **not** stand is citing the risk paper as support for it. That paper
examines risk across optimization campaigns and reports no added benefit from
risk-aware model ranking in its tested setting, because optimization stochasticity
obscures it. Multi-seed scoring remains worth doing; it is a hypothesis from our
own measurements, not a literature-backed selection rule.

### 8.11 Revised recommendation

The latest shortlist is **(1) gradient-guided population search with local
competition, (2) independent gradient-guided searches with adaptive exploration,
(3) gradient-guided sampling of complete WT-relative edit combinations**. See
§10 for their distinctions, references, and comparison plan.

Germinal informs the shared gradient-combination mechanism. PEX informs distance
management. BO is a possible later evaluation-allocation layer. None is a proven
winner for this project, and none requires replacing mosaic in the first phase.

### 8.12 Evidence to retain in any comparison

From the companion review — what must be held constant or recorded for a policy
comparison to mean anything:

- identical allowed sequence space and evaluation criteria across policies
- total compute including proposals, backward passes, full predictions, repeat
  seeds and surrogate fitting
- unique *feasible* candidates and their diversity, not just best cheap loss
- cases where cheap and expensive scores **disagree**, recorded explicitly
- structural sampling variation, surrogate uncertainty and biological uncertainty
  kept separate
- repeated search runs, with selection distinguished from later evaluation to
  limit selection bias

## 10. Search-policy handoff for Claude — 2026-09-22

> **Scope note (2026-10-04).** Off the proof-of-concept critical path (§19.4).
> The population-versus-independent comparison varies *search memory*, a
> second-order question stacked on an untested first-order one. §19.5 sequences
> it after the gradient itself is shown to work. The differentiable-proposal /
> non-differentiable-retention split described in §10.1 does remain the
> architecture the proof of concept runs on.

This section captures the latest user discussion and supersedes earlier priority
rankings. It is a research recommendation, **not an implemented or validated
algorithm**. Existing project measurements above are retained as reported; this
documentation update did not rerun them.

### 10.1 Agreed scope and the central decision

- Retain **mosaic + frozen OpenDDE + frozen AbLang2**, using gradients to guide
  sequence proposals. Optimizing sequence inputs does not require training these
  model weights.
- Keep the framework and permitted designable regions fixed. Diversity means different
  combinations of positions and amino acids **inside those designable regions**, while retaining
  the specified site.
- The immediate computational target is improved **ipSAE**. Full-path confidence
  evaluation is intended to participate in search selection. The user is developing
  a separate property model; this shortlist does not depend on its completion.
- The current MCMC path is an **unfinished prototype**. Neither its name, CLI
  description, nor results from another antibody task establish a P17 baseline.
- Begin without a new surrogate, diffusion model, or learned search policy.
  Population/archival updates and empirical sampling statistics can use evaluations
  without fitting an additional neural model.

The key question is **which promising alternatives remain available as parents,
and how the search revises an early choice of edited positions**. Wiring a score
into a log is insufficient: its evaluations must change retention or later search.
Confidence selection can be nondifferentiable while proposal gradients come from
the existing differentiable objective. That split is our proposed adaptation;
the gradient is not being represented as a gradient of ipSAE itself.

### 10.2 Policies worth comparing

| Priority | Policy | Search memory and decision | Main hypothesis | Paper inspiration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Gradient-guided population with local competition** | Multiple active parents; offspring compete partly with similar candidates, with global elites retained | Alternative designable-position edit combinations survive long enough to improve instead of all descendants following one early winner | AdaLead, ME-GIDE; PEX for distance-aware parent selection |
| 2 | **Independent gradient-guided searches with adaptive exploration** | Several separate trajectories; each can revise edits and take occasional worse moves; no parent exchange | Multiple trajectories and adequate exploration may deliver most of the benefit without population machinery | EvoProtGrad/PPDE, ME-GIDE's entropy control |
| 3 | **Gradient-guided complete edit-combination sampling relative to WT** | Sample feasible complete combinations; retain evaluated combinations and update sampling statistics | Reconsidering the whole edit allocation can find combinations that are unattractive along a stepwise path | BADASS's fixed-reference sampling, LaMBO-2's position-selection emphasis |

These are **proposed hybrids**, not claims to reproduce those papers. Their shared
components should be kept comparable so that the first experiment tests search
memory rather than several unrelated changes at once.

**Policy 1 — preferred organizing direction.** Use the same mosaic gradient
guidance to propose offspring from several retained parents. Full confidence
evaluations inform the active population. A candidate should not have to beat the
single global best to remain useful: competition among similar designable region sequences can
preserve different edit combinations. Start with simple designable-region sequence similarity;
a high-dimensional descriptor grid or learned embedding is not a prerequisite.
This resembles quality-diversity search but is not exact ME-GIDE. A globally
ranked top-k beam alone can still converge to many near-duplicates.

**Policy 2 — essential simple reference.** Run separate stochastic searches with
the same feasible space, proposal information, and access to confidence feedback.
Adjust exploration based on observed proposal concentration or stagnation rather
than assuming a raw temperature has the same meaning across gradient scales.
Allow revisions to the current combination. Comparing this with policy 1 tests
whether population interaction adds value beyond independent attempts. Call it
an optimization heuristic unless a specific target distribution and correct
proposal correction are implemented.

**Policy 3 — exploratory follow-up.** Propose complete feasible edit sets relative
to WT, using gradient information to bias both position choice and substitutions.
Past evaluations can update sampling preferences without a learned surrogate.
Keep actual evaluated combinations: averages associated with individual edits
are not independent causal effects, because substitutions can interact. This
policy changes the proposal family as well as search memory, so test it after the
simpler comparison if searches repeatedly settle on the same position sets.

### 10.3 Shared requirement: an edit budget is not a step budget

The budget counts differences from WT in the **current sequence**, not how many
search operations have occurred. Spending the full budget must not freeze the
chosen positions. All policies should be able to consider:

- Reverting an edited residue to WT.
- Changing the amino acid at an already edited position.
- Exchanging one edited position for another while preserving the final cap.

The existing prototype permits reversions and can perform a revert-then-add path;
the proposed improvement is efficient reconsideration, not claiming these moves
are impossible today. Explicit exchange proposals are an option to test. Hard
designable region/framework and WT-distance constraints still apply to every retained candidate.
No framework expansion or numerical hyperparameter choice was agreed.

### 10.4 What the follow-up evolutionary papers actually establish

**ME-GIDE** combines MAP-Elites with proposals informed by fitness and descriptor
gradients; it adjusts proposal temperature toward a chosen entropy. Its protein
experiment uses a language-model likelihood objective and embedding descriptors,
not measured fitness or ipSAE. Borrowing population diversity and entropy control
while selecting with nondifferentiable confidence is an adaptation. Its particular
descriptor construction is not required for our initial test.
[Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05138)

**BADASS** separates the sequence optimizer from the trained Seq2Fitness scorer.
The optimizer samples around a fixed reference, updates mutation statistics from
evaluated sequences, and alternates heating and cooling. Its original proposals
do not require gradients. Gradient seeding here is our extension. Published large
batches and single-mutant initialization were evaluated with much cheaper scorers;
they do not establish efficiency for full complex prediction.
[Paper](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013119)

**AlphaDesign** is precedent for evolutionary optimization of structure-predictor
objectives without differentiating the reward through the full search. It also
includes downstream inverse-folding redesign and validation; outcomes of the
complete pipeline cannot be attributed solely to its evolutionary policy. Its de
novo design setting differs from strict few-edit repair.
[Paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s44320-025-00119-z)

**Quality-Diversity for One-Shot Biological Sequence Design** supports studying
archives and conservative scoring; our review of it was limited by access.
**Evolution-informed multiproperty design** adds a related example of balancing
distance and batch diversity. Both remain background references, not additional
first-phase policies. Their links and review limits are in §8.13.

### 10.5 Germinal supplies a shared gradient mechanism

Germinal jointly guides antibody designable region design with a structure predictor and an
sequence language model, with experimental validation. Its relevance here is
**how competing guidance signals are combined**, not how a population is retained.
The paper's AF-M/IgLM setup differs from OpenDDE/AbLang2 and from a strict edit cap.
[Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03187-0)

The repository inspected in this discussion supports gradient norm balancing and
weighted, PCGrad, or MGDA merging. Its discrete semigreedy refinement is a separate
candidate-generation/evaluation step, not the same algorithm as EvoProtGrad.
For reproducibility, the inspected revision was
`1e1c1a5b79884ae45abae030c9df90d9423a990a`:
[design.py](https://github.com/SantiagoMille/germinal/blob/1e1c1a5b79884ae45abae030c9df90d9423a990a/colabdesign/colabdesign/af/design.py).

Mosaic's reviewed P17 composition is a weighted scalar sum with gradient clipping.
Clipping does not resolve opposing gradient directions. A useful shared ablation
is the current sum versus relative norm balancing, then conflict-aware merging if
needed. Measure individual gradient norms and alignment before attributing a
failure to one model overpowering the other. Normalization changes proposal scale,
which is another reason to record entropy rather than transplant temperatures.
Hold this mechanism fixed when first comparing policies 1 and 2.

➕ **Revisited 2026-10-05 (§24.5).** This remains the one genuine search-policy
item for which Germinal is the right reference, and it becomes *more* important
after a site term and an off-site repulsion term are added, since those are in
direct opposition by construction. The prerequisite this section asked for —
measuring individual gradient norms and alignment before attributing failure to

`examples/p17_pose_diagnostics.py` already computes `cdr_gradient_norms` and

### 10.6 Role of the other papers and what is deferred

- **LaMBO-2 is a diffusion-based Bayesian optimization framework**, not merely a
  diffusion model. Borrowing its emphasis on edit-position choice does not require
  adopting its denoiser or training its property models. In our smaller allowed
  space, its published saliency advantage remains a hypothesis to test.
- **PEX** motivates keeping useful alternatives across WT distances. It can
  inform population parent selection without relaxing the hard budget.
- **AdaLead** motivates adaptive parent retention and rollouts. Its parent
  threshold and proposal entropy address separate decisions; recombination is
  optional initially.
- **BO-EVO and BoGA** become relevant if expensive-evaluation allocation proves
  limiting and accumulated scores support a useful surrogate. BO can coexist
  with gradient-guided evolution; it is not an obligatory replacement for MCMC.
- **MosPro** is another gradient-balancing reference. **GGS** changes a learned
  landscape, adding work beyond the initial frozen-model comparison.
- **BindCraft, EasyNano, Fast SeqProp** inform continuous/discrete refinement and
  proxy limitations. They do not settle the population question.
- **Proteina-Complexa's Feynman–Kac steering** was deprioritized by the user because
  its generative trajectory setting is a poor direct fit for this mosaic plan.
- **LaMBO-1, CbAS, GFlowNets, EVOLVEpro, LM-guided antibody evolution,
  RosettaSearch, and the risk paper** remain in the reference inventory, with
  their training requirements and transfer limits in the companion survey.

All paper links are collected in §8.13; this grouping describes their roles rather
than proposing another expanded experiment list.

### 10.7 First comparison and interpretation

First compare **policy 1 versus policy 2** under matched total compute and the
same frozen models, allowed positions, edit cap, initialization scheme, proposal
guidance, and confidence-evaluation protocol. Account for backward passes, cheap
forward calls, full predictions, and repeated structural seeds. Equal iteration
counts alone are insufficient.

Record best and retained-population ipSAE, unique feasible sequences, diversity
of edited positions and substitutions, proposal entropy, and disagreement between
the cheap objective and full confidence. Preserve the context of each evaluation,
including sequence, parent, model configuration, and random seed. Later evaluation
of selected candidates should be separated from the predictions used to select
them. Structural-seed variance is distinct from search-run variance.

If the population wins, test whether local competition contributes beyond global
top-k retention. If both policies repeatedly reuse the same edit sets, test policy
3. Gradient-merging and explicit position-selection ablations can then identify
which shared components matter. No paper establishes in advance that these
policies improve P17 ipSAE; that remains the purpose of the comparison.

### 11.2 On gradient fidelity: the literature treats this as unsolved everywhere, not just here

The user's stated uncertainty — how much does the in-loop gradient actually
reflect what a discrete mutation does, and does moving along it improve the
real objective — breaks into three separable claims, each with literature
behind it, none of them resolved even by the closest published analogs:

1. **Soft-input gradient → hard-mutation effect.** The gradient is evaluated
   at a one-hot/softmax input; the network was trained on discrete tokens.
   Standard soft/hard mismatch (Fast SeqProp's whole premise).
2. **First-order linearity vs. a nonlinear loss.** `delta[pos,aa]` is a
   Taylor-1 estimate of a large discrete jump. PPDE's own paper: "accumulation
   of errors from first-order Taylor approximations along a long path can lead
   to worse sampler efficiency despite better exploration" — directly relevant
   given `max_path_length=2`. The epistasis literature adds a structural
   reason this may bite harder on P17 specifically: first-order/pairwise
   approximations hold when a landscape is low-order-dominated, and break down
   under "cooperative conformational transitions" — P17→JN.1 needs 5
   coordinated anchor residue changes (including an indel) to reform one interface,
   closer to the failure case than the case where linear proposals work well.
3. **Proxy-gradient → real-objective direction.** The gradient in play is of
   the cheap distogram/contact loss, not ipSAE, and per §6 the proxy can keep
   improving while ipSAE doesn't follow. No per-step correlation between the
   two has been measured on P17.

⚠️ **Correction (Codex).** The epistasis point in item 2 was overstated: five
altered target anchor residues motivate concern about interface disruption, but they
don't themselves demonstrate that five coordinated antibody-side mutations
are required, or that local/first-order gradients fail here specifically.
That remains a hypothesis worth testing, not a structural argument the
anchor residue count alone establishes.

None of this is unique to mosaic. Germinal's own paper "shows computational
effects of some design modifications, but none of these ablations are
connected to experimental outcomes." BindEnergyCraft's gradient-density fix
(§11.1) is validated as denser, not as more correlated with binding. The
honest framing, matching §10.1's existing split, is mitigation — dense-gradient
objectives, non-differentiable retention gates, cross-model validation — not
proof that the proposal gradient is locally correct. Nobody in this literature
has that proof for this class of method.

### 12.1 Corrections that affect implementation

- `pTMEnergy` needs PAE logits, which the distogram-only path does not produce.
  Adding its gradient is a separate feasibility/ablation task, not a prerequisite
  for comparing retention policies. Its paper also reports retrospective

- The six control rows represent **two biological cases with three prediction
  seeds each**. They support sanity checks and seed variability analysis, not
  selecting a generally superior metric for redesigned antibodies.
- At the pinned BindCraft2 revision, the second `i_pDAE` direction transposes the
  extracted designed-chain-to-target PAE block instead of extracting the reverse PAE

  antibody edits or high-order epistasis. Both remain hypotheses.
- Stale backward-memory measurements cannot prove that confidence gradients are

### 12.2 Implemented behavior

![P17 model and search flow: frozen OpenDDE and AbLang2 guide constrained proposals; full OpenDDE confidence drives independent or population retention.](figures/p17_optimization_flow.svg)

[PNG](figures/p17_optimization_flow.png) · [PDF](figures/p17_optimization_flow.pdf) ·
[Editable SVG](figures/p17_optimization_flow.svg) ·
[Figure source](figures/draw_p17_optimization_flow.py)

[`src/mosaic/search.py`](../src/mosaic/search.py) separates a cheap loss/gradient
callback from a confidence-score callback. The cheap loss is minimized; confidence
is maximized. Both callbacks use fixed model seeds so complete-sequence caching
is meaningful within a run. Search RNG is separate from model RNG.

- Both policies start with the same number of WT slots and use the same proposal
  distribution. Single substitutions include reversions and changing an existing
  edit; at the cap, explicit revert-and-add exchanges reconsider edited positions.
  Every scored candidate obeys the fixed-position mask and WT Hamming cap.
- Proposal temperature is calibrated from the current gradient deltas to a
  target normalized entropy, `H(p) / log(number of feasible moves)`. Tied minima
  can make the requested entropy unattainable; achieved entropy is logged.
  This is an adaptation, not an implementation of exact ME-GIDE or MH sampling.
- Independent slots compare offspring only against their own current parent.
  Population offspring compete with the nearest active sequence by Hamming
  distance; ties challenge the lower-scoring incumbent. Initial duplicate slots
  admit new alternatives regardless of score, while preserving one incumbent
  copy. Once filled, the highest-scoring active candidate cannot be replaced by
  a worse candidate. Duplicate active sequences are not reinserted.
- Both policies otherwise accept confidence improvements and ties, and can
  accept worsening confidence according to the shared acceptance temperature.
  Best evaluated candidates remain archived even if they are not active parents.
- Unique confidence calls, unique parent-gradient calls, and proposal attempts
  have separate ceilings. Duplicate evaluations are cached. Counts and timings
  are recorded; equal budget ceilings are **not a claim of matched GPU time**.

[`examples/p17_confidence_search.py`](../examples/p17_confidence_search.py) wires
this to the existing `build_composite_losses` distogram + AbLang2 configuration,
including its pose guidance and edit penalty. It does not add `pTMEnergy`, change
gradient merging, or train weights. Every new feasible proposal receives full
OpenDDE **forward-only** scoring before retention; expensive-evaluation screening
or allocation is deferred.

The provisional selection score is the arithmetic mean, across a fixed list of
structural seeds, of each seed's minimum directional ipSAE. It reuses
`ipsae_d0res_asym_max` from the native control analysis, operating on mean PAE
with the recorded cutoff. Mosaic's existing `IPSAE_min` loss uses a different
logit-expectation calculation and is deliberately not substituted. Distance
cutoff controls interface-count diagnostics only. ipTM is logged, not optimized
as the retention score. Selection seeds are not held-out validation seeds.

### 12.3 Outputs and reproducibility

Each run requires a new output directory and writes:

- `config.json`: policy/model seeds, score definition and cutoffs, sequences,
  masks, model settings, source hashes, package versions, hardware and setup cost.
- `events.jsonl`: flushed after each event; evaluated sequences and per-seed
  metrics, ancestry, competitors, acceptance decisions, entropy, and counters.
- `candidates.csv`: all uniquely scored candidates, final active/best flags, and
  cheap losses when their gradients were evaluated. Blank cheap loss means the
  candidate was scored but never needed as a gradient parent.
- `summary.json`: final population IDs, best candidate, stopping reason, call
  counts and timings, including the number of full structural predictions.

Compilation is included in measured call time. The one WT pose-calibration
forward is recorded separately as setup. Calls are serial; no batching-speedup
claim is made. Logs support inspection after interruption, but automatic resume
and held-out rescoring are not implemented in this first version. Raw PAE tensors
are not persisted. Fixed seeds do not eliminate cross-process GPU-kernel variation.

## 13. Claude: memory instrumentation added — 2026-09-22

§12.4 named "inspect memory" as one of three things to check before a larger
pilot, but nothing in `config.json`/`events.jsonl`/`summary.json` actually
recorded GPU memory — only call timing. An OOM would just crash the run with
no margin information, and a successful run gave no sense of how close to the
ceiling it came. This closes that specific gap.

### 13.1 What was added

- `device_memory_stats()` in `examples/p17_confidence_search.py` — queries
  `jax.Device.memory_stats()` per device (keyed by `str(device)`, so a
  multi-GPU run stays disambiguated), explicitly marking
  `{"supported": False, "note": ...}` on any backend/exception where it's
  unavailable rather than silently omitting it. Verified against a real CUDA
  device in this environment (`jax==0.10.0`, `CudaDevice(id=0)`) — confirmed
  working, not assumed from the API docs.
- `gradient_fn` and `predict_sequence` (the two GPU-touching closures in
  `main()`) now snapshot memory before and after every call. Outputs are
  converted to numpy before the "after" snapshot to force synchronization, so
  it reflects real post-call state rather than an in-flight async dispatch.
  Snapshots write to a new `memory.jsonl` (`before`/`after`/`call_index`, plus
  `seed` for confidence calls), inside a `try/finally` so exceptions from the
  protected model call can be logged before propagating. Subsequent review found
  that device-input creation remains outside that block: its failures are not
  logged. This is still an outstanding code fix as of 2026-09-23.
- `config.json` gains `memory_at_setup` (right after model loading, before any
  search call); `summary.json` gains `memory_at_completion`.
- `src/mosaic/search.py` was **not touched**. Memory instrumentation lives
  entirely in the example script, where JAX/GPU specifics already live —
  keeping the shared harness backend-agnostic and weight-free-testable, the
  property that let it get 25 CPU-only tests in the first place.

### 13.2 The caveat that matters for reading this data

`peak_bytes_in_use` is a **running allocator maximum**, not a per-call peak.
A new input shape does not reset it: earlier setup, compilation or model calls
can dominate even the first call of that shape. An increased peak identifies a
new high-water mark during the measured interval; an unchanged peak does not
identify that call's maximum. `bytes_in_use` records live allocator usage at each
snapshot, not temporary allocations between snapshots.

The monotonicity test checks that the counter does not decrease across its one
matmul interval; it does not prove per-call attribution or crash logging. The
original function docstring's first-shape claim still needs the matching code
edit. These clarifications were added during review on 2026-09-23.

### 17.2 Behavior of the completed pilot and unchanged pose calculation

`BinderPoseRMSD` fits the predicted target CA atoms to the reference target,
applies that transform to the predicted designed chain, and measures designed chain CA RMSD.
Independently aligning the designed chain measures internal shape and would remove the
rigid-body displacement that the pose metric needs to retain. Report both,
alongside target-fit RMSD; a poorly fitting target makes the pose interpretation
uncertain even when the numerical calculation completes.

The full proposal objective includes coordinate pose RMSD, contact guidance,
ipTM, bidirectional interface PAE, pTMEnergy, AbLang2 and the edit penalty. The
coordinate pathway is differentiable. The separately evaluated ipSAE score
controlled retention and therefore future parents in the completed pilot; RMSD did not.
Consequently, the search can retain a higher-confidence candidate with worse
reference-pose agreement.

Clipping and entropy normalization matter. `ClippedGradient` uses a custom
backward rule, and the proposal distribution adapts its temperature to a target
entropy. Multiplying a loss coefficient can be absorbed by clipping or by a
change in temperature. A logged nonzero RMSD scalar therefore does not prove
useful sequence guidance, and a nonzero sequence gradient does not prove an
improving discrete mutation. The hinge has no upper bound on its loss value.
Neither the hinge nor clipping establishes an optimal balance between terms.

The runner constructs the target from sequence without a structural
template. This may contribute to reference disagreement, but it is an untested
explanation. A template is conditioning, not a guarantee of fixed coordinates.
Check residue mapping, CA extraction, units and alignment conventions before
attributing the roughly 16 Å target/internal discrepancies to the model.

### 17.3 Test 1: pose measurement and guidance influence

Keep this a diagnostic experiment, before another optimization campaign:

1. **Verify correspondence.** Check chain identity, sequence order, residue
   numbering/insertion codes, missing CA atoms and coordinate units against
   `P17_JN1.pdb`. Fail explicitly on an ambiguous mapping or degenerate target
   alignment. Any residue mask must be recorded and shared between loss and
   reporting; do not silently omit missing residues.
2. **Verify geometry.** Identity and a rigid transform of the entire complex
   must produce approximately zero pose error. Moving only the designed chain must
   preserve its independently aligned internal RMSD while changing pose RMSD.
   Cross-check the differentiable loss against the independent reporting
   calculation on identical coordinates and residue correspondence.
3. **Measure actual guidance influence.** For the same sequence, model seed,
   settings and random-key schedule, compare the composite gradient with pose
   enabled against pose weight zero. Keep the loss structure/key schedule fixed.
   Report designable-restricted gradient norms, gradient difference/direction, mutation
   deltas, and change in the normalized feasible-proposal probabilities. The
   difference is a change in the composite clipped gradient, not necessarily
   the isolated raw RMSD gradient.
4. **Connect gradients to discrete predictions.** Check a small, prespecified
   set of feasible proposed edits with full forward predictions under matched
   structural seeds. Record predicted first-order changes and observed pose,
   target-fit and confidence changes. A diagnostic effect on proposals is not
   itself evidence of improved predicted poses.
5. **Record variability and artifacts.** Save reference/proposal/forward
   provenance, model and search seeds, CIF/PDB/NPZ, per-term diagnostics, timing
   and memory. Include a repeated baseline because the completed run was not
   exactly repeatable. Distinguish structures used for proposal gradients from
   the separate retention predictions.

The stage must produce a machine-readable pass/fail report with reasons.
Finite tensors and exit code 0 are necessary but insufficient. The report must
cover correspondence/geometry checks, meaningful proposal influence relative
to repeat variability, and whether the reference alignment is interpretable.
Numerical tolerances and any target-fit threshold must be specified before the
comparison and recorded as provisional; no scientifically validated cutoff has
been established here. If any prerequisite is unresolved, stop before Test 2
and retain the diagnostic evidence. In particular, unexplained target-fit RMSD
near 16 Å must not be waved through merely because gradients are finite.

### 17.6 Implemented workflow and launch commands

New entry point: `examples/run_p17_pose_experiment.sh`, backed by

```bash

```

The dry-run creates no output directories, applies no patches, and loads no
models. The real launch applies the existing three dependency patches once,
records the plan and exact worker commands, then executes sequential stages:

| Stage | Default work | Barrier |
|---|---|---|
| `diagnostic/` | Two workers with proposal-model seeds 0 and 1; each evaluates pose on, on-repeat, off; forward WT and the top on/off proposed edits under structural seeds 0/1, plus WT repeats | Both workers must exit 0 and both reports must pass every required check |
| `search/` | A neither, B guidance, C retention, D both; search seeds 0/1 per arm, eight workers total | Every search worker must succeed |
| `heldout/` | WT plus distinct archived winners; structural seeds 101/102/103, sharded across allocated GPUs | Every shard must finish and merged CIF/PDB/NPZ paths must exist |

Two GPUs are used during diagnostics; the eight search workers use all eight
allocated GPUs. Fewer GPUs run bounded batches. Each worker must fit on one GPU;
memory is not pooled. Workers explicitly select the CUDA backend. The diagnostic
has six full gradient calls total and 12–16 forward calls total, depending on
whether the top on/off edit is identical. Search defaults remain width 4,
designable-only, five edits, proposal entropy 0.6 and confidence temperature 0.02;
ceilings are 32 unique scored sequences, 32 unique parent gradients and 320
proposals per worker. Selection uses structural seeds 0/1, so 32 score calls
mean 64 standalone full predictions. Search gradients use proposal-model seed 0.
Held-out predictions are not fed back into selection.

**Diagnostic checks and provisional thresholds.** Reference chain/residue/CA
correspondence is explicit, including insertion codes, sequence order and all
CA indices. Missing/ambiguous CA atoms, coincident/collinear alignment points,
or adjacent CA spacing outside 2.5–4.5 Å fail the audit. Spacing is a unit/gap
sanity check, not proof of physical validity. The reference PDB, CA arrays and
designable mask are saved. Identity, global-rigid and designed-chain-only translation controls
cross-check the independent NumPy reporter against the actual JAX loss, with
1e-3 Å tolerance; every diagnostic forward prediction receives the same
cross-check on identical coordinates.

The full composite loss keeps its pose term present at weight zero, preserving
random-key scheduling and scalar auxiliaries. The diagnostic measures designable region
norms, direction, gradient differences, feasible mutation deltas and normalized
proposal probabilities. The total-variation distance between on/off proposals
must exceed both 1e-4 and three times the on/on-repeat variation. Paired pose
scalars must agree within max(1e-3 Å, three times the repeat difference). Target
fit must be at most **3 Å** in all diagnostic forward and gradient evaluations.
These are configurable, provisional checks, recorded before execution; they are
not validated biological cutoffs. The previously observed roughly 16 Å target
fit would stop automatic continuation. A gate pass establishes interpretable
measurement and detectable proposal influence, not improvement or fitness.

The probe rule is fixed before seeing outcomes: evaluate WT and the highest
probability feasible edit under each objective, deduplicated. Predicted
first-order **composite-loss** deltas are saved alongside observed pose,
target-fit and confidence changes; they are not labeled isolated RMSD-gradient
predictions. Repeats are within each worker process; they do not characterize
fresh-process or cross-GPU variability exposed by the earlier validation run.
Gradient-path coordinates are not exported: saved CIF/PDB/NPZ files are the
separate forward predictions, with proposal scalar diagnostics recorded in logs.

**Retention semantics.** `--weight-pose` defaults to 1.0; the ablation toggles
1.0 versus 0.0 without changing other losses. `--retention-pose-margin` is absent
by default, preserving confidence-only retention. Arms C/D set it to 3 Å:
ceiling = worst selection-seed WT pose RMSD + margin. WT is evaluated once as
part of the normal budget and is feasible by construction. The ceiling is
fixed thereafter for that run. Violation is max(0, worst-seed candidate pose
RMSD − ceiling). Lower violation wins before raw mean ipSAE. Competition ties,
duplicate filling, elite protection, archived winner and CSV ranks obey this
ordering; stochastic acceptance cannot increase an incumbent's violation.
WT-relative feasibility means baseline preservation, not a correct reference pose.

Each worker calibrates from its own WT predictions, so predictor variability
can produce different ceilings across arms; these are saved explicitly. For
comparison only, `tables/search_runs.csv` applies one common reporting ceiling:
median of all search-run worst-seed WT RMSDs + margin, and reports the evaluated
feasible fraction (including WT), raw best score and actual compute counts.
`tables/heldout_candidates.csv` reports mean/worst confidence and all three
RMSDs, using the held-out WT worst-seed RMSD + margin as a common reporting
ceiling for every winner. This reporting does not alter the archived winners.
Source-run mappings preserve shared winners rather than counting them as
independent predictions.

Outputs contain `plan.json`, `experiment.json`, `status.tsv`, `commands.sh`,
`logs/`, `tables/`, `diagnostic/`, `search/` and `heldout/` as reached. Each
worker retains organized CSVs, logs, CIF/PDB and confidence arrays. Diagnostics
add `diagnostic.json`, `tables/diagnostic_proposals.csv`, gradient NPZs, reference
audit and a separate `repeat/` output tree. A failed worker or report stops later
stages; completed evidence remains on disk. Fresh output directories are
required; this launcher does not resume failed runs.

### 18.1 Clarify what the geometric requirement means

The intended behavior is to permit a range of nearby arrangements, rather than
continually reward exact reproduction of every reference coordinate. Before
choosing a different penalty, distinguish four questions:

- **Reference frame:** should a common rigid transformation of the whole scene
  leave the measurement unchanged?
- **Placement:** which relative translations and rotations count as acceptable?
- **Shape:** should internal deformation contribute to the same measurement?
- **Tolerance:** does the bound concern an average displacement, every point,
  or separate position and orientation errors?

Coordinate RMSD combines placement and internal shape differences. A bound on
RMSD is an average-error condition, not a bound on each point's displacement.
Adding a tolerance or smoothing the penalty does not change what the underlying
metric measures. These are metric-definition questions, distinct from whether
automatic differentiation computes the intended derivative.

### 18.2 Papers and what they establish

1. **Berenson, Srinivasa and Kuffner (2011), “Task Space Regions: A framework
   for pose-constrained manipulation planning,” IJRR.**
   [Author-hosted paper](https://personalrobotics.cs.washington.edu/publications/berenson2011task.pdf).
   Sections 4.1–4.2 describe acceptable rigid poses through bounds on translation
   and orientation in a reference frame. Distance to the region is zero inside
   the allowed bounds. The framework combines this representation with sampling
   and constraint-satisfaction strategies.

   **Interpretation for the geometry discussion:** an acceptable set of poses is
   a useful way to express freedom of movement. The paper's rigid-pose regions
   are not equivalent to an RMSD threshold on a deformable point cloud, and its
   planning guarantees do not transfer automatically to a learned model.

2. **Schulman et al. (2013), “Finding Locally Optimal, Collision-Free
   Trajectories with Sequential Convex Optimization,” RSS (TrajOpt).**
   [Proceedings paper](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss09/p31.pdf).
   The method uses hinge penalties for collision constraints, sequential convex
   approximations, and an outer procedure that increases penalty coefficients
   when necessary. Constraint handling is part of the optimization procedure,
   not just the choice of a scalar loss.

   **Interpretation:** a hinge is an established baseline, not evidence of an
   inherently inadequate formulation. A fixed weighted penalty alone does not
   establish final constraint satisfaction. This paper does not show that a
   different penalty would fix the historical model failures.

3. **Barron (2019), “A General and Adaptive Robust Loss Function,” CVPR.**
   [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.03077).
   Section 1 and Figure 1 relate several losses through a common family and
   show how their derivatives respond to residual magnitude. Depending on the
   loss, influence can grow, approach a constant, or decrease for large errors.
   The paper includes registration and other computer-vision applications.

   **Interpretation:** smoothness and useful corrective influence are separate
   properties. Robust losses can intentionally suppress large errors as outliers;
   that behavior can conflict with a geometric requirement in which large
   displacement is meaningful. A smoother formula is not sufficient evidence
   that its guidance is preferable.

4. **Levinson et al. (2020), “An Analysis of SVD for Deep Rotation
   Estimation,” NeurIPS.**
   [Proceedings paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/fec3392b0dc073244d38eba1feb8e6b7-Paper.pdf).
   Sections 3.2–3.4 analyze gradients and continuity of SVD-based rotation
   estimation. The paper supports this construction while identifying
   degeneracies and conditions associated with large or undefined derivatives.

   **Interpretation:** assess alignment differentiation separately from the
   outer penalty. A finite loss value does not establish a correct gradient;
   conversely, the presence of an SVD does not imply a broken gradient. Avoid
   treating every repeated singular value as proof of failure: the behavior of
   the composite rotation map matters, not only individual SVD factors.

### 18.3 Questions for a synthetic geometry comparison

The following are proposed behaviors to assess, **not newly completed tests**:

| Toy behavior | Question it isolates |
|---|---|
| Apply the same rigid transform to the entire scene | Is the measurement independent of the global coordinate frame? |
| Translate one point cloud relative to the reference frame | Does the metric register relative placement rather than align it away? |
| Rotate one cloud without changing its internal shape | How does orientation contribute to the reported error? |
| Deform the cloud while retaining its overall placement | Is shape error being mixed with placement error intentionally? |
| Examine points inside, at, and outside an allowed region | Do the loss and its derivative express the intended tolerance? |
| Examine well-conditioned and degenerate alignment inputs | Are derivative failures distinguished from valid gradients and finite forward outputs? |

At a nonsmooth boundary, a unique ordinary derivative need not exist; derivative
checks must account for that rather than silently treating every discrepancy as
an autodiff defect. Likewise, numerical agreement on ordinary toy inputs does
not establish reliable behavior at degenerate alignments.

### 19.1 The hypothesis and the pass/fail scale

The scale bar is already measured (§§3.1–3.2, native torch, three seeds, no MSA,
no template). It is the only thing that defines success:

| Readout | High-confidence regime (P17+Alpha) | Low-confidence regime (P17+JN.1, WT) |
|---|---:|---:|
| ipSAE minimum (PAE cutoff 12) | 0.7949–0.7954 | 0.0000–0.1629 |
| ipTM | 0.9006–0.9017 | 0.2225–0.5794 |
| Cross-chain gPDE | 0.691–0.702 | 4.497–12.466 |
| Target-aligned designed-chain pose RMSD | 1.98–2.96 Å | 22.37–57.56 Å |
| Target Cα RMSD after alignment | 0.66–0.74 Å | 1.84–1.87 Å |

Two properties of this table carry the proof of concept:

- **The separation is large and monotone.** Roughly 5× on ipSAE, ~17× on
  cross-chain gPDE at its widest, an order of magnitude on pose. There is room
  for a partial result to be visible rather than lost in noise.
- **Both chains fold in both cases** (plDDT 94.2 vs 88.3–89.8) and **the target
  realigns in both cases** (0.66–0.74 Å vs 1.84–1.87 Å). The entire gap is
  designed chain *placement* plus interface confidence — exactly the two quantities a
  sequence gradient could plausibly move. This is what makes the system a usable
  gradient test bed, and it is a stronger reason to use it than the biology.

A pass is movement toward the left column on both readouts, from a trajectory
driven only by the gradient. A partial pass — confidence moves, pose does not —
is a real and reportable outcome, and is the outcome the one existing data point
hints at (§19.2). Nothing here establishes binding, and the proof of concept
does not need it to.

⚠️ **The scale bar above comes from native PyTorch OpenDDE; the gradient work
runs the JAX/mosaic path.** Those paths are known not to agree: §17.12 and the
status handoff record that stable-aggregation Mosaic and direct JAX differ in
their shared atom37 coordinates, PAE and pLDDT for both seeds, and the post-fix
forward batch was JN.1-only with no paired Alpha control. Until the separation
was reproduced in the path that computes the gradients, there was no calibrated
scale to measure recovery against — step 0 of §19.5.

✅ **Resolved 2026-10-05.** Both poles reproduce in the JAX gradient path:
Alpha 0.8067 / 2.47 Å / 0.71 Å and JN.1 WT 0.0023 / 47.36 Å / 1.83 Å. Four of
the six values fall inside the native ranges; two sit marginally outside, both
in the direction of closer agreement (see §20.1). See [§20.1](#201-step-0-the-scale-bar-reproduces-in-the-gradient-path).

### 19.3 Three controls the historical plan is missing

None of these appears in §9's pending list or §17's two-test plan. Under the
proof-of-concept framing they are the highest-value work in the project.

#### A. Gradient sanity, with no search at all

One forward-plus-backward pass through the full path on WT P17+JN.1, inspecting
the sequence gradient directly. Cost: hours, one GPU, no search machinery.

What it can check, well-posed:

- finite and nonzero gradient at every designable position;
- gradient magnitude **concentrated at interface-proximal designable positions** rather
  than spread uniformly or dominated by fixed positions;
- reproducible across model seeds **and across fresh processes** — the latter is
  untested for the gradient path, and §17.12's JAX repeat variability plus the
  status doc's fresh-process differences make it a live risk;
- per-term decomposition: which of contact, pose RMSD, ipTM, bidirectional PAE,
  `pTMEnergy` and AbLang2 actually contributes gradient norm after
  `ClippedGradient` (§17.2 — clipping and entropy recalibration can absorb a
  term's weight entirely).

⚠️ **Correction to an earlier chat suggestion.** It was proposed that this check
test whether the gradient points toward "Alpha-like residues at the five
anchor residues." That is not well posed: the five anchor residue substitutions in §2 are
**specified-site residues on the target**, and the correct compensating changes on the
**designed chain** side are unknown — there is no ground-truth answer to compare a
direction against. The checks listed above replace it; a direction-correctness
check requires control B, which supplies a known answer.

#### B. The recoverability control — the actual proof of concept

**This is the main gap.** P17 → JN.1 is not known to have a solution. Under a
5-edit designable-only budget it may well have none, and even unconstrained there is no
guarantee any P17-framework sequence makes OpenDDE confident about this interface.
So a negative result in that direction is **uninterpretable**: bad gradient, or
empty feasible set, and the experiment cannot distinguish them.

The fix is to run the same machinery where a solution provably exists:

1. Start from **P17+Alpha**, at ipSAE 0.795 / pose 1.98–2.96 Å.
2. Mutate designable positions until confidence collapses into the low-confidence regime.
   Record the damaged sequence and its metrics.
3. Run the **identical** gradient search from the damaged sequence.
4. Pass = metrics climb back toward 0.795 / 2–3 Å.

A solution exists by construction — it is the sequence you just left. This makes
both outcomes informative, which the JN.1 direction cannot do.

Two further reasons this is the better test bed, both already in the record:
**`P17_Alpha.pdb` is experimental** (PDB 8GZ5, X-ray, 1.70 Å), so pose RMSD is
measured against a real structure rather than the modeled, PyRosetta-relaxed
`P17_JN1.pdb`; and the Alpha case is the one where the predictor is *reproducible
to ±0.001 ipTM*, versus JN.1's 0.22/0.30/0.58 across seeds, so recovery is
measured against a stable rather than an unstable reference.

Design points to settle before running it:

- **How much damage.** Enough to reach the low-confidence regime, no more. Sweep
  damage severity if cheap — recovery from 1–2 destroyed positions versus 5–8 is
  itself the interesting curve, and a graded difficulty axis is more informative
  than one binary attempt.
- **Trivial-recovery concern.** The search may simply revert to the exact
  original residues. For a gradient proof of concept that is a *pass*, not a
  cheat. A harder variant forbids the original residue at damaged positions, so
  recovery must find a different solution.
- Damaged-sequence selection must be fixed **before** seeing recovery results,
  and recorded, or the control becomes a selection-biased search for an easy case.

#### C. Run unconstrained before constrained

Drop the edit cap, the designable-only mask and the AbLang2 term for the first attempt
at both B and the JN.1 direction. Rationale: if the gradient cannot restore
confidence with *unlimited* edits over *all* positions, a 5-edit designable-only result
tells you nothing, and the constrained run spends cluster time on a strictly
harder version of an unanswered question. Unconstrained also removes the two
leading confounds — an empty feasible set, and AbLang2 naturalness pulling
against the structural terms at weight 0.10. Reintroduce the constraints only
once the unconstrained version has a known answer; at that point they become a
measurement of *how much* constraint the gradient tolerates, which is a result.

### 19.4 What this reframing deprioritizes

Not deleted — these sections hold real corrections worth keeping — but off the
critical path, and not to be launched on cluster time before §19.5's ladder:

| Deprioritized | Why it is not the proof of concept |
|---|---|
| Edit budget 5 vs 7 (§7) | Presupposes a working gradient; §19.3C says start unconstrained anyway. |

Also newly moot: the recurring caveat that `P17_JN1.pdb` is a **modeled** rather
than experimental reference. For a biological claim that is a weakness. For

### 19.5 Minimal experiment ladder

Each rung is interpretable on its own and gates the next.

| Step | Experiment | Passes if |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Paired Alpha + JN.1 forward control **in the JAX/mosaic gradient path**: 64 steps, stable aggregation, 3 seeds, backbone checks on | The §19.1 separation reproduces with valid geometry. Without this there is no calibrated scale (§19.1). |
| **1** | Gradient sanity, no search (§19.3A) | Gradient finite, nonzero, interface-concentrated, reproducible across fresh processes; per-term norms known. |
| **2** | Recoverability control, unconstrained (§19.3B + C) | Damaged-Alpha metrics climb back toward 0.795 / 2–3 Å. **This is the proof of concept.** |
| **3** | JN.1 direction, unconstrained | Confidence and/or pose move toward the high-confidence regime. A null here, given step 2 passing, is evidence about the *target*, not the gradient — which is exactly the discrimination the current design cannot make. |
| **4** | JN.1 direction, constrained (designable-only, 5–7 edits, AbLang2) | The original project question, now interpretable. |
| **5** | Search policy, retention metrics, pose-loss alternatives (§§8, 10, 17.4, 18) | Only worth compute once 2–3 have answers. |

**Cost, and a correction to an earlier draft of this table.** Step 0 is cheap
and its cost is measured: a warm 64-step forward took ~2.4 s at 13.66–14.43 GiB
in the post-fix batch. Step 1 is cheap only in *call count* — it is a forward
**plus backward**, so it runs the full gradient path and its memory cannot be
inferred from those forward-only figures. The nearest measurement is the audited
pose archive's 52.69/52.65 GiB peak inside a 125.85 GiB preallocated pool, at
**eight** sampling steps; at 64 steps gradient memory and runtime are unmeasured
on any path.

That makes step 1 more valuable, not less: a handful of gradient calls is the
cheapest way to measure gradient-path memory, runtime and repeat behavior at 64
steps before committing a search stage to them. Step 2 is the first step whose
cost is dominated by *many* gradient calls rather than a few.

### 19.6 Design changes this framing implies

Three concrete changes, all consequences of the reframing rather than new
research directions:

1. **Pose must be able to pull, not only veto.** Retention ranks on mean ipSAE,
   with pose entering arms C/D as a feasibility ordering only
   (`max(0, worst-seed RMSD − ceiling)`, §17.6). If pose recovery is half the
   claim, a design in which pose can only reject candidates and never select
   them cannot demonstrate it. For the PoC, prefer either a declared combined
   objective, or — cleaner — **separate confidence-only and pose-only arms**, so
   it is visible which readout the gradient actually moves. Avoid silently adding
   Ångströms to a dimensionless score (§17.4 already warns about this).
2. **Measure gradient repeat noise before assuming the influence gate can
   pass.** ⚠️ An earlier draft of this section called the gate "unsatisfiable by
   construction" and proposed replacing or demoting it. That was wrong, and the
   code is right: `assess_influence`
   (`examples/p17_pose_diagnostics.py:190`) computes
   `required = max(min_proposal_tv, repeat_factor × repeat_proposal_tv)` and
   sets `resolvable = required < 1.0`. The threshold is **recomputed from each
   run's own measured repeat noise**, so it is unreachable only when
   `repeat_factor × repeat_tv ≥ 1` — at the default factor of 3, when repeat TV
   reaches about 1/3. §17.10's run hit exactly that (repeat TV 0.3845/0.5564 →
   required 1.1536/1.6692, above total variation's maximum of 1), and the gate
   then correctly reported `influence_status=inconclusive_repeat_variability`
   with `proposal_influence=null` rather than declaring no influence.

   So the blocker was that run's **data**, not the rule. The actionable
   consequence is a sequencing one: gradient-path repeat behavior is unmeasured
   at 64 steps, and stable aggregation's bitwise-identical repeats were
   demonstrated on the **forward** path only. Whether this gate can pass at all
   is therefore decided by §19.3A's reproducibility check — which is another
   reason to run step 1 before step 2. Do **not** clip the threshold after
   seeing results (§17.10 is right about that), and do **not** relax the
   geometry gates (`predicted_backbone_plausible`, `interpretable_target_fit`,
   the 3 Å target-fit limit): those are what caught the 14–20 Å backbone
   failure that voided the pilot.
3. **64 sampling steps, not 8.** Eight-step predictions failed backbone checks on
   every path and have not been retested since the numerical fixes. The cluster
   wrapper already pins 64; keep it pinned everywhere, including any new PoC
   runner.

### 19.7 What a successful proof of concept would and would not establish

> **Outcome (2026-10-05).** This section was written before any result.
> [§20.6](#206-what-these-results-support-and-what-they-do-not) states what the
> measurements actually support, which is narrower than "would" below in one
> specific way: pose sits in the proposal gradient, so pose recovery is partly
> circular and needs the arm in §19.6 item 1 to separate.

**Would:** that a frozen structure predictor's sequence gradient carries usable
information about interface formation — enough that following it recovers that
predictor's own confidence and pose metrics from a degraded starting point. That
is a statement about gradient-guided design as a method, demonstrated on a system
with a known answer, and it is transferable.

**Would not:** that any designed sequence binds anything. Every metric in play is
OpenDDE's opinion; §6's caveat is unchanged and unchangeable by this experiment.
The proof of concept deliberately does not need the biological claim, which is
why it can actually be completed.

### 20.4 What the winners actually did: alternative repair, not reversion

Substitutions relative to the reference (positions 1-indexed):

| candidate | edits from reference | ipSAE |
|---|---|---:|
| 0 (damaged) | `100G→C`, `106N→A` | 0.0000 |
| 1 | `100G→C`, `101W→L`, `102G→F`, `106N→A` | 0.7018 |
| 2 | `32Y→L`, `100G→C`, `102G→F`, `106N→A` | 0.6870 |
| 3 | `104E→A`, `106N→A` | 0.8347 |
| 4 | `30S→M`, `100G→C`, `105R→C`, `106N→A` | 0.8009 |

Three of four **kept the lethal `100 G→C`** and compensated elsewhere; only
candidate 3 reverted it. Exact reversion of both damaged positions was within
budget and reachable — the edit cap is measured from the start sequence — so the
search had the option and declined it, because the alternatives score better.

Two observations follow, both relevant beyond this control:

1. **The gradient knows the start sequence but not the reference sequence.**
   `EditBudget` (`src/mosaic/losses/transformations.py:137`) is a hinge on
   `E(s) = Σ(1 − ⟨s, s_ref⟩)·designable` anchored to a one-hot of the
   *starting* sequence, at weight 5.0 — the heaviest term. The discrete cap and
   the fixed-position mask are likewise start-relative. The reference's
   *coordinates* enter through `BinderPoseRMSD` and the specified contact site, but its
   *sequence* is never supplied. So "it found a different sequence achieving the
   reference geometry" is exactly what the objective asked for.
2. **The predictor's confidence optimum is not the experimental sequence.**
   Candidate 3 (0.8347) and the single edit `106 N→A` (0.8174) both exceed the
   1.70 Å crystal structure's own 0.8067. Every substitution the search chose —
   `30 S→M`, `32 Y→L`, `101 W→L`, `102 G→F`, `104 E→A`, `105 R→C` — moves
   toward hydrophobic and away from polar or charged, six for six. That is a
   recognized failure mode of predictor-confidence objectives, and it is §6's
   caveat with numbers attached: the search optimizes the predictor's opinion,
   and that opinion peaks away from the validated answer. It is a methodological
   observation about the objective, not a biological claim.

`102 G→F` was found independently by candidates 1 and 2 — the only repeated
compensating substitution, immediately adjacent to the damaged position. The
move trajectory (§20.9) shows it was a single proposal in `independent_seed0`,
accepted at once with a realized +0.6896, while the same substitution in
`independent_seed1` realized −0.0037: repeated across winners, but not a
reproducible effect.

> the reversion does not survive the move trajectory. Three of the four runs
> never proposed the crystal residue at position 100; the fourth proposed it
> three times and accepted it every time.

### 20.6 What these results support, and what they do not

**Supported.** A frozen structure predictor's gradient with respect to designed chain
sequence carries information that, when followed, reliably improves that
predictor's own interface confidence and target-aligned designed-chain pose. This held
across 4 Alpha search seeds and 8 JN.1 search seeds, on structural seeds never
used for selection, with verified backbone geometry. On a one-substitution
repair with a known solution it recovers fully; on the JN.1 interface it closes
roughly 42% of the confidence gap and 71% of the pose gap.

**Not supported, and not claimed.**

- **That anything binds.** Every quantity here is OpenDDE's opinion. §6 is
  unchanged, and §20.4's hydrophobic trend plus two scores above the crystal
  reference are direct evidence that the opinion's optimum is not the answer.
- **That pose recovery is independent of the objective.** `BinderPoseRMSD` is in
  the proposal gradient, so the 71% pose closure is partly circular. Separating
  guidance from measurement needs an arm where pose is scored but not
  optimized — §17.4's arms A and C, and §19.6's first point. **This is now the
  single most important open item.**
- **That confidence gain generalizes beyond the predictor.** ipSAE drives
  retention. Held-out seeds establish generalization across prediction
  stochasticity, not beyond the model.
- **That the reference arrangement is correct.** `P17_JN1.pdb` is a modeled,
  PyRosetta-relaxed transplant, so "71% of the pose gap" is 71% toward an
  assumed arrangement. The Alpha control does not share this limitation, its
  reference being experimental.
- **Anything about search policy.** One policy (population) was run, per §17's
  standing decision and §19.4. No comparison was made.
- **That the constraints are what limit the result.** The JN.1 arm ran
  constrained only — 5 edits, designable-only. §19.3C's argument is untouched: without
  the unconstrained arm, a partial result cannot be attributed to the gradient
  rather than to the feasible set. That arm remains unbuilt.

A natural reading of §20.5 is that pose moved partway and would move further

Two of eight were still improving at the wall. The other six plateaued — but

6–23 evaluations is at least as consistent with a local basin as with a
ceiling. That points at exploration, not only at evaluation count.

**"More search" means three separable things, and only the first is confirmed
binding here:**

| | Status | Cost to test |
|---|---|---|
| More evaluations | **Confirmed binding** — all eight hit the ceiling | Raise `--max-score-calls`; ~2.4 s per warm forward, so 300 calls × 2 selection seeds is roughly 25 min per worker |
| More exploration | Plausible — six plateaus under a cold chain. `temp`/`proposal_temp` have never been swept (§5) | Same run, warmer acceptance and/or higher entropy target |
| More edits | ~~**Unknown.**~~ **Answered 2026-10-05 for the Alpha control (§22.1):** five edits sufficed for an 18 Å gap, were inside the budget, and were not found — a search limit, not a feasible-set limit. ⚠️ **A caution specific to pose.** Retention ranks on mean ipSAE; pose enters
only through the proposal gradient (§14). So a larger evaluation budget buys
more *confidence* optimization, and pose improves only as far as the gradient
drags it. Extending the budget alone is not an experiment about pose. Driving
pose deliberately requires it in retention — the mirror image of the
pose-weight-zero arm: one tests whether the observed pose gain is circular, the
other tests whether pose can be moved on purpose. Both are needed, and they are
different runs.

⚠️ **Two cautions on reading this table.** These are proposals *along the
chain*, including moves later superseded, so an accepted move is not a move
present in any winner. And a high acceptance rate at a position is consistent
with that position mattering *or* with the loss being flat there — acceptance is
evaluated on the true loss at temperature 0.02, so it does carry signal, but not
an attribution.

**This also weakens an earlier concern.** §20.4 noted that all six substitutions
chosen by the Alpha winners moved toward hydrophobic and away from polar or

The JN.1 proposals do not show that direction: `31 V→D` and `31 V→E` add

does not generalize beyond the Alpha winners and should not be carried as a
general claim about the objective.

**Did the gradient ever name the right residue?** §20.9 above reads the JN.1
runs, where no correct answer is known. The Alpha control does have one, so the
same extraction run against its four searches can ask a sharper question:
`examples/p17_search_trajectory.py` reconstructs each proposal with its
predicted Δloss, its realized score change, and — given `--reference` — whether

|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `independent_seed0` | 33 | 17 | 12/33 (36%) | S, T, F×3, W |
| `independent_seed1` | 34 | 21 | 14/34 (41%) | M, A×2, H, T, Y |
| `population_seed0` | 33 | 20 | 13/33 (39%) | N, **G×3**, M, P, Q×2 |
| `population_seed1` | 32 | 13 | 10/32 (31%) | S, I, T, A |

Position 100 — the damaged `G→C` site — was the most-visited position in every
run, 24 proposals across the four. **Exactly one run ever proposed `G` there.**
`population_seed0` proposed it three times, all three were accepted, and the
realized score deltas were +0.1271, +0.7559 and +0.7543. The other three runs
proposed eighteen substitutions at that position between them — S, T, F, W, M,

So the gradient's positional signal and its substitutional signal come apart,
and this is the sharper version of §20.9's position-101 observation: the
gradient reliably identified *which* position was broken and mostly failed to
name *what* belonged there. When it did name it, the move worked immediately
and was never reversed.

> **Correction to §20.4.** That section says the search "had the option and
> declined it". That is wrong in both directions. In three of four runs `G` at
> 100 was never proposed at all, so nothing was declined; in the fourth it was
> proposed and accepted every time. The winner-level table in §20.4 stands —
> three of four winners kept the damaged `C` — but the reason is a proposal
> distribution that rarely offered the reference residue, not a search that
> weighed it and preferred an alternative.

The sign-agreement column is the other measurable here, and it needs its
caveat stated plainly: `predicted_loss_delta` is a Taylor-1 delta on the
*proposal loss*, which carries the pose hinge and the auxiliary weights, while
the realized delta is measured on the *retention objective*, mean
directional-min ipSAE. These are different objectives by construction (§19.6),
so disagreement is not by itself an error.

⚠️ **Corrected 2026-10-05, and the original reasoning was wrong.** This
paragraph previously read the 31–41% sign agreement as "below the 50% a coin
would give" and concluded the two objectives were mildly *anti*-correlated.
That inference does not hold: 50% is not the null here. The proposal mechanism
selects moves by `softmax(−delta/temp)`, so it predicts an improvement for
91–96% of the moves it samples, while improvements materialize 9–16% of the
time — which puts raw sign agreement near 20% by arithmetic alone, with no
anti-correlation required. §21.5 computes the statistic that does carry
information, P(realized improvement | predicted improvement) against the base
rate, and finds them equal. The conclusion to draw is an absence of
discrimination among the sampled moves, not anti-correlation.

⚠️ Four runs, ~130 proposals, one damaged position, one target. The sign-
agreement figure in particular is a small sample over a non-random set of
moves — the search chose them, so they are concentrated where the proposal
loss expected gains. Treat all of this as a measurement of this control, not a
property of OpenDDE gradients.

### 20.10 What the pose literature contributed, and what it left unbuilt

§18 reviewed four papers on pose constraints and §18.4 concluded by retaining
the existing hinge, changing no code. Read against §20.8's stall, two of those
papers speak directly to the situation, and the accounting is worth stating
plainly.

**TrajOpt (Schulman et al. 2013) is the directly applicable unimplemented
idea.** Its mechanism is not the hinge — it is *penalty continuation*: when a
constraint is not satisfied, increase the coefficient and re-solve, in an outer
loop. §18.2 recorded exactly this ("an outer procedure that increases penalty
coefficients when necessary"). A search that reaches 15–30 Å and stops is the
case that mechanism addresses, and nothing in this repository implements it.

⚠️ **Corrected 2026-10-05.** An earlier revision argued here that "naively
raising `--weight-pose` may do nothing", because `ClippedGradient` and the
entropy-recalibrated proposal temperature would absorb it, and concluded that
TrajOpt-style escalation must therefore act on a retention threshold rather
than a loss coefficient. **That argument does not hold.** Two errors:

- **Uniform scaling is not the same as changing one term's share.** Norm
  clipping and an entropy-matched temperature do absorb a uniform rescaling of
  the whole composite objective, because neither the gradient direction nor the
  normalized proposal distribution changes. Raising `--weight-pose` alone is
  not uniform: it changes pose's *relative* contribution to the composite
  direction, and renormalizing the total afterwards preserves that changed
  direction. So the coefficient is not absorbed.
- **A constant hinge derivative is not a constant input gradient.** The Barron
  paragraph below says gradient magnitude is constant beyond tolerance. That is
  true of ∂hinge/∂RMSD, which is 1 past the tolerance. It is not true of
  ∂loss/∂sequence, which carries ∂RMSD/∂coords and ∂coords/∂sequence as well,
  neither of which is constant in the displacement.

What survives is narrower: penalty continuation is unimplemented here, and
whether escalating the coefficient, escalating a retention threshold, or
neither moves pose is **an untested hypothesis**. The measured facts to reason
from are §21.3 (pose moves without the pose term at all) and §21.4 (268 extra
evaluations bought confidence, not pose) — not an absorption argument.

**Barron (2019) shows the hinge's influence profile is an unmade choice.** The
current `relu(rmsd − tolerance)` is linear beyond tolerance, so gradient
magnitude is constant whether the designed chain is 47 Å or 5 Å from the reference.
§18.2 noted that robust losses deliberately *suppress* large residuals as
outliers, which would be wrong here; the converse is equally relevant, since a
profile with growing influence at large residuals pushes harder in exactly the
47 Å regime these runs start from. §18.4 explicitly declined to choose. That
remains an open, cheap, testable alternative rather than a recommendation.

**Berenson, Srinivasa and Kuffner (2011)** contributed the conceptual
separation that an RMSD threshold is not a task-space region, because RMSD
conflates placement with internal shape. `tests/test_pose_rmsd_geometry.py`
then confirmed that empirically on toy clouds. It changed no loss.

**Levinson et al. (2020)** produced the one hard numerical result: the
alignment gradient is unreliable at degenerate alignments — zeroed, nonfinite,
or finite-and-wrong, depending on the rotation and the backend
(`src/mosaic/util.py`, `tests/test_pose_rmsd_geometry.py`). `kabsch_conditioning`
exists to detect it and is **still not called anywhere in the pipeline**, so
whether any gradient call in these runs hit a poorly conditioned alignment is
unmeasured. For a 195-residue target it is unlikely; it is also a one-line
addition to the reference audit.

**Net accounting.** The literature review supplied framing and one negative
numerical finding. It produced no change to the objective. The single mechanism
it offers for a search that stalls partway — penalty continuation — is
unimplemented, and §18.4's decision to keep the hinge as the baseline is why.
That decision was defensible when no result existed to diagnose; §20.8 is the
first evidence that would inform it.

### 21.2 The decoy control is interpretable, and it comes back negative

The decoy is the real JN.1 target with its 16 specified-contact-site positions
permuted: 92.39% identity to the real target, composition matched, fold
preserved outside the specified site, two specified-site residues unchanged by chance.

**Its fold gate passed.** Mean target pLDDT **0.835** against the declared
0.70 minimum, and `fold_check.json` records `interpretable: true`. That is the
declared criterion being met — it is not independent proof that the fold is
preserved. pLDDT is the same model's self-assessment, and the gate's threshold
was chosen, not derived. What it rules out is the clearly vacuous case, a decoy
the predictor cannot fold at all (the whole-chain shuffle at 0.405 and the
trimmed IL7RA at 0.425, both rejected before this run). Against the decoy, WT P17 scores ipSAE 0.0000 with mean
pose 25.59 Å and target fit 2.58 Å.

At matched compute — best ipSAE within the first 32 scored sequences, which is
all the decoy and `posezero` arms were given:

| Arm | per-seed best | mean |
|---|---|---:|
| **decoy** (specified site permuted, pose on) | 0.452, 0.378, 0.533, 0.426 | **0.447** |

What this supports, stated precisely: **high predictor confidence is obtainable

are different optimization problems with different achievable ceilings, so the
decoy's absolute score is not a subtractable baseline. "Substantially
target-nonspecific", as an earlier revision of this section put it, overstates
what two four-seed arms on two different targets can establish.

Limits of this comparison, which do not rescue the result but bound it: four

not rest on a marginal difference; but it is a demonstration that the metric can
be driven without the specified site, not a decomposition of the real
target's gain. Rerunning the decoy's held-out stage once the defect in §21.7 is
fixed is the cheap way to put it on the same footing.

compute.**

⚠️ **And it is worse than unresolved: the two arms are not comparable at all.**
Read from the saved configs on 2026-10-05, `budget` ran at **300 score calls,
`target_entropy` 0.8, `acceptance_temperature` 0.05** while `posezero` and
`decoy` ran at **32, 0.6 and 0.02**. §20.11 item 2 designed `budget`
deliberately as a budget *and exploration* arm, which is legitimate as
exploration but means it varies three factors against the others. Restricting
to the first 32 scored sequences, as the table above does, controls the call
count and leaves entropy and acceptance temperature differing. So this
comparison cannot settle the pose-guidance question in either direction, and
the matched-compute table should be read as an upper bound on how much the
pose term could be contributing rather than as an estimate of it. And `budget` at its full 300 evaluations reaches a mean of 21.9 Å
against 22.9 Å at 32 — so 268 additional evaluations bought roughly 1 Å of pose
while raising mean ipSAE from 0.225 to 0.654. That is direct support for
§20.8's caution that extending the evaluation budget is an experiment about
confidence, not about pose.

A useful by-product: the two arms' WT baselines were measured independently in
fresh processes and agree to **0.07 Å of worst-case pose (52.75 vs 52.82 Å) and
0.003 ipSAE (0.0026 vs 0.0000)**. That is the first cross-process noise floor
for the gradient path's held-out scoring, and every change above is orders of
magnitude larger than it.

Read against §21.2, though, the mechanism is less encouraging than the number:
if confidence can be earned by docking anywhere that forms plausible contacts,
pose improves simply by leaving a 48 Å nowhere-placement, without the new
placement being the reference interface. Best pose reached is 13.98 Å against
the Alpha reference's 2.63 Å (§20.1), and the remaining candidates sit at
22–28 Å, inside the non-binding range.

### 21.5 The first-order delta does not discriminate among the moves it selects

This is the measurement §6 and §11.2 item 3 identified as never having been
made on P17, and `--save-saliency` plus the per-proposal event log now make it
possible. Each `proposal` event records `predicted_loss_delta`, the Taylor-1
estimate for the chosen move; pairing it with the realized ipSAE change from
parent to child over all 314–319 proposals per run:

| seed | predicted to improve | actually improved | **P(ipSAE up \| predicted up)** | base rate | Spearman on magnitude |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 91% | 15% | **15%** | 15% | −0.11 |
| 1 | 94% | 9% | **8%** | 9% | −0.06 |
| 2 | 93% | 9% | **10%** | 9% | +0.13 |
| 3 | 96% | 16% | **16%** | 16% | −0.39 |

**The conditional equals the base rate in every seed, to within one point.**
Magnitude is no better: Spearman among predicted-improvers flips sign across
seeds and never exceeds 0.4.

⚠️ **What this does and does not measure.** It is a statistic *conditional on
the pool the gradient already chose*. The proposal distribution is
`softmax(−delta/temp)` over feasible moves, so by the time a move is logged it
has already been selected for having a favourable delta — hence the 91–96%
column. The measurement therefore shows **no discrimination within that
selected pool**. It does not show that the pool itself is no better than a
uniformly drawn one, which is the question that matters for whether the
gradient earns its cost, and which this statistic cannot reach. An earlier
revision of this section wrote "proposals that do not beat random" and
described the pipeline as "diverse random search"; **no random-proposal
comparison has been run**, so neither statement was established. §21.8 item 1
is that experiment.

What the gradient costs, regardless: per run, 300 score calls at 4.9 s and 108
gradient calls at 11.1 s, so `gradient_seconds` is **1196 of 2664 s — 45% of
wall-clock**. Redirected to scoring, that is roughly 1.8× more candidates
evaluated. Whether that trade is favourable is exactly what §21.8 item 1
settles.

⚠️ **This does not show that autodiff is wrong.** `predicted_loss_delta`
predicts the **composite proposal loss** — contact, pose RMSD, ipTM,
bidirectional PAE, pTMEnergy, AbLang2, edit penalty (§14) — not ipSAE. So the
measurement is of the proxy-to-objective gap (§11.2 item 3), and it cannot
separate that from a bad first-order approximation (§11.2 item 2). Separating
them requires the realized *proposal loss* logged for every scored candidate;
`proposal_metrics` currently fires only for the 108 that became gradient
parents. That is a logging change, not an experiment.

### 21.6 The population layer, by contrast, is working

Final active population, five-edit budget — so two candidates can differ by at
most 10 positions:

| seed | pairwise Hamming among 4 parents | winner lineage depth | parent slots crossed |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 0 | 8, 9, 10, 9, 7, 9 | 11 | 0, 1, 2 |
| 1 | 9, 10, 6, 9, 10, 10 | 7 | 0 |
| 2 | 10, 8, 10, 9, 8, 10 | 35 | 0, 1, 2 |
| 3 | 8, 9, 7, 7, 8, 9 | 33 | 0, 1, 2, 3 |

Parents hold 6–10 differences out of a maximum of 10 — near-disjoint edit sets,
not near-duplicates — and in three of four seeds the winning lineage passed
through three or four different parent slots. §10.2's policy-1 hypothesis, that
local competition lets alternative designable-position edit combinations survive instead of all
descendants following one early winner, is behaving as designed.

Acceptance rates: `budget` 18–39%, `posezero` 55–81%, `decoy` 33–63%. The lower
`budget` figures follow from its longer runs, where a stronger incumbent is
harder to displace.

⚠️ **This is observed behavior, not a measured contribution.** Diverse parents
and multi-slot lineages show the population mechanism doing what it was
designed to do; they do not establish that it improved the outcome. The
comparison that would is §10.7's original policy-1-versus-policy-2 run under
matched compute, which has never been executed — every arm here is
`population`. An earlier revision of this section concluded "the search memory
is contributing and the gradient-informed proposal is not", and described the
pipeline as diverse random search with an expensive scorer. Neither half of
that is established: no random-proposal arm and no independent-policy arm has
been run.

What can be said is narrower and still useful. The expensive ipSAE filter over
300 candidates is demonstrably doing work (§21.4). The gradient's
discrimination within its selected moves is absent (§21.5). The population
holds genuinely distinct edit sets rather than collapsing. Which of these
drives the result is open, and two cheap arms would close it.

### 21.7 Defects and corrections recorded during this analysis

- **The decoy arm's held-out stage failed on a code defect, not a result.** All
  eight shards raised `ValueError: predicted structure sequence does not match
  scored complex` (`p17_search_outputs.py:128`, via
  `p17_rescore_winners.py:473`). Cause: the decoy target exists only as a
  *sequence*, generated in memory and recorded as `decoy_sequence` in
  `fold_check.json`; there is no PDB for it. `p17_alpha_recovery.py` passes
  `--target-sequence` to the search and the fold check, but
  `p17_rescore_winners.py` accepts only `--complex/--designed chain-chain/
  --target-chain`, so rescoring fell back to the real JN.1 target. **The
  consistency guard worked**: nothing was saved against the wrong complex. Fix
  is to add `--target-sequence` to the rescorer, mirroring the existing
  `--complex` plumbing. Until then the decoy comparison rests on selection
  seeds.
- ⚠️ **Corrected during analysis: a base-rate artifact.** §21.5 was first
  computed as "sign agreement 13–21%, below the 50% chance level," which reads
  as the gradient being anti-predictive. It is not: the proposal mechanism
  predicts improvement for 91–96% of proposals by construction, since
  `softmax(−delta/temp)` selects on exactly that quantity, while improvements
  materialize 9–16% of the time. The conditional-versus-base-rate comparison in
  §21.5 is the correct statistic and supports "uninformative," not
  "anti-predictive."
- **`recommended_rung` in the Alpha calibration is confidence-only.**
  `reached_non_binding` tests mean ipSAE against a ceiling and never inspects
  pose, so it recommended the 2-edit rung whose pose was 3.69 Å — a rung with
  no pose headroom, where half the hypothesis cannot be tested. §20.3 reached
  the same conclusion from the other direction. The check should require both
  axes degraded; `--select-rung 5` is the current workaround.
- **Three settings move together in the `budget` arm, not one.** Read from the
  saved configs: `budget` used 300 score calls, `target_entropy` 0.8 and
  `acceptance_temperature` 0.05; `posezero` and `decoy` used 32, 0.6 and 0.02.
  `p17_confidence_search.py`'s own defaults are 0.6 and 0.02, matching
  §§17.6 and 20.8; `run_p17_followups.sh` overrides them for `plan_budget`
  only (`BUDGET_ENTROPY=0.8`, `BUDGET_ACCEPT=0.05`). This was deliberate per
  §20.11 item 2, but it confounds both §21.3's pose comparison and §21.4's
  budget attribution, and it is corrected in both. **Every future arm
  comparison must pin entropy and acceptance temperature across all cells.**

**Overclaims corrected on review, 2026-10-05.** An external review of this
section's first revision identified four inferences that its own data does not
support. All four are corrected in place above; recorded here so the pattern is
visible rather than silently edited away:

| Claim as first written | Why it failed | Where corrected |
|---|---|---|
| "proposals that do not beat random", "diverse random search with an expensive scorer" | No random-proposal arm was run. The conditional-vs-base-rate statistic is computed *within* the pool the gradient selected, so it cannot speak to whether that pool beats a uniform one. | §21.5, §21.6 |

### 22.2 The diagnosis: a placement failure, not a folding failure

> ⚠️ **Superseded in detail by [§25](#25-test-a-executed-the-designs-are-at-the-site-and-the-failure-is-registry--2026-10-05) (2026-10-05).** This section's
> conclusion that the failure is placement rather than folding stands. Its
> characterisation of that placement — "docked 14–27 Å from the reference site"
> — does not: measured on the structures, the designs *contact the intended
> specified site* at a mean closest approach of 2.7 Å, and the pose error decomposes
> into a ~170° framework-mediated flip at the site and a 12–21° rotation with a
> 13–19 Å offset. Read §25.1 before quoting the displacement figures.

The held-out prediction tables carry `binder_internal_rmsd_A` and interface
residue counts alongside pose, and read together they localize the problem.
From §21's arms (held-out, mean of three seeds):

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| budget | 1 | 0.678 | 13.98 Å | 1.87 Å | **1.44 Å** | 36/36 |
| budget | 2 | 0.771 | 22.89 Å | 1.80 Å | **2.00 Å** | 36/48 |
| budget | 3 | 0.602 | 23.48 Å | 1.79 Å | **1.59 Å** | 34/45 |

| posezero | 3 | 0.495 | 19.51 Å | 1.76 Å | **1.70 Å** | 39/47 |

Three things hold across every row:

1. **The designed chain folds correctly.** Independently aligned designed chain RMSD is
   1.44–3.72 Å. Contrast §17.10's audited archive, where designed-chain-internal ran
   to ~16 Å and the predictions were malformed.
2. **The target folds and superimposes.** Target fit 1.76–1.94 Å, inside the
   3 Å gate. On the Alpha control it is 0.72–0.73 Å, so there the *entire* gap
   is designed-chain placement with nothing else contaminating it.
3. **A large interface forms.** 34–50 residues per side against 2/1 at WT, and
   against 51/42 for the real P17+Alpha complex at ipSAE 0.795 (§3.2).

**So the search produces a correctly folded designed chain making a large, confident
interface, at 14–27 Å of target-aligned pose error.** (This section originally
read that error as a 14–27 Å *displacement from the site*; §25.1 measured it
instead as a flip and a rotation **at** the site. The placement conclusion
holds; the displacement reading does not.) Not a fold failure, not an
absence of interface. A placement failure.

That one fact is consistent with every other result in §§21–22 and explains
them without further assumptions:

- **The decoy (§21.2).** If any sufficiently good interface earns confidence,
  permuting the real specified site does not block the search — it docks elsewhere.
- **The ipSAE/pose anti-correlation (§21.4).** The highest-confidence candidate
  had the worse pose. Some sites are evidently easier to make confident than
  the specified site.
- **Budget buying confidence but not pose (§21.4).** More evaluations find
  better interfaces, not better-placed ones.
- **`posezero` matching the pose-on arm (§21.3).** Pose moves because leaving a
  48 Å nowhere-placement for *any* contact site improves it, with or without a
  pose term.
- **The Alpha 2-edit rung (§20.3).** Confidence collapsed 0.807 → 0.005 while
  pose stayed at 3.69 Å: the same decoupling, in the damage direction.

### 22.3 Where the bottleneck is, and what that implies for the proposed fixes

**No stage of the pipeline selects for site.** Retention ranks on mean
directional-min ipSAE (§12.2), which is computed over whatever interface the
prediction formed and is therefore site-agnostic. Pose enters only through the
proposal gradient (§14), which §21.5 shows does not discriminate among the
moves it selects. The contact term does anchor on specified-site residues — weight
0.5, anchor residues for JN.1 and contact-derived for Alpha (§21) — but it sits inside
that same non-discriminating proposal. Optional pose-aware retention exists
(§17.6, `--retention-pose-margin`) but is a *feasibility veto* ordered before
raw ipSAE, never a selection objective, and no arm run so far enabled it.

Against that diagnosis, the two fixes under discussion both aim at the wrong
stage:

**Dynamic or escalating pose weight.** Aims at gradient strength. But
`posezero` removed the pose term entirely and pose moved about as far (§21.3),
and at matched compute pose-on versus pose-off is 22.9 vs 25.3 Å with
overlapping per-seed values. The term is not driving the outcome when it *is*
active, so amplifying it targets a stage the evidence does not implicate. It
would have *some* effect — §20.10's "the coefficient gets absorbed" argument
was wrong, and is corrected there — but TrajOpt-style continuation is a
remedy for a constraint that the optimizer is trying and failing to satisfy,
whereas here the selection step never asks for it at all. Cheap, worth testing
eventually, not first.

**A larger edit budget.** §22.1 is direct evidence against this being the
current limit: five edits *sufficed* for an 18 Å gap, were inside the budget,
and were not found. Raising 5 → 7 multiplies the feasible set roughly 4,800×
(C(29,5)·19⁵ ≈ 2.9e11 → C(29,7)·19⁷ ≈ 1.4e15, §7) while the search already
fails to locate a reachable solution in the smaller space, so it enlarges a
search problem rather than relieving a feasibility one. For **JN.1
specifically** it may still be necessary, since no 5-edit solution is known to
exist there (§20.8) — but that question is unanswerable until the search can
hit a target known to be reachable. Premature, not wrong in principle.

> ✅ **Measured 2026-10-06, and this argument is confirmed on JN.1 itself
> ([§27.3](#273-budget-7-buys-flips-not-placement)).** The budget-7 cells gained no confidence and raised the
> flipped-pose rate in both registry arms (23 → 26 of 33 off, 18 → 27 of 33
> on; mean rotation 99–117° → 135–140°). "Enlarges a search problem rather
> than relieving a feasibility one" is no longer an inference from the Alpha
> control — it is a direct observation on the target, and §7 is closed at
> budget 5.

**What the diagnosis actually points at: site-specific retention.** Two forms,
in order of preference. A literature review commissioned after this section was
written supports the direction and supplies parameter values from released code;
its proof-of-concept-framed summary is
[`docs/literature_review/README.md`](literature_review/README.md),
and the headline is that the two pipelines closest to this one keep a gradient
term as weak as ours and put the decisive site enforcement in **selection**, as
booleans checked before any confidence metric is consulted.

- **Site-restricted confidence.** Score candidates on interface confidence
  computed only over contacts involving the specified site, rather than over
  whatever interface formed. This makes "confident *here*" the selection
  criterion instead of "confident somewhere", needs no reference structure at
  scoring time — only the site index, which both references already supply —
  and is the one change that would also have failed the decoy, since a permuted
  specified site cannot score well on its own positions. It is a new metric, not a
  flag.
- **Pose in retention**, ranking on pose RMSD or on a declared combination
  rather than using it as a veto. Cruder, and it selects on agreement with a
  reference arrangement, which is acceptable for a proof of concept — the
  question is whether the gradient can reach a *specified* pose — but would be
  circular for a design claim. Closer to existing code.

The decisive property of either: if pose does not move when pose or site **is**
the selection criterion, that is close to decisive about the gradient, because
every other explanation has then been removed. If it does move, the objective
was the limiting factor all along and the gradient was never the problem.

4. ✅ **Done — §22.5.** No seed reverted any damaged residue; three of four

5. ✅ **Answered — [§25](#25-test-a-executed-the-designs-are-at-the-site-and-the-failure-is-registry--2026-10-05).** There is no alternative

### 22.5 What the winners did: no reversions, and position found without identity

§22.1 left open whether the recovered candidates reverted the damaged residues
or repaired elsewhere. The saved sequences answer it, and the answer is
stronger than the recovery fractions suggest. Damage was
`S30A N33H A97P Y107D A108W`; WT was five reversions away, the budget was
exactly five, and WT scores 0.8073 against the best found 0.513.

| seed | edits from start | at damaged positions | elsewhere | **reversions to WT** | Hamming to WT |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---:|
| 0 (0.104) | 3 | `W108E` | `W101P`, `Y109W` | **none** | 5 → **7** |
| 1 (0.083) | 1 | `W108G` | — | **none** | 5 → 5 |
| 2 (0.513) | 5 | `W108E` | `W101E`, `K103H`, `E104G`, `R105F` | **none** | 5 → **9** |

**Not one damaged residue was restored, in any seed.** Three of four moved
*further* from WT, and the best — seed 2, spending its full budget — finished at
Hamming 9, nearly twice its starting distance from the known answer.

Three readings follow, in increasing order of how much they change the
diagnosis.

**The feasible ball is centred on the damaged start, not on WT.** The edit cap
counts differences from the search's own start sequence (`--start-sequence`
becomes that run's WT, §19.3B), so the undamaged sequence sits exactly on the
boundary, reachable only by the single exact path of five specific reversions.
Any non-reverting move stays inside budget while increasing WT distance. The
control remains valid — a solution is in the feasible set — but it is a
needle-threading test rather than a gentle one, and the 19% pose recovery in
§22.1 should be quoted with that attached. A deliberately easier variant would
give the search budget headroom beyond the damage (cap 7 against 5 damaged
positions), which is a different and more forgiving question.

**The gradient localizes the damage but not the identity.** Position 108 was
edited by three of the four seeds — `W108E`, `W108G`, `W108E`. All three
correctly identified 108 as the position to fix. **None chose `A`**, the actual
answer, and none of the 19 non-WT alternatives they did choose recovered the
reference confidence. The same pattern appears weakly at 101 and 109, also
edited repeatedly and never to WT.

This is considerably more specific than "the first-order delta does not
discriminate" (§21.5). Positional information is present and correct; residue
identity at those positions is not. It is also the first result in this project
that bears directly on §8.3's LaMBO-2 question — whether *position selection*
is the transferable part of gradient guidance — and it answers it in the
affirmative while answering the substitution question in the negative.

**Edits concentrate in designable region 3.** Of the eleven substitutions across all four
seeds, nine fall in 97–109 and two in designable region 1 (27, 30–33 region). That matches
§20.9's saliency finding and LaMBO-2's own Figure 3 concentration on CDRH3,
and it means the position-level signal is not diffuse.

⚠️ **Corrected 2026-10-05: this section read endpoints as a trajectory, and
the trajectory says something different.** Everything above is computed from
*final winners*. As the
[search-policy and pose review](search_policy_and_pose_literature_review.md)
§§4 and 9 correctly object, a residue absent from a winner may have been
proposed, or even accepted, earlier in the run — endpoint differences do not

The event logs settle it directly for the **2-edit rung** (damage `G100C`,

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| independent_seed0 | 33 | 9 | 0 |
| independent_seed1 | 34 | 6 | 0 |

`population_seed0` proposed `pos 100 → G`, the exact reversion, three times and

Two further limits now explicit. The 5-edit rung's trajectory has **not** been

And final Hamming distance from the start measures neither the number of moves
explored nor the depth of the trajectory, so the "moved further from WT" column
describes where runs stopped, not where they went.

⚠️ Also: one damaged start, one damage seed, four search seeds, 32 score calls
each. A single run cannot establish that reversion is unreachable, only that it
was not reached here.

Nothing here establishes a measured property. What §22 adds to §21 is that the failure is
specifically one of **placement**, with the designed chain's fold, the target's fold
and the interface size all intact — and that it persists on a problem whose
solution was reachable throughout.

## 23. Scale-up readiness: what the large run needs, and why it targets JN.1 — 2026-10-05

Everything run so far has been diagnostic. No run has committed to a large
evaluation budget with a large seed count, and the results in §§20–22 are what
determine whether that commitment is worth making and what has to be fixed
first.

### 23.1 The target is JN.1, by decision

**The large-scale run targets P17 → JN.1.** That is the project's objective
(§1); Alpha is instrumentation, and a campaign that only ever runs its control
never attempts the thing it exists to answer.

The cost of that choice, stated once so it is not forgotten when the results
arrive: **a null result on JN.1 remains ambiguous.** §20.8 lists it and §22.1
did not change it — no five-edit JN.1 solution is known to exist, so a run that
finds nothing cannot distinguish a search limit from an empty feasible set. The
Alpha control is the only place that distinction is available, and §22.1 used it
to establish that the limit there was the search, not the feasible set.

**Mitigation, cheap: carry a small Alpha arm at identical settings alongside
the JN.1 run.** Two seeds on one of eight GPUs. It is not a second experiment —
it is the interpretability anchor that lets a JN.1 null be read against whether
the same search, at the same budget and settings, could reach an answer known
to be reachable. Without it, a large JN.1 null produces the same
uninterpretable outcome the project has already paid for twice.

### 23.2 Why 32 calls could not have answered anything

The four Alpha seeds spent **1, 3, 5 and 3** edits of a five-edit budget across
32 evaluations (§22.5). A search that cannot traverse its own budget is
characterizing the neighbourhood of its start, not searching. The JN.1 batch in
§20.8 had the same ceiling, and §21.4 showed what lifting it to 300 does:
mean best ipSAE 0.225 → 0.654.

Seed variance collapses with budget, which decides the compute split:

| | per-seed best | mean | spread |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Alpha, 32 calls | 0.104, 0.083, 0.513, 0.086 | 0.197 | one seed 5× the others |
| JN.1, 300 calls | 0.678, 0.772, 0.601, 0.565 | 0.654 | ±0.09 |

At 32 calls the result is dominated by whether one seed gets lucky. Taking the
32-call spread at face value (sd ≈ 0.21 on a mean of 0.20), detecting a 0.1
difference between arms at 80% power would need on the order of **70 seeds per
arm** — prohibitive. The 300-call spread is less than half as wide relative to
its mean.

**So: evaluations before seeds.** Raise `--max-score-calls` first, then size
seed count from the post-fix spread. This finally supplies the measured variance
§11.3 step 4 asked for in place of its placeholder 20, and it supersedes that
placeholder.

### 23.3 Blocking before a multi-day commitment

| | Why it blocks | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The retention objective** | Retention ranks on site-agnostic ipSAE. The decoy shows that is satisfiable without the specified site (§21.2), and §25 shows the designed chain reaching the right site in the wrong registry — including a ~170° flip that engages it with the fixed region — with an intact fold and a 36-residue interface. Scaling up optimizes harder toward a quantity that cannot see either failure. | Site-restricted confidence is a new metric; a registry term exists (§26) but is untested on a real model; pose-ranked retention is closer to existing code (§22.3) |
| **2. Resume** | §12.3: automatic resume is not implemented. At 300 calls a worker is ~45 min; at the contemplated scale it is hours, and a worker lost at hour 20 loses its whole trajectory. Eight workers compound that. | Checkpoint the population and archive per accepted candidate; the event log already records everything needed |
| **3. `target_entropy`** | 0.8 in the §21 configs against 0.6 in §§17.6 and 20.8. Nothing can be held constant across arms until this is pinned, and it already confounds §21.4's comparison against the 32-call batch. | Pick one, record it |
| **4. The two known defects** | `p17_rescore_winners.py` lacks `--target-sequence`, which voided the decoy's held-out stage (§21.7); `recommended_rung` is confidence-only and recommends rungs with no pose headroom (§21.7). | Small, both diagnosed |

### 23.4 Worth settling first, though not blocking

**The random-proposal arm (§21.8 item 1) is a 45%-of-budget decision.**
`gradient_seconds` is 1196 of 2664 s per worker. At scale that is the difference
between ~300 and ~550 evaluations per worker for the same wall-clock. Given
§21.5 and §22.5 — no discrimination among selected moves, and no reversion
found even with the answer five moves away — settling whether the gradient earns
that share before committing a multi-day run is worth one short run.

**Position-vs-identity (§22.5) suggests a cheaper intervention than any of the
above**, and it has published prior art — RSS selects the positions to change
from gradient norms and draws the replacement residue from a pretrained
sequence model's conditional distribution, with a Metropolis–Hastings
correction that keeps the composite move reversible
([note](literature_review/continuous_relaxation_and_the_discretization_gap.md)
Q4). AbLang2 is already loaded here and used only as a naturalness penalty at
weight 0.10; promoting it to the identity proposal is the same mechanism
without a relaxed representation, so it fits inside the existing harness.

If positional signal is reliable and substitution choice is not, then
enumerating all 19 substitutions at the top-k gradient-ranked positions is a
small, bounded experiment: the positions come from the existing saliency matrix,
and the cost is 19·k forward scores with no gradient calls at all. At k=5 that
is 95 evaluations, less than a third of one current worker's budget. It tests
directly whether the identity gap can be closed by brute force where the
gradient has already narrowed the position set.

**Not worth doing yet:** sweeping exploration temperatures (§20.8's second row).
With the retention objective mis-specified, that tunes how fast the search
reaches the wrong place.

### 23.5 The shape of the run, once the above is cleared

- JN.1 as the target, with a two-seed Alpha arm at identical settings (§23.1).
- Evaluation budget raised first; seed count sized from the resulting spread
  (§23.2).
- Site-specific retention, with raw ipSAE still recorded so results remain
  comparable to §§20–22 (§23.3 item 1).
- Held-out structural seeds kept disjoint from selection seeds, as every run
  since §15 has done.
- Pose, target fit, designed-chain-internal RMSD and interface counts all reported per
  candidate: §22.2 shows the first three are what localize a failure, and
  interface count is what distinguishes "no interface" from "wrong site".

A passing result would be the first evidence that this pipeline can place a
designed chain, not merely make the predictor confident about one. It would still not be
evidence of binding.

### 23.6 The pre-flight: four tests, two of them free

The large JN.1 run's main risk is not that it fails. It is that it succeeds at
optimizing a site-agnostic objective and so produces an expensive confirmation
of §21.2. The pre-flight below is chosen on one criterion: **each test must
change what the large run looks like, or it is not worth running first.**

The ordering principle is to try to falsify the plan cheaply. The plan is "put
a site gate in retention" (§§22.3, 24.7). The cheapest way to learn whether
that works is to apply the gate to the candidates already on disk.

#### Test A — retrospective site gate on every saved structure. No GPU.

> ✅ **Executed 2026-10-05; results in [§25](#25-test-a-executed-the-designs-are-at-the-site-and-the-failure-is-registry--2026-10-05).** Outcome: **neither branch below was
> right.** The designs are at the specified site, so the second branch ("the
> gate is a no-op because the search never generates on-site candidates") is
> wrong on its stated reason. But the gate is a no-op anyway, for the opposite
> reason: on the runs §§21–22 quote it passes 12/12 non-WT, because the site
> *and* the contact face are both already correct and the residual error is
> **registry** (§25.4). What the test actually redirected the run toward is the
> pairwise registry restraint (§26), not a selection gate. The confirmation on
> the followups archive remains outstanding (§25.6).

Every run since §15 saves `structures/candidate_NNNNN/seed_N.pdb` and the
matching confidence arrays, and across §§20–22 that is on the order of a
thousand scored candidates with their predicted complexes. Compute Germinal's
gate on each one offline: `binder_near_hotspot` at 5.3 Å, site contacts at
6.0 Å with a ≥3 minimum, and `percent_interface_cdr`, plus the set-overlap
measures `EpiRecall = |P ∩ E|/|E|` and `EpiPrecision = |P ∩ E|/|P|` at a
declared 4 Å heavy-atom cutoff.

What it decides, and this is the point of running it first:

- **If a usable fraction passes**, a retention gate is immediately actionable —
  the search already produces on-site candidates and is simply not selecting
  them. The large run gets the gate and little else changes.
- **If essentially none passes**, the gate is a no-op: it cannot select what
  the search never generates. The large run would then need an intervention
  *upstream* of selection — the gradient-side reduction flip and off-site
  repulsion (§24.7 steps 2–3), or the input-space route the review favours
  (site-only target, or lysinating the non-specified site surface), which makes an
  off-site pose geometrically unavailable rather than merely unrewarded.

Either outcome redirects the run. No other test here has that property at zero
compute cost.

#### Test B — where the gradient ranked the right answer. No GPU.

`tables/saliency.csv` records, per gradient call, a first-order `delta_loss`
and its `rank` for every feasible (position, residue) pair. On the 5-edit Alpha
rung the correct answer is known — five specific reversions. Reading off the
rank the correct residue received at each damaged position converts §22.5's
"the gradient did not pick it" into "the gradient ranked it *N*th of ~551."

That distinction decides whether the identity gap is a **ranking** problem,
which proposal temperature or top-k enumeration could address, or an **absence
of signal**, which they cannot. §23.4's cheap intervention — enumerate all 19
substitutions at the top-k gradient-ranked positions, 95 forward scores at
k=5 — is worth building only under the first reading. The archive is on the
cluster; nothing needs recomputing.

#### Test C — one short batch, three measurements that share it

Eight workers, roughly an hour, all at the settings the large run will use:

| | Decides | State |
|---|---|---|
| **Per-term gradient norms and pairwise cosines** | Whether anything is being overpowered or opposed before two mutually opposed terms are added (§24.5). A few gradient calls; the machinery exists in `p17_pose_diagnostics.py` | ❌ **still unrun, and now blocking** — §27.5 shows the registry null is uninterpretable without it |
| **Random-proposal arm at matched wall-clock** | How the large run splits its budget. `gradient_seconds` is 1196 of 2664 s per worker, so this is ~300 against ~550 evaluations for the same time (§21.8) | ❌ unrun |
| **Independent against population, 5-edit Alpha rung** | Which policy the large run uses. Both existing contrasts are void or trivial (§24.9) | ✅ **answered on JN.1 instead** — null at 10 seeds (§27.2). Not the Alpha rung this row asked for, so the known-answer version is still open |

⚠️ **The order was not followed.** The overnight screen of 2026-10-06 ran a
three-factor comparison *before* this batch, which is why its registry null
cannot be read (§27.5) and why its policy result arrived on JN.1 rather than on
the rung with a known answer. The first row above is now the single highest-value
unrun measurement in the project.

#### Test D — alpha5 at 300 calls

The only interpretable arm, re-run at the large run's budget. It separates
"pose is call-limited" from "pose is gradient-limited" on a problem whose
solution provably sits inside the feasible set, and it doubles as the
calibration for the two-seed Alpha anchor §23.1 requires alongside the JN.1
run. Without it, a JN.1 null at scale is the same uninterpretable outcome this
project has already paid for twice.

#### What is hygiene rather than a test

Not experiments, but they will corrupt or waste the large run if left: resume
(§23.3 item 2), `target_entropy` pinned at one value, the rescorer's
`--target-sequence` (absent, which voided the decoy's held-out stage), and the
pose-blind `recommended_rung`. Clear these in the same pass.

#### Recommended order

A and B first, because they are free and A can redirect everything after it.
Then the hygiene items. Then C. Then D. Then the large JN.1 run with a two-seed
Alpha arm carried alongside, at whatever objective and policy A–D selected.

A passing pre-flight establishes that the large run measures the intended
quantity. It does not establish that the gradient can place a designed chain, which is
what the large run is for.

## 24. Should the search policy be polished against Germinal? — 2026-10-05

The question raised after the literature review: the search policy may need
polishing, taking Germinal and comparable pipelines as the reference. This
section answers it from the measurements already in §§20–23 and from the
[commissioned review](literature_review/README.md).

**Short answer: not yet, and not against Germinal's policy — because Germinal's
advantage is not in its search policy.** Sequence objective and selection
first, policy *attribution* second, policy sophistication third. The reasoning
follows; §24.7 is the ordering.

### 24.1 What Germinal actually differs by

Set side by side at the level of mechanism rather than outcome:

| Component | This project | Published Germinal |
|---|---|---|
| Gradient-time site term | `BinderTargetContact`, weight **0.5**, **8 Å** cutoff, specified-site columns restricted | anchor residue-masked AfDesign `i_con`, weight **0.2**, **20 Å** cutoff, `inter_contact_number` 10 |
| Objective composition | weighted scalar sum + `ClippedGradient` | normalized gradients; weighted, PCGrad or MGDA merging |
| Repulsive term | none | `L_paratope ∝ L_CDR − λ·L_framework` (contact face side) |
| Representation | discrete moves from a fixed start | continuous relaxation then discrete refinement |
| Search memory | population with local competition | staged trajectories plus downstream filtering |
| **Selection** | **mean min-directional ipSAE, site-agnostic** | **five filters, three site/contact face-specific, and no confidence metric at all** |

On the gradient-term axis **ours is stronger and sharper** — weight 0.5 against
0.2, an 8 Å cutoff against 20 Å. On representation, §5 shows the older pipeline
already has continuous relaxation followed by discrete search. On search
memory, §21.6 shows our population behaving as intended.

The decisive difference is the last row. Germinal's initial filter
(`configs/filter/initial/vhh.yaml`) is `clashes < 1`, `binder_near_hotspot ==
true`, `cdr3_hotspot_contacts > 0`, `percent_interface_cdr > 0.5`,
`interface_shape_comp > 0.6` — and it **computes `ipsae` while declining to
filter on it**, treating confidence and site as separate, non-substitutable
axes. A candidate that misses the site dies at that gate regardless of its
confidence.

⚠️ **Qualified by [§25.4](#254-what-this-means-the-gap-is-registry-not-location).** That is the right description of the gate, but it
is not a remedy here: our candidates do **not** miss the site, so on the runs
§§21–22 quote this gate passes 12/12 non-WT. A gate on aggregate proximity and
contact-face share cannot discriminate when the site and the face are both
already correct, which is why §25.4 moves the registry restraint (§26) ahead
of it.

**So "refer to Germinal" has a precise answer, and it is about selection, not
search.** Polishing the policy against Germinal would copy the component that
is not carrying its result.

### 24.2 Why the policy is not the binding constraint

Three measurements already in this document point the same way.

**The population is doing its job.** §21.6: final active parents sit 6–10
Hamming apart out of a maximum of 10 under a five-edit budget — near-disjoint
edit sets rather than near-duplicates — and the winning lineage crossed three
or four parent slots in three of four seeds. That is §10.2's policy-1
hypothesis behaving as designed.

**The search is competent at the thing it is being asked for.** §22.2: the
designed chain folds correctly (independently aligned RMSD 1.4–3.7 Å), the target folds
and superimposes (1.76–1.94 Å), and a 34–50 residue interface forms against 2/1
at WT. The search finds good interfaces. It finds them in the wrong place.

**Nothing in the loop asks for the right place.** §22.3: retention ranks on
site-agnostic confidence; the site term lives only in the proposal. A better
search under that retention metric locates higher-confidence off-site poses
*faster*. This is the same argument §23.3 makes against raising the evaluation
budget before fixing the objective, and it applies to policy sophistication
without modification.

To that the review adds a mechanistic reason the metric cannot be rescued by
tuning: ipSAE's pair set is
`outer(chains==c1, chains==c2) & (PAE < pae_cutoff)`, which never references
the intended site. **No reduction over that set can be made site-aware**, which
is why the two hardenings already applied here — mean rather than `max_i`, min
rather than max over directions, both stricter than published ipSAE — changed
nothing.

### 24.3 The one policy-layer mechanism the literature supports

Of the review's four layers — conditioning, gradient-time loss, in-trajectory
rejection, selection filter — exactly one is a *search-policy* intervention
rather than an objective or a filter: **in-trajectory rejection**, and it exists
in only one pipeline.

RFantibody's `terminate_bad_targeting` computes, mid-generation on the denoised
structure, the Cβ distance from every specified residue to every designed loop
residue, takes the per-residue minimum, and if the closest specified residue is
further than **10 Å** from any designed loop it discards the trajectory and
reseeds; after **20** such failures it aborts the input specification as
badly posed. We have no analogue: a trajectory that wanders off-site runs to
its call ceiling and then competes on confidence.

Two caveats worth carrying. It ships **off by default**, and the authors still
report that "the lack of an effective filter is the main limitation of the
RFantibody pipeline at the moment" — so it is a real mechanism, not a solved
problem. And no paper states which timestep the published campaigns evaluated
it at.

This is the item to reach for *if* §24.7's steps 1–3 do not resolve placement,
because it acts on the trajectory rather than on the objective.

### 24.4 Policy work that is measurement, not polish

Two arms have never run, and both are about attribution rather than
improvement. Until they do, polishing the policy means improving a component
whose contribution is unmeasured **in both directions**.

- **Random-proposal arm** (§21.8 item 1). Same population policy, same
  retention, proposals drawn uniformly from feasible moves, matched on
  wall-clock. §21.5's conditional-versus-base-rate statistic cannot reach this
  question — it is computed within the pool the gradient already selected — so
  this is the only experiment that attributes any of the result to the
  gradient. It is also a 45%-of-wall-clock decision.
- **Independent versus population** (§10.7). Never executed; every arm in
  §§21–22 is `population`. §21.6 was corrected precisely because diverse
  parents are observed behavior rather than measured contribution.

### 24.5 Gradient merging: more important after the objective changes, with a cheap prerequisite

This is the one genuine policy item where Germinal is the right reference, and
it has been open since §10.5: our composition is a **weighted scalar sum with
`ClippedGradient`**, and clipping does not resolve opposing gradient
directions. Germinal normalizes per-term gradients and merges them with
weighted, PCGrad or MGDA schemes.

It matters *more* after the objective changes, not before. §14's composite
already carries seven terms. Adding a site term and an off-site repulsion term
(§24.7 steps 1 and 3) takes that to nine, with the two newest in **direct
opposition by construction** — attract here, repel there. A weighted scalar sum
under a single clip is exactly where that conflict gets absorbed, and §17.2
already warns that multiplying a coefficient can be absorbed by clipping or by
a compensating temperature change.

**The prerequisite is cheap and has never been run.** §10.5 asked for it in
September: measure the individual gradient norms and their alignment before
attributing anything to one term overpowering another. The machinery exists —
`examples/p17_pose_diagnostics.py` already computes `cdr_gradient_norms` and
`on_off_cosine` for the pose-on/pose-off pair. Extending it to report a norm
per term and the pairwise cosines across all terms is a small change, and it
answers whether anything is actually being overpowered or opposed rather than
assuming it. Do that before adopting any merging scheme.

### 24.6 What not to do

- **Do not adopt Germinal's representation** (continuous relaxation then
  discrete refinement) as a placement fix. §5 shows the older pipeline already
  has it; the confidence harness deliberately starts from a fixed sequence; and
  nothing connects representation to the placement failure. §22.5's result —
  the known answer sitting on the feasible-set boundary, reachable only by one
  exact path — is not a representation problem.

  > ➕ **Scope of that "no", clarified 2026-10-07.** It stands as written: no
  > evidence connects the reparameterization to placement, so a relaxed stage is
  > not a registry fix. A **separate** case for a relaxed stage — about *residue
  > identity* selection and about traversing the edit budget (§23.2), not about
  > placement — is reviewed in
  > [`literature_review/continuous_relaxation_and_the_discretization_gap.md`](literature_review/continuous_relaxation_and_the_discretization_gap.md).
  > That note also records why it cannot simply be adopted as built: every
  > surveyed relaxed method initializes from noise and carries no distance
  > constraint, because all of them design from scratch, and the one measured
  > fact about rounding a relaxed optimum is that it does not survive the round
  > trip without a separate repair stage.
- **Do not sweep exploration temperatures yet** (§20.8's second row, and
  `target_entropy` still inconsistent at 0.8 versus 0.6 per §21.7). With the
  retention objective mis-specified this tunes how fast the search reaches the
  wrong place. Pin the value, then sweep after step 1.
- **Do not raise the edit budget** as a policy change. §22.1 is direct evidence
  against it: five edits sufficed, were inside the budget, and were not found.

### 24.7 Recommended sequence

> ⚠️ **Re-ranked by [§25.4](#254-what-this-means-the-gap-is-registry-not-location) (2026-10-05); the numbering below is kept so the
> cross-references elsewhere still resolve.** Test A measured the residual
> failure as *registry at the correct site*, not absence from the site, so
> **step 2 (the registry restraint) now leads** and step 1 drops: the site gate
> passes 12/12 non-WT on the runs §§21–22 quote. Step 2b likewise addresses
> site *coverage*, which §25.1 shows is largely already achieved. Current

|---|---|---|---|

at all.

### 24.8 The counter-argument to this section

Stated so it is not lost: **the search genuinely is underpowered.** §23.2 shows
four Alpha seeds spending 1, 3, 5 and 3 edits of a five-edit budget across 32
evaluations — a search that cannot traverse its own budget is characterizing the
neighbourhood of its start. §21.4 shows the JN.1 arms gaining 0.2–0.5 ipSAE when
the ceiling rose from 32 to 300.

That is a real limitation, and it is an argument for **evaluation budget**,
which §23.2 already ranks ahead of seed count. It is not an argument for a more
sophisticated policy: more evaluations under a site-agnostic retention metric
buy confidence rather than placement, which §21.4 measured directly — 268
additional evaluations bought roughly 1 Å of pose and 0.43 of ipSAE.

### 24.9 Is population the right default for the proof of concept?

§17's decision (2026-09-30) made population "the provisional policy for the
next controlled experiment" and every arm in §§21–22 is population. The MD has
not stated what the evidence for that default actually is. The ledger:

| Evidence | Status |
|---|---|
| §17.1 pilot: population 0.1351 against independent 0.0304 mean best selection ipSAE, four seeds per arm | **Voided.** §17.10 found 14.12–19.81 Å median adjacent-Cα distances across the archives those sequences came from |
| §20: Alpha 2-edit rung, both population runs outscoring both independent runs, 0.835/0.801 against 0.702/0.687, no overlap, valid geometry | Stands, but **two seeds per arm**, and §20 itself establishes the rung was a one-edit problem |
| The only candidate that reverted the lethal substitution came from `population_seed0` | Stands. §22.5's trajectory check confirms it independently: that run proposed `G100 → G` three times and accepted all three, while the two independent runs touched damaged positions 6 and 9 times and never proposed the correct residue |

So: two directionally consistent contrasts, one void on geometry and one on a
problem subsequently shown to be trivially easy, and **neither is a
matched-compute policy comparison.** §20's own verdict — "establishes nothing
about policy" — is correct, and §10.7's policy-1-versus-policy-2 comparison has
still never been executed.

The third row is the one that carries weight, for a reason that is mechanistic
rather than statistical. **The only occasion on which anything in this project
proposed a correct residue, it came from a population run**, and that is
precisely what local competition is supposed to buy: a lineage that would have
been discarded under single-trajectory selection survived long enough to find
it. One observation, on the easy rung — but it is the only data point on the
capability §22.5 identifies as the actual gap.

**Decision: retain population as the default, stop describing it as settled,
and run independent as a matched control on the 5-edit rung.** Both existing
contrasts are on void or trivial problems; there is no comparison at all on a
rung where both axes have room. That control also discharges §10.7.

> ✅ **Run 2026-10-06; the control came back null, and the decision reverses.
> See [§27.2](#272-the-three-contrasts-all-inside-the-screens-own-power).** At
> matched compute — 10 seeds, 200 score calls, `target_entropy` and

> ipSAE against population's 0.529** and **27.4 Å mean pose against 30.5 Å**,
> both differences inside the screen's own ~0.11 ipSAE / ~6 Å sensitivity.
> §10.7's comparison is discharged with no detectable difference.
>
> That removes the aggregate case for population and leaves the
> proof-of-concept argument in the closing paragraph below standing: it is the
> simpler configuration, its extra machinery has unmeasured contribution, and
> nothing measurable is lost by dropping it. **The default should now be
> independent** (§27.7 item 3). The single mechanistic observation in the third
> row of the ledger — that the only correct residue ever proposed in this
> project came from a population run — is unaffected by this screen and stays
> on the record as the one reason to revisit.

⚠️ **The proof-of-concept argument against this default, stated rather than
buried.** A proof of concept wants the simplest configuration that can
demonstrate its claim, and population adds local competition, Hamming-distance
niching, elite protection and duplicate-slot filling whose contribution is
unmeasured (§21.6). A success under that configuration cannot be attributed to
the gradient, and a failure acquires an extra component to blame. §10.2 itself
calls independent search the "essential simple reference." That argument loses
here only because the single success came from population — which is a weak
reason, and would be overturned by the matched control going the other way.

## 25. Test A executed: the designs are at the site, and the failure is registry — 2026-10-05

§23.6's Test A ran. It applies Germinal's published site criteria offline to
predicted complexes already on disk — 854 structures across the local archives,
**no GPU and no new predictions**. The script was prototyped as
`scratchpad/site_gate.py` and now lives at `examples/p17_site_gate_audit.py`;
per-structure output is `site_gate.csv`.

It was chosen because it could falsify the plan at zero cost. It did, twice,
and the corrected plan is narrower than the original.

### 25.1 The designs are at the specified site, not away from it

**§22.2's reading — "docked 14–27 Å from the reference site" — is wrong in
detail, and this section supersedes it.** Across the JN.1 held-out predictions
with structures available locally:

| | pose RMSD | EpiRecall | closest approach to specified site |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| WT (damaged start) | 47.4 Å | 0.067 | 14.0 Å |
| non-WT designs | 20.7 Å | **0.642** | **2.7 Å** |

`corr(pose RMSD, EpiRecall) = −0.881` and `corr(pose RMSD, closest approach) =
+0.897`. Pose RMSD does track site agreement — but the designs are **contacting
the specified site**, at a mean closest approach of 2.7 Å, while still scoring
20 Å of target-aligned designed-chain RMSD.

That is possible because a 123-residue domain can touch a 5-residue specified site with
the wrong part of itself. Decomposing the residual rigid motion after target
alignment into rotation and centroid shift separates two modes:

| mode | n | rotation | centroid shift | contact-face–site contacts | % interface on contact face |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **framework-mediated** | 9 | **168–171°** | 9–11 Å | **0** | 0.09–0.13 |
| **contact-face-mediated, displaced** | 14 | 12–21° | 13–19 Å | 3–4 | 0.50–0.89 |
| WT | 3 | 131–169° | 33–50 Å | 0 | — |

A ~170° rotation with a 10 Å centroid shift is the designed chain docked **upside-down**
at the specified site, engaging it with the fixed region rather than the contact face. The literature has
a name for this and it is not "wrong site": Germinal calls it a
**framework-mediated interaction** and classifies it as a design failure, and
the unverified RFantibody lead in
[the review](literature_review/README.md)
describes the same thing. So §21.2's framing of a site-agnostic objective is
right, but the geometry it permits is a flip at the correct site, not a
migration to a different one.

### 25.2 On this archive the gate separates the modes, and only its contact-face criteria do the work

> ⚠️ **Read [§25.4](#254-what-this-means-the-gap-is-registry-not-location)'s
> closing paragraph with this subsection.** What follows held on the one
> archive available when it was written. On the runs §§21–22 actually quote,
> the same full gate passes **12/12** non-WT and cannot reject two of three
> damaged Alpha starts, so it is **not** the actionable change this subsection
> concludes it is. The conclusion is retained because the mode separation it
> measures is real and is what identified the flip.

⚠️ **The first version of this test was incomplete and would have shipped a
useless conclusion.** It implemented two of Germinal's three site criteria —
proximity at 5.3 Å and site contacts at 6.0 Å with a ≥3 minimum — and
**23 of 24** non-WT predictions passed. On that basis a retention gate looks
like a no-op. The omitted criterion was `percent_interface_cdr > 0.5`, which
exists precisely to catch a designed chain touching the right site with the wrong face.

With the contact-face criteria included:

| gate | non-WT passing | WT passing |
|---|---:|---:|
| proximity + site contacts (incomplete) | 23/24 | — |
| **+ `cdr3_hotspot_contacts > 0` + `percent_interface_cdr > 0.5`** | **13/24** | **0/3** |

The full gate rejects precisely the nine framework-mediated predictions and
retains the contact-face-mediated ones, and it rejects WT. **So a retention gate is
immediately actionable** — the search does produce candidates worth keeping, and
nothing currently selects them. But the load-bearing criterion is the
designable-restricted one, not site proximity, which is the opposite of what a
proximity-first reading of §22.3 would have built.

### 25.3 Correction to §21.4: the retention metric is not choosing the flip

§21.4 inferred from the anti-correlation between ipSAE and pose that retention
"actively preferred the worse pose." On this evidence it does not prefer the
framework-mediated mode: `corr(% interface CDR, ipSAE) = +0.523`, and all five
top-ipSAE candidates are contact-face-mediated, at 0.59–0.86. **ipSAE mildly favours the
correct binding mode.**

What it fails to do is discriminate pose *within* that mode. Its top picks sit
at 19.2–26.3 Å while the best available candidate in the same run is at 13.0 Å.
That is a weaker and more specific indictment than §21.4's, and it relocates the
problem.

### 25.4 What this means: the gap is registry, not location

Chaining §§25.1–25.3: the search reaches the intended site, roughly half of its
output engages that site with the contact face, a gate can separate that half from
the flipped half, and within the correct half the remaining error is 12–21° of
rotation and 13–19 Å of offset. **Right site, right face, wrong registry.**

Nothing in the pipeline specifies registry. A site gate asserts that designable regions
contact the specified site; it does not assert *which* designable-region residue pairs with *which*
specified-site residue. That is the **pairwise site-by-contact-face distogram restraint**
the review recommended on prior-art grounds — Germinal's BCE over named contact
pairs, EasyNano's per-designable region-residue ELU at an 8 Å set-point — and it now has a
measured justification in this project's own data rather than a borrowed one.

Re-ranking §24.7 accordingly: the **pairwise registry restraint** moves ahead
of the contact-term reduction flip, because the flip addresses site *coverage*
and the measured residual is registry. It is implemented in
[§26](#26-the-registry-restraint-a-gradient-term-that-can-see-a-flip--2026-10-05).

⚠️ **And the site gate drops.** §25.2's conclusion that the gate separates the
modes held only on the one archive available when that subsection was written.
On the runs §§21–22 actually quote it passes **12/12 non-WT in both JN.1 arms**
and cannot reject two of three damaged Alpha starts, because the flipped
candidates there retain 56–76% contact-face interface and so satisfy the contact-face criteria
too. A gate cannot discriminate when the site *and* the face are both correct.

### 25.5 The Alpha archives: no flip mode, and the 2-edit rung confirmed uninformative about pose

The same analysis on the local Alpha runs, 15 held-out predictions:

| | pose RMSD | rotation | centroid shift | % interface on contact face | ipSAE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| non-WT, all 12 | 1.6–3.9 Å | **4–11°** | 0.6–3.0 Å | 0.67–0.82 | 0.68–0.84 |
| WT (damaged) | 1.9–15.3 Å | 7–92° | 0.9–4.7 Å | 0.68–0.74 | 0.000 |

Two findings. **The framework-mediated mode does not appear at all** on Alpha —
zero of twelve below the 20% designable region threshold, mean rotation 8°, mean centroid
shift 1.8 Å. In this sample the flip is specific to the JN.1 case, where no
solution is known to exist and the search is hunting, rather than a general
pathology of the pipeline.

And **the 2-edit rung had no pose headroom whatsoever**, which independently
confirms §20.3 and §22.1's reasoning for moving to 5 edits: the damaged start
already sat at 1.9–4.0 Å pose with ipSAE 0.000, so that rung only ever tested
confidence recovery. The full gate passes 12/12 non-WT *and* 3/3 WT there,
because WT is at the reference pose — a gate cannot discriminate on a problem
where everything is already on-site.

### 25.6 Limits, and what would confirm this

- **One JN.1 run.** 24 non-WT held-out predictions, 8 candidates × 3 seeds, from
  `p17_jn1_recovery_20261005_004100_3539586`. This is **not** the followups run
  whose numbers §§21–22 quote; that archive's structures are not local, the
  review bundle having excluded them. Re-running this analysis on
  `p17_followup.tar.gz` is the confirmation step, and it needs no GPU.
- **The 5-edit Alpha rung is unchecked.** It is the one rung where both axes
  have room and a solution provably exists, so it is where the flip mode would
  matter most. Its archive is on the cluster.
- **Site definition.** JN.1 uses the five literature anchor residues; Alpha uses an
  8 Å contact-derived specified site of 18 residues. A different definition changes the
  counts, and the JN.1 recall denominator of 5 makes EpiRecall coarse.
- **The 170°-versus-15° split is clean in this sample but rests on eight JN.1
  candidates.** `corr(% interface CDR, ipSAE)` is `+0.523` on JN.1 and `−0.644`
  on Alpha, where all twelve points sit inside a narrow band — the Alpha figure
  should be read as noise, not as a contradiction.
- Nothing here establishes binding, and a gate that retains 13 of 24 candidates
  establishes only that the modes are separable, not that the retained half is
  correct.

## 26. The registry restraint: a gradient term that can see a flip — 2026-10-05

§25 localized the remaining pose failure to **orientation at the correct
site**: the designed chain reaches the specified site, presents its designable regions, and is
rotated. Nothing in the objective could distinguish that from the reference,
because every site criterion in the pipeline and in the literature reduces over
*aggregate proximity*, which a flip satisfies. This section adds a term that
does not, and records what it is and is not known to do.

### 26.1 Why aggregate proximity cannot see the failure

`BinderTargetContact` (`src/mosaic/losses/structure_prediction.py:303-340`)
restricts the distogram to specified-site columns, takes each designed-chain residue's top-3
specified site partners, and averages over designed-chain residues. The question it asks is
*"is residue i near any specified-site residue"*. A designed chain flipped at the correct
specified site answers yes: its designable-region residues are still near specified-site residues, merely
the wrong ones. §25 measured candidates rotated 156–178° carrying 56–76% designable region
interface and passing Germinal's full site gate 12/12.

The same holds for every published site mechanism in
[the review](literature_review/README.md):
RFantibody's 10 Å abort, Boltz-2's `pocket: max_distance`, BindCraft's
anchor residue-masked `i_con`, EasyNano's 8 Å ELU — all are min-distance or
proximity-reduced, and a flip preserves the quantity each measures. **Site
specification and registry specification are different objectives, and the
literature only implements the first.**

### 26.2 What was added

**`BinderTargetRegistry`** scores the *named* pairs that are in contact in the
reference:

```
loss = -mean over (i,j) in reference_contacts of log P(d_ij < cutoff)
```

Exchanging partners breaks it, which is what makes it orientation-sensitive
rather than only proximity-sensitive. It operates on the **distogram**, not on
coordinates: §25 and the review together establish that no published work
optimizes sequence through an inter-chain coordinate RMSD, that every pipeline
specifying placement does so in distogram or contact space, and that this
project's own coordinate pose term is measurably inert (§21.3).

`reference_contact_pairs()` extracts the pair set, restrictable to the
designable designed chain positions and to the specified site, and returns an empty array
rather than a degenerate objective when the reference has no contacts at the
chosen cutoff — which the builder then refuses.

An optional `repel_pairs`/`repel_weight` carries the complementary half:
specified pairs that must *not* form, for instance the partners a flip creates.
Germinal implements the contact face-side analogue (`L_CDR − λ·L_framework`); **no
published pipeline implements the target-side form**. Off by default.

Wiring: `build_composite_losses` gains `registry_weight`,
`registry_repel_weight` and `registry_contact_distance`, all defaulting to
off/`CONTACT_DISTANCE`, so every existing caller's objective is unchanged.
`p17_confidence_search.py` exposes `--weight-registry` and
`--registry-contact-distance`, records both in run metadata, and passes them
through `proposal_kwargs` so they stay **constant across the pose-weight
toggle** — the paired pose-on/pose-off diagnostic therefore still varies only
what it is meant to vary.

### 26.3 Measured on the real reference

The premise is that the existing term is flip-blind, and testing it required
getting the flip geometry right. A first attempt tumbled the designed chain so that
the contact face pointed away from the target; both terms penalized that heavily
(registry +10484, aggregate +8420), because it is not the observed failure.
Modelling the flips §25 actually measured — designable regions retained at the specified site — on
`P17_JN1.pdb` with its 44 designable-restricted reference contacts at 8 Å:

| 180° flip, contact face still engaged | centroid shift | contact-face–site min | **registry Δ** | aggregate Δ | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| spin about the approach axis through the contact face | 18.1 Å | 2.6 Å | **+1100.6** | +333.6 | 3.3× |
| **tumble through the contact face** | 24.3 Å | 2.7 Å | **+1815.0** | **+39.0** | **47×** |
| tumble through the centroid (contact face turned away) | 0.0 Å | 22.6 Å | +10484 | +8420 | 1.2× |

The second row is the closest analogue to the observed geometry, and the
aggregate term is effectively blind to it.

A second property emerged that was not designed for. **At the reference the
registry term scores 0.098 and the aggregate term scores 616.9.** The aggregate
term asks every designable-region residue to sit near one of five specified-site residues, which the
real crystal structure does not satisfy, so most of its magnitude is
irreducible and its dynamic range is compressed. The registry term is ~0 at the
reference by construction, so its value reads directly as distance from
reference registry — which also makes it usable as a reported diagnostic, not
only as an objective.

### 26.4 Validation, and its limits

`tests/test_registry_loss.py`, **10 tests, all CPU, no checkpoint**:

- a flip that preserves every pairwise *distance* while exchanging partners is
  penalized, and the aggregate term is confirmed nearly invariant to the same
  flip — the premise pinned as a test rather than asserted;
- translating the designed chain away is monotonically worse;
- the repel half is inert at weight zero, costs ~nothing on the reference pose
  where the forbidden pairs are 36 Å apart, and bites on the flipped pose;
- gradients are finite and nonzero with respect to the distogram;
- subset restriction and the empty-pair-set case;
- two real-reference tests: registry ~0 at the reference, and registry more
  sensitive than the aggregate term on both contact face-preserving flips.

Full suite after the change: **435 passed, 1 skipped**. Ruff clean on the
loss module, both examples and the new tests.

⚠️ **Two defects of mine, caught by these tests and recorded rather than
quietly fixed.** The repel term initially *added a log-probability*, which is
large and negative for a non-contacting pair — so it rewarded exactly the pairs
it was meant to discourage, and was unbounded below. It now penalizes contact
*probability*, bounded in [0, 1]. Separately, two test fixtures were
geometrically wrong: one shifted the designed chain *through* the target, so "4 Å away"
was a 2 Å approach, and one asserted a repulsion penalty on pairs 36 Å apart.
The loss was correct in both cases and the fixtures were not; both are
documented in the test file so the distinction is not lost.

⚠️ **What has not been tested: anything with a real model.** Every test above
uses synthetic distograms built from explicit coordinates. The term has had no
full-model forward or backward pass, so its gradient norm relative to the other
seven composite terms under `ClippedGradient` is unmeasured, and so is its
interaction with the entropy-recalibrated proposal temperature. §17.2's warning
applies directly: a logged nonzero scalar does not prove useful sequence
guidance, and multiplying a coefficient can be absorbed by clipping or by a
compensating temperature change.

> ⚠️ **Superseded in part 2026-10-06 by [§27](#27-the-first-real-model-test-of-the-registry-term-and-three-null-effects--2026-10-06).** The term has now run against a real
> model, at weight 0.5, in a 10-seed factorial. It was **live and sensitive** —
> logged in every `_on` cell and absent from every `_off` cell, reading a loss
> near 5.15 against 0.098 at the reference — and its effect on confidence was
> **null** (+0.072 ipSAE at budget 5, −0.020 at budget 7, both inside the
> screen's detection threshold). **The gradient-norm measurement this paragraph
> asks for was still not made**, so §27.5 cannot distinguish the term not
> helping from its gradient being absorbed. The warning above therefore stands
> unchanged, and it is now blocking the interpretation of a completed run
> rather than a future one.

### 26.5 Before the large run

> ⚠️ **This was not done, and the screen that followed it shows why it
> mattered (2026-10-06).** `p17_overnight_screen_20261006_055001` went
> straight to a `--weight-registry 0.5` comparison across 10 seeds without
> either measurement below — exactly what the closing paragraph of this
> subsection advised against. The result is a null that cannot be read
> (§27.5). Both items remain outstanding and are now **item 1 of §27.7**,
> promoted from prerequisite to the thing that decides whether an existing
> result means anything.

One short batch, not a long one. It answers two questions at once and both are
prerequisites rather than experiments:

1. **Per-term gradient norms and pairwise cosines** with the registry term
   active — the measurement §10.5 asked for in September and §24.5 restated,
   now with a concrete reason: the registry and the off-site repulsion oppose
   each other by construction, and a single clipped weighted sum is where such
   a conflict disappears. `examples/p17_pose_diagnostics.py` already computes
   `cdr_gradient_norms` and `on_off_cosine` for one pair; extending it per term
   is small.
2. **Whether the registry term's gradient survives clipping** at a candidate
   weight. If it does not, the weight is not the lever (§20.10's corrected
   analysis) and the composition has to change first.

A sweep of `--weight-registry` is deliberately **not** proposed ahead of that
measurement: §§21.3 and 24.5 both show that weight sweeps on a term whose
gradient is being absorbed measure nothing.

What this section does **not** establish: that the registry restraint improves
pose. It establishes that a term now exists which can distinguish the geometry
§25 identified, that it is 3–47× more sensitive to it than what the pipeline
had, and that it is off unless asked for. Whether following it moves a real
prediction is the question the next run answers.

## 27. The first real-model test of the registry term, and three null effects — 2026-10-06

Run `p17_overnight_screen_20261006_055001`, launched by
`examples/run_p17_overnight_screen.sh`. This is the first time the registry
restraint of [§26](#26-the-registry-restraint-a-gradient-term-that-can-see-a-flip--2026-10-05) met a real model, and it is also the first
matched-compute comparison of the population and independent policies and of
the 5- versus 7-edit budget. It supersedes §26.4's "no real-model test yet",
§24.9's open policy question and §7's open budget decision.

A factorial over three factors — registry weight (0 or 0.5), edit budget
(5 or 7), policy (population or independent) — at **10 search seeds per cell**
and **200 score calls per seed**, 64 sampling steps, bf16, stable aggregation,
held-out structural seeds 101/102/103. `target_entropy` 0.6 and
`acceptance_temperature` 0.02 were **pinned across every cell**, which is
§21.7's requirement being met for the first time. The plan records its own
detectable effect size: **~0.11 ipSAE and ~6 Å pose** at this seed count
(§23.2).

**Headline: all three factors come back inside that detection threshold on
confidence.** The registry term does not move ipSAE, the policy choice does not
move ipSAE, and the edit budget does not move it consistently. The one effect
that clears the pose bar does not replicate across budgets. Meanwhile every one
of the 165 scored structures is CAPRI `incorrect`, and raising the budget from
5 to 7 measurably **increases** the rate of upside-down docking. Nothing here
rescues §26's premise and nothing here refutes it either, for a reason that is
a sequencing error rather than a result (§27.5).

### 27.1 What ran, and what did not

| cell | registry | budget | policy | state |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| `pop_off5` | 0.0 | 5 | population | ✅ complete |
| `pop_on5` | 0.5 | 5 | population | ✅ complete |
| `pop_off7` | 0.0 | 7 | population | ✅ complete |
| `pop_on7` | 0.5 | 7 | population | ✅ complete |
| `indep_on5` | 0.5 | 5 | independent | ✅ complete |
| `apgm_on5` | 0.5 | 5 | population + `simplex_APGM` seeding | ❌ failed |
| `bc_on5` | 0.5 | 5 | population + 4-stage anneal seeding | ❌ failed |
| `bc_on7` | 0.5 | 7 | population + 4-stage anneal seeding | ❌ failed |

The three seeding cells died **after completing all 125 optimization steps**,
on a return-arity defect: without a `trajectory_fn`, `bindcraft_design` and
`colabdesign_stage` return a bare `(N, 20)` array while `simplex_APGM` returns
`(x, best_x)`, so unpacking as a 2-tuple raised "too many values to unpack".
The fix and a shape assertion are in `examples/p17_continuous_seed.py`. **So
continuous seeding remains untested against a real model**, and the three
cells that would have tested it produced no scored candidates. That is a lost
arm, not a negative result about seeding.

All five completed cells reached **200 of 200 score calls**. §21.4 found every
seed taking its largest step between 100 and 250 evaluations, so 200 sits
inside the band where the search was still improving. **This screen is cut off
in the same way the 32-call batch was** (§20.8), at a higher ceiling.

### 27.5 The registry term was live, and the measurement that would interpret its null was skipped

Before reading the null as "the term does not work", two checks.

**The toggle worked.** `registry_log_p` appears in the `proposal_metrics`
events of every `_on` cell (535–599 logged values per cell) and is **absent
from both `_off` cells**. So the term was wired, evaluated and recorded, and
the comparison is not a plumbing artifact.

**The term was also reading the geometry it was built to read.** Logged
`registry_log_p` runs −3.1 to −9.4, mean −5.15, so the loss `−mean log P` sits
near **5.15 against §26.3's 0.098 at the reference** — roughly 50× its
reference value. The term is not saturated and not inert; it is correctly
reporting that these poses are far from reference registry.

**But its share of the composite is small.** Multiplying each logged scalar by
its weight (§14, plus `--weight-registry 0.5`) at a representative gradient
call:

| term | weight | logged value | weighted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| designed-chain pose RMSD | 1.0 | 24.36 | **24.4** |
| target contact | 0.5 | −8.03 | 4.0 |
| **registry** | **0.5** | **−4.93** | **2.5** |
| designed-chain-to-target PAE | 0.05 | 26.52 | 1.3 |
| target-to-designed-chain PAE | 0.05 | 26.67 | 1.3 |
| AbLang2 | 0.10 | 9.38 | 0.9 |
| pTMEnergy | 0.025 | −2.83 | 0.07 |
| ipTM | 0.025 | 0.18 | 0.004 |

⚠️ **That table is loss magnitude, not gradient norm, and the distinction is
the whole point.** A term's share of the loss value says nothing directly about
its share of the *gradient* after `ClippedGradient` and the entropy-recalibrated
proposal temperature. [§26.5](#265-before-the-large-run) named exactly
this measurement as the prerequisite — per-term gradient norms and pairwise
cosines, and whether the registry gradient survives clipping at a candidate
weight — and said in terms: *"A sweep of `--weight-registry` is deliberately
**not** proposed ahead of that measurement: §§21.3 and 24.5 both show that
weight sweeps on a term whose gradient is being absorbed measure nothing."*

**This screen ran the weight comparison anyway, at 0.5, without that
measurement.** The consequence is the one §26.5 predicted: the null cannot
distinguish

1. the registry restraint not helping placement, from
2. the registry gradient being absorbed by clipping or by the compensating
   temperature change, so that the `_on` and `_off` cells differ in their loss
   but barely in the proposals they actually draw.

Nothing in the saved data separates those. The screen was therefore the wrong
experiment to run first, and the per-term norm measurement — which is a few
gradient calls in `examples/p17_pose_diagnostics.py`, not a cluster batch — is
now a prerequisite for *interpreting a run that has already been paid for*.
This is the same ordering error §23.3 warned about one level up: scaling before
the objective is known to be measurable.

### 27.6 Limits, and a wider noise floor than §21.3 reported

- **Ten seeds, 200 calls, one target.** The plan's own power statement
  (~0.11 ipSAE, ~6 Å) is what every "null" above is relative to. A null at this
  sensitivity does not exclude a real effect of, say, 0.05 ipSAE.
- **Cut off, not converged.** All five cells hit 200/200 score calls, inside
  §21.4's still-improving band. Any of these factors could behave differently
  at a budget where the search is allowed to finish.
- **The WT baseline spread is larger than previously recorded.** The five cells
  measured WT independently in fresh processes:

  | cell | WT mean ipSAE | WT mean pose | WT worst pose | WT target fit |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | `pop_off5` | 0.0026 | 48.33 Å | 52.93 Å | 1.842 Å |
  | `pop_on5` | 0.0000 | 47.46 Å | 52.83 Å | 1.834 Å |
  | `pop_off7` | 0.0000 | **43.72 Å** | 52.44 Å | 1.803 Å |
  | `pop_on7` | 0.0024 | 47.38 Å | 53.04 Å | 1.840 Å |
  | `indep_on5` | 0.0024 | 47.47 Å | 52.51 Å | 1.840 Å |

  Worst-case pose agrees to **0.60 Å** across five processes and target fit to
  0.039 Å, but **mean pose spans 4.61 Å** (43.72–48.33). §21.3 reported a
  0.07 Å cross-process agreement on worst-case pose from *two* arms and called
  it the noise floor for held-out scoring; on five arms the worst-case figure
  holds up and **the mean-pose figure does not**. Mean WT pose is an average
  over three seeds of an unstable non-binding prediction (§3.3), so it is the
  noisier statistic. Every mean-pose contrast in §27.2 should be read against

- **No per-seed significance testing** was performed here; "inside noise" above
  means "smaller than the plan's declared detectable effect and smaller than
  the within-cell spread", not a computed p-value.
- **The three seeding cells are missing**, so the factorial is incomplete and
  the seeding factor is entirely unmeasured.

## 28. Partial continuity: what the harness already has, and what it would cost — 2026-10-07

A question raised in chat: if the gradient is already computed, can *part* of the
optimization be made continuous? This section records the feasibility audit. It
introduces **no result, no code change and no run** — the design options at the
end are options, not a plan, and the ordering in §27.7 is unaffected except where
item 5 already says the seeding stage should not be rerun as built.

**Short answer: yes, and three of the four pieces already exist.** The model's
input is natively a soft sequence, the gradient is already a full `[N, 20]`
Jacobian over that soft representation, and the full path has already executed
125 continuous optimization steps on the cluster. What does not exist is a
rounding rule worth trusting (§27.7 item 5) and any test coverage.

### 28.1 The three pieces that already exist

**The model takes a position-specific scoring matrix, not tokens.** Both GPU
paths in `p17_confidence_search.py` call
`model.model_output(PSSM=x, features=..., recycling_steps=..., sampling_steps=...)`.
A discrete sequence is supplied as a one-hot `PSSM`; it is a special case of an
input the interface already accepts. No new model plumbing is required to feed a
soft sequence.

**The gradient is already the object a continuous step needs.** The harness
builds `x = jax.nn.one_hot(sequence, len(TOKENS))`
(`p17_confidence_search.py:811`) and the evaluator returns
`(value, aux), gradient` with `gradient = ∂loss/∂x`, a full `[N, 20]` array.
`_moves` (`src/mosaic/search.py:95-97`) then **reduces** that array to discrete
per-move scalars —
`delta = gradient[pos, aa] - gradient[pos, sequence[pos]]` — and the search keeps
only the sampled move. The continuous gradient is computed, used once in reduced
form, and discarded. A continuous step would read the array already in memory.

**It has already run end to end.** The three seeding cells of §27.1
(`apgm_on5`, `bc_on5`, `bc_on7`) executed **all 125 continuous optimization steps
through the full gradient path at 64 sampling steps, bf16, one H200 per worker**,
and then failed on an optimizer return-arity defect while unpacking the result.
The compute succeeded; the plumbing did not. That is stronger evidence of
feasibility than any estimate: the expensive part is demonstrated, and
`examples/p17_continuous_seed.py` is the one code path that has ever fed the
model a non-one-hot `PSSM`.

**Memory: one datapoint, not a guarantee.** A continuous step is the same
forward-plus-backward the harness already pays for, and §20.1 established that
the full-path gradient runs at 64 sampling steps. ⚠️ But the three cells that
executed 125 steps were **not** memory-logged in the recovered archive, and a
continuous optimizer carries state the discrete path does not — momentum in
`simplex_APGM`, stage buffers in the annealed variants — while running without
the harness's concurrent population state. So the honest claim is that the stage
**completed on one H200 at 64 steps**, not that memory is unchanged. An earlier
revision of this subsection asserted "no new memory risk to assess"; that
exceeded the evidence and is withdrawn.

### 28.2 Continuity is confined to the proposal, and that is the architecture already chosen

Retention ranks on mean directional-min ipSAE computed from a **separate forward
prediction** (§12.2). Nothing technical prevents predicting from a soft `PSSM`
and reading a confidence metric off it — the interface would accept it. The
constraint is semantic rather than technical: a retained candidate has to be a
discrete sequence for the held-out rescoring protocol to mean anything, for the
edit count to be defined against the start sequence, and for the candidate to be
exportable as a result at all.

So retention stays discrete by design, and any continuous component lives in
**proposal generation**. That is exactly the differentiable-proposal /
non-differentiable-retention split §10.1 committed to in September, and §11.1
records BindCraft2 arriving at the same split independently. Partial continuity
is therefore native to this architecture; full continuity is not available
within it.

### 28.3 Interior inputs are the real exposure, and partial relaxation bounds it

The gradient is evaluated **at a one-hot vertex**. The first continuous step is
therefore well founded — it follows the true local gradient of the composite
objective at a point the model has been fed throughout this project. Subsequent
steps evaluate the model at **interior** matrices the harness has never
supplied.

That is §11.2 item 1 with a concrete location: the objective is differentiable in
a soft sequence, but the network was trained on discrete tokens, and interior
points are an untested extrapolation rather than a validated regime. §4's
poly-Trp featurization note does not help here — it establishes that any residue
*can* be substituted mid-search, not that interior mixtures are interpreted
sensibly.

The consequence is a design preference with a mechanism behind it: **relaxing
*k* rows and leaving the remaining designable rows one-hot keeps the input mostly
in the regime the model has been used in.** Relaxing all 29 maximizes exposure to
the least-tested part of the input space, which is what the current seeding stage
does.

### 28.4 A continuous step addresses one of §21.5's two candidate causes

§21.5 measured that `predicted_loss_delta` carries **no discrimination among the
moves the proposal selects**: P(ipSAE improves | the delta predicted an
improvement) equalled the base rate in all four seeds. That statistic is about a
particular object — a **Taylor-1 extrapolation of the effect of a complete
discrete substitution**, taken from a gradient evaluated at a different point
(§5's third known issue: "a long linear extrapolation across a non-linear loss").

A continuous step is the opposite regime. It is small, and it recomputes the true
gradient at each new point rather than extrapolating from the old one. PPDE's own
caution, recorded in §11.2 item 2, is that **"accumulation of errors from
first-order Taylor approximations along a long path"** degrades a sampler — which
is an argument for short steps with fresh gradients.

⚠️ **Inference, and it covers only one of two candidate causes.** §21.5's null
has **two** possible sources and that section says explicitly that it cannot
separate them: a bad first-order approximation (§11.2 item 2) and the
proxy-to-objective gap (§11.2 item 3) — `predicted_loss_delta` predicts the
*composite proposal loss* while the realized delta is measured on *mean
directional-min ipSAE*, which are different objectives by construction (§19.6).

Continuous steps address the first and do **nothing** about the second. If the
null is driven by the objective mismatch, a continuous proposal inherits it
whole: smaller steps along a direction that does not track the retention metric
still do not track the retention metric. So this subsection does not establish
that partial continuity sidesteps §21.5's failure; it establishes that it
sidesteps *one* of the two explanations, and §21.5 does not say which is
operative.

An earlier revision of this subsection claimed continuity "doesn't inherit the
failure §21.5 measured; it sidesteps it", without that split. That was too
strong and is corrected here. Separating the two causes needs the realized
proposal loss logged for every scored candidate, which is §27.7's and §21.8
item 4's outstanding logging change, not an experiment.

### 28.5 Four degrees of partial continuity

Cheapest first. Cost units are the measured ones from §21.5: a gradient call is
~11.1 s, a score call ~4.9 s.

| # | Mechanism | Cost | New machinery |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Continuous refinement of one proposal.** From the current parent, take *j* small simplex steps restricted to the designable rows, round, and offer the result as **one additional candidate** alongside the ordinary single substitutions. Retention decides as usual. | *j* gradient calls per proposal | A rounding rule; nothing else |
| 2 | **Masked continuous over *k* frozen positions.** Select the *k* positions from the existing saliency matrix, relax only those rows, hold the other designable rows one-hot. | Same order as 1 | Row mask (exists as `designable_idx`) plus a rounding rule |
| 3 | **Full two-stage** — relax all 29 rows, project to the budget, hand a discrete start to the harness. Built, as `p17_continuous_seed.py`. | One relaxed run per search | Exists, and carries the three defects in §27.7 item 5 |
| 4 | **Logit-space MCMC with a Metropolis–Hastings correction**, in the shape the literature review records for RSS: Langevin walks plus model-informed jumps, never discretizing during search. | Largest | A differentiable surrogate for the sequence model and a reversible kernel; weeks |

Mechanisms 1 and 2 leave retention, the objective, the budget accounting and the
held-out protocol untouched, which is why they are cheap to interpret as well as
cheap to run: they change only which candidates get offered.

### 28.6 The masked variant narrows the rounding problem rather than removing it

This is the reason to prefer mechanism 2 over mechanism 3, and it is structural
rather than a matter of degree.

§27.7 item 5 lists three defects in the built seeding stage. The second —
`project_to_budget` consuming `argmax` of a truncated relaxed optimum, ranked by
margins in a saturating probability space — **exists only because all 29 rows are
relaxed and most of them must then be discarded.** If exactly *k* rows are
relaxed with *k* equal to the edit budget, every relaxed row is a substitution by
construction: there is no top-*k* selection, no margin ranking, and no
truncation.

⚠️ **Removing the truncation does not remove the discretization.** The *k*
relaxed rows still have to be rounded to one-hot, and a soft solution can lose
its advantage in that rounding whatever its support size — which is exactly what
RSO measured when one-hot-encoding its relaxed result gave unusable designs
([note](literature_review/continuous_relaxation_and_the_discretization_gap.md)
Q2). What the masked variant does is **narrow the rounding problem from a
29-row selection-plus-rounding operation to a *k*-row rounding operation**, and
remove the margin ranking that has no published validation. An earlier revision
said the failure surface "is not mitigated, it is absent"; that is true of the
*truncation* and false of the *discretization*, and the distinction is the one
that decides whether the idea works.

It also splits the work along the line §22.5 *suggests*. ⚠️ Position selection
being reliable is **not established**: three of four seeds edited position 108 and
§20.9 found position 101 drawing 31 proposals across nine accepted identities, but
§22.5's own correction records that winners cannot reconstruct a trajectory, that
the 5-edit rung's event log has never been read, and that the position/identity
asymmetry is "a frequency statement, and a weak one at this sample size". The
split below is therefore built on a hypothesis about where the gradient is
informative, not on a measured property. Mechanism 2 uses the gradient
for the former and continuous optimization for the latter, which is the same
division of labour the literature review records for RSS's jump kernel, reached
from a different direction:
[`literature_review/continuous_relaxation_and_the_discretization_gap.md`](literature_review/continuous_relaxation_and_the_discretization_gap.md) Q4.

The first and third defects of §27.7 item 5 still apply and still need settling:
the relaxed variable must be **initialized at the parent** rather than from noise,
and the budget must be accounted once rather than twice.

### 28.7 What this does not address

- **Retention is still site-agnostic.** §25 localized the residual failure as
  registry at the correct site, and §27.2 found the registry term null at the one
  weight tried. A better proposal mechanism reaches the inverted mode too, and
  §21.4 measured that more evaluations buy confidence rather than placement. **A
  continuous component is an identity-selection and budget-traversal change, not
  a placement fix**, and should not be reported as one. §24.6's boundary is
  unchanged.
- **§26.5's measurement still gates the interpretation of everything.** Per-term
  gradient norms and pairwise cosines remain unrun (§27.7 item 1). A continuous
  component adds steps along a composite direction whose per-term balance has
  never been measured.
- **No test coverage.** Nothing in `tests/` references `p17_continuous_seed.py`
  or `project_to_budget`. Three cells already lost a night to a defect a shape
  assertion catches.
- **Nothing here is evidence that the gradient carries usable information.** The
  random-proposal arm (§21.8 item 1) remains the only experiment that attributes
  any of this result to the gradient, and it is still unrun.


### 28.8 Status

A feasibility audit against the code, plus design options. No code was written,
no run was launched, and no number in this document changed. **This section makes
the idea better defined; it does not make it validated**, and nothing here is
evidence that partial continuity has an advantage.

The sequencing in §27.7 stands, with one correction to how Test B was described
there and in an earlier revision of this subsection. ⚠️ Test B reads the rank the
correct residue received **at the gradient calls that actually occurred**. That
tells you whether the discrete first-order ranking surfaced the answer at those
points; it does **not** establish whether identity information is available
elsewhere along a trajectory, continuous or otherwise. So Test B discriminates
between "ranked high and not sampled" and "ranked low at these points" — which is
enough to choose between top-*k* enumeration and a different proposal mechanism —
but it cannot settle whether continuous exploration has value. Calling it a gate
on "whether the identity information is in the gradient at all" was too strong.

The decisive readout for any of this remains an advantage at the **final,
feasible, discrete** candidate under the original constraints — not a lower
relaxed loss, not successful execution, and not a more sophisticated search.

## 29. The attribution screen, planned — 2026-10-07

**This section records a plan, not a result.** Nothing in it has been run, and
the launcher it describes has not been syntax-checked or dry-run (§29.7). It is
written to be checked before compute is committed.

Entry point: `examples/run_p17_attribution_screen.sh`, modelled on the §27
launcher so that only the cell definitions are new.

### 29.1 The question, and why §21.5 cannot reach it

§21.5 paired every proposal's `predicted_loss_delta` with the realized ipSAE
change and found **P(ipSAE improves | the delta predicted an improvement) equal
to the base rate in all four seeds** — 91–96% of sampled moves were predicted to
improve, 9–16% did. That looks like a verdict on the gradient and is not one.
The proposal distribution is `softmax(−delta/temp)` over the feasible move set,
so by the time a move is logged it has already been selected for having a
favourable delta. The statistic is therefore **conditional on the pool the
gradient chose**, and it shows no discrimination *within* that pool while saying
nothing about whether the pool beats a uniformly drawn one. §21.6 records the
same gap, and §24.4 lists this arm as never executed.

So the question this screen asks is the one no run in this project has asked:
**does sampling from the gradient-weighted pool beat sampling uniformly from the
same feasible move set, at equal compute?** It is open in both directions. §21.8
item 1 called it the cheapest result that would reframe the project, and after
§27 closed the registry term, the policy choice and the edit budget, it is the
last untested first-order assumption the design rests on.

### 29.2 The design

Four cells, JN.1 as the only input, twenty search seeds each, eighty workers:

| cell | proposal source | `target_entropy` | policy |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `grad_pop` | gradient-weighted | 0.6 | population |
| `grad_indep` | gradient-weighted | 0.6 | independent |
| `unif_pop` | **uniform** | **1.0** | population |
| `unif_indep` | **uniform** | **1.0** | independent |

**The uniform arm needs no new code path, and that is worth stating precisely
because it is checkable.** `_proposal_distribution` in `src/mosaic/search.py`
contains `if scale == 0 or target_entropy == 1: return np.full(count, 1 / count),
1.0, None`, and `SearchConfig.__post_init__` validates
`0 < target_entropy <= 1`. So `--target-entropy 1.0` is an in-range value that
draws uniformly over the move set `_moves` already builds — the same designable
mask, the same edit cap, the same revert-and-exchange rules. **The only
difference between a `grad_` and a `unif_` cell is which move gets sampled.**

Pinned identically in all four cells: `edit_budget` 5, `weight_registry` 0.0,
200 score calls, 200 gradient-call ceiling, 64 sampling steps, bf16, stable
aggregation, `acceptance_temperature` 0.02, held-out structural seeds
101/102/103, `--save-saliency` on.

⚠️ **One asymmetry cannot be pinned, and it is the manipulation itself.**
`target_entropy` is 0.6 in the gradient cells and 1.0 in the uniform cells.
§21.7's standing rule is that every future comparison pins entropy and
acceptance temperature across cells; this is the single case where that is
impossible, since the entropy *is* the variable. Recorded here rather than
passed over. Nothing else differs.

### 29.3 What it matches, and what it therefore cannot answer

Both arms still compute the gradient; the uniform arm simply does not sample
from it. The cells are therefore matched on score calls, gradient calls **and
wall-clock**, which makes this a clean one-variable comparison of the proposal
distribution with no compute confound.

⚠️ **It does not test whether the gradient earns its cost.** §21.5 measured
`gradient_seconds` at **1196 of 2664 s per worker — 45% of wall-clock** — so
skipping the gradient entirely would buy roughly **1.8× more candidates scored
in the same time**. That is a different question, and answering it needs a
bypass at the single gradient call site (`src/mosaic/search.py:320`), where
substituting a constant array makes every delta zero, hits the `scale == 0`
branch, and yields the same uniform distribution while leaving `gradient_calls`
at zero. **That bypass is not in this screen and has not been written or
tested.** A null here must not be reported as "the gradient does not earn its
45%"; it would mean "gradient-weighted sampling did not beat uniform sampling at
equal compute", which is weaker and more specific.

### 29.4 Why policy is the second factor rather than a repeat

§27.2 measured independent against population at ten seeds and found it null —
0.470 against 0.529 ipSAE, 27.4 against 30.5 Å pose, both inside that screen's
own ~0.11 / ~6 Å sensitivity — which reversed §24.9's default. Re-running policy
alone would therefore spend compute on a settled question.

The hypothesis here is an **interaction**, and it is cheap because both proposal
arms must run regardless: every arm in §27 used gradient proposals, and if all
parents climb the same gradient then the population layer has little diversity
to preserve, which would itself explain a null. Crossing policy with proposal
source tests that. §21.6 found the population holding genuinely distinct edit
sets (6–10 Hamming apart out of a maximum of 10), so the machinery works; what
is unmeasured is whether it contributes.

### 29.5 Power, stated as a limit

§23.2's variances give roughly **0.11 ipSAE** sensitivity at ten seeds. Scaling
by √(10/20) puts twenty seeds at roughly **0.078 ipSAE**.

- The **proposal factor** is a whole-distribution change and is the effect this
  design can resolve.
- ⚠️ The **policy main effect probably cannot be resolved.** §27.2 measured it
  at 0.059, which sits below 0.078. Policy here is a screen for an interaction,
  not a settled comparison, and a 0.03 gap is not a result.

Cost, from §21.5's measured per-call times and §27's realized call counts
(~45 gradient calls per 200 score calls): about 980 s of scoring plus about
500 s of gradient per worker, so **~25 min per worker**, 80 workers over 8 GPUs
≈ **4–5 h**. That fits one night with headroom, which is why twenty seeds rather
than ten.

### 29.6 What is deliberately excluded, and why

| Excluded | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Continuous seeding** | Not ready. §27.7 item 5 and §28 list three defects that would each make the result ambiguous — noise initialization, `argmax` of a budget-truncated relaxed optimum, and a double-counted budget — plus no test coverage for the stage or its projection. §28.9 also records that continuity's advantage is a hypothesis about *how* to use the gradient, which is downstream of whether the gradient has anything to use. That is what this screen establishes. |
| **The registry term** (`weight_registry` 0.0) | §27.2 found it null at 0.5 and §27.5 shows that null is uninterpretable until the per-term gradient-norm measurement is made. Putting an uninterpreted term into an attribution experiment confounds the thing being attributed. |
| **Edit budget 7** | Closed by §27.3: no confidence gain, and the flipped-pose rate rose to 26–27 of 33 against 18–23 at budget 5. |

| **Per-term gradient norms and cosines** | Still outstanding (§§26.5, 27.7 item 1), still blocking the interpretation of §27's registry null, and `p17_pose_diagnostics.py` does not yet report per term. It is minutes of GPU and should run before or alongside, not instead. |
| **Test B** | Free and GPU-free — the rank the correct residue received at the damaged positions on the 5-edit Alpha rung. Should be read tonight regardless; §28.8 records what it can and cannot settle. |

## 30. The attribution screen executed: the gradient moves pose and not confidence — 2026-10-07

The screen planned in §29 ran on the cluster, all four cells passed, and the
result splits cleanly along the two readouts. **Interface confidence: null.
Target-aligned pose: 5.1σ.** The CAPRI audit in §30.2 then grades the pose gain
and finds it real but rigid-body, with every structure still incorrect. §30.3
records a CPU-only synthetic experiment that was run the same day and that
changes the ordering of what should run next.

Run: `p17_attribution_screen_20261007_082205`, 80 workers, 4 cells × 20 search
seeds.

### 30.1 The manipulation did what the plan said it would, and compute was matched

§29.2 claimed `--target-entropy 1.0` would make `_proposal_distribution` return
an exactly uniform distribution with no new code path. That is checkable from
the event logs and it checks out:

| cell | proposals logged | entropy | proposal temperature |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `grad_pop` | 5,177 | all 0.6 | finite, median 0.027 |
| `grad_indep` | 5,205 | all 0.6 | finite, median 0.025 |
| `unif_pop` | 4,001 | **all exactly 1.0** | **null** |
| `unif_indep` | 3,998 | **all exactly 1.0** | **null** |

Not one proposal in either uniform cell carried a temperature, and not one in
either gradient cell failed to. The manipulation held on every move.

Compute matched to the precision the design needed:

| quantity | all four cells |
|---|---|
| score calls per seed | 200.0, exactly, in every cell |
| distinct evaluations per cell | ~3,980 |
| gradient calls per seed | 56–63, **in every cell including the uniform ones** |
| wall-clock per seed | 27.9–29.1 min |

The third row is what makes the uniform arms valid §21.5 controls rather than
cheaper runs: they computed the gradient and discarded it. §29.3's matched-compute
claim therefore holds as written, and so does its warning — this says nothing
about whether the gradient earns its 45% of wall-clock, because both arms paid
for it.

**One finding the plan did not anticipate: a cache-hit asymmetry.** The gradient
cells re-proposed an already-evaluated candidate on 23.1–23.5% of attempts
against 0.5% in the uniform cells. Sharpening the proposal distribution
concentrates it, and a concentrated distribution keeps rediscovering its own
favourites — so the gradient arms spent roughly 1,200 attempts per cell on
candidates they had already scored. That is a real inefficiency of the mechanism
and it **cuts against** the gradient, which makes the result below stronger
rather than weaker: the gradient won the pose comparison while wasting a quarter
of its proposals.

### 30.2 The two readouts answer differently, and the pose answer survives a CAPRI audit

Marginals over the proposal factor, 40 non-WT winners per arm:

| readout | gradient | uniform | difference | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean ipSAE | 0.5666 ± 0.0282 | 0.5333 ± 0.0248 | +0.033 | 0.9σ — **null** |
| mean pose (Å) | 29.26 ± 2.21 | 50.92 ± 3.58 | **−21.66** | **5.1σ** |

The ipSAE gap of 0.033 sits well inside §29.5's stated 0.078 sensitivity, so it
is not a result in either direction. The pose gap is not close to the noise
floor.

**The uniform arm does not move pose at all.** Its per-cell means are 47.90 and
53.94 Å against a WT start measured in the same run at ~47.2 Å (the
`unif_indep` WT baseline: mean ipSAE 0.0000, worst pose 53.03 Å, target fit
1.82 Å). Uniform search raises confidence from 0.000 to 0.533 while leaving the
designed chain where it began, or worse. That is the sharpest statement of
§19.6's concern this project has produced: **the retention metric can be
climbed without the pose moving**, so confidence and placement are separable in
practice and not only in principle.

⚠️ **The gradient improves the distribution, not the ceiling.** Best pose was
~12.2–12.6 Å in three of the four cells, uniform cells included. Whatever the
gradient contributes, it is not access to better optima — it is that more of its
candidates land near the ones both arms can reach.

**The CAPRI audit** (`examples/p17_capri_audit.py`, new, DockQ CLI installed
isolated via `uv tool install`) grades all 252 held-out structures. Aggregated
**per candidate**, which is the independent unit — the three held-out seeds of
one candidate are correlated and are not three measurements (n = 20 per cell):

| metric | gradient | uniform | |
|---|---|---|---|
| LRMSD (Å) | 29.53 ± 2.25 | 51.35 ± 3.62 | 5.1σ |
| iRMSD (Å) | 12.52 ± 0.77 | 17.69 ± 0.84 | 4.5σ |
| DockQ | 0.0556 ± 0.0068 | 0.0283 ± 0.0051 | 3.2σ |
| fnat | 0.0292 ± 0.0067 | 0.0143 ± 0.0042 | 1.9σ |

Two things follow. First, **the effect is graded across the metrics, strongest
on the most rigid-body one and weakest on the most contact-specific one** —
5.1σ on ligand RMSD, 1.9σ on fraction of native contacts. The gradient is
moving the designed chain to roughly the right place and is barely forming the
right contacts. Second, **LRMSD reproduces the pose readout almost exactly**
(29.53 / 51.35 against 29.26 / 50.92), which settles a standing ambiguity:
"target-aligned pose RMSD" is ligand RMSD in all but name.

⚠️ **All 240 non-WT structures are CAPRI incorrect.** Best DockQ anywhere is
**0.174** — `grad_pop` candidate 16, seed 101, 8 of 59 native contacts —
against an acceptable threshold of 0.23. §27.4's audit found 0.185 over 165
structures; this one found 0.174 over 240. More compute, more seeds and a
measured proposal advantage did not produce a single acceptable interface. The
pose gain is movement within the incorrect class.

**Policy was null for the third time**, consistent with §29.5's prediction that
it would not resolve: §24.9 made it a default on reasoning, §27.2 measured it
null at ten seeds, and it did not separate here either. §29.4's interaction
hypothesis — that population has no diversity to preserve when all parents climb
the same gradient — is not supported, but the design was a screen for it and not
powered to settle it.

### 30.3 A CPU synthetic experiment relocates the continuous-stage problem from rounding to truncation

Before committing a cluster night to the continuous ladder of §28.5, the
question "does relax-and-round beat discrete search" was asked where the answer
can be checked exactly. `examples/p17_rounding_gap_synthetic.py` (new, CPU only)
builds a frustrated Potts model, takes the **multilinear extension** as its
relaxation — exact at one-hot vertices, so the relaxed and discrete objectives
are literally the same function — and carries the real `EditBudget` hinge at the
real weight of 5.0. It evaluates every candidate at a one-hot vertex exactly as
the harness does, and it calls the **real `run_gradient_search`** for its
discrete arm.

**There is no soft-to-hard rounding gap.** The relaxed optimum placed
**0.977–0.999** of its probability mass on a single residue at every coupling
strength and hinge setting tried. The relaxation converges to a vertex on its
own; nothing is lost in the rounding that §28.6 and RSO's result warned about.

**The failure is truncation, and the hinge is what controls it.** The relaxed
optimum wants more edits than the budget allows, and `project_to_budget`
discards the surplus:

| coupling strength | edits the relaxed optimum wanted (budget 5) |
|---|---|
| ≤ 0.3 | 5.0 — hinge holds |
| 1.0 | 7.0 |
| 3.0 | 10.5 |
| any, hinge off | 13–18, costing 57–66% of the objective after projection |

So the hinge prevents truncation exactly when it is strong relative to the rest
of the objective, and loses when it is not. **Which regime a real run sits in is
set by the hinge's gradient against the other terms' — which is §26.5's
unmeasured quantity.**

**Against an independent optimum, discrete search is already at the ceiling.**
An earlier version of this experiment compared the hand-off against "best
known", which was the same discrete search with more calls — circular, and
corrected by enumerating the feasible set exactly.

At budget 2, all **147,117** feasible sequences enumerated, 30 instances:

| arm | % of exhaustive optimum | reached it exactly |
|---|---|---|
| discrete search, no relaxation | — | **30 / 30** |
| relax-and-round alone | 94.9% | — |
| relax then hand off to discrete | 100% | 30 / 30 |

At budget 5, 30 instances (SEM 0.855, which is the number that matters):

| arm | % of reference |
|---|---|
| **discrete search, no relaxation** | **99.9%** (reached the reference 28/30) |
| relax 2 steps, no search | 41.0% |
| relax 2 steps then discrete | 99.8% |
| relax 5 steps, no search | 95.6% |
| relax 5 steps then discrete | 99.5% |

**Every hand-off came in marginally worse than discrete search alone**, and
every difference is far inside the 0.855 SEM — so the honest reading is that
they are indistinguishable, with no hint of an advantage. The conclusion is
narrow and worth stating precisely: **where discrete search already saturates
its budget, continuous seeding has no headroom to add.** It does not show that
relaxation cannot help; it shows there is nothing to win in this regime, and
that the regime is set by the budget relative to the problem size.

⚠️ **This is a synthetic Potts model, not OpenDDE.** It shares the budget
hinge, the projection code path and the real search, and it does not share the
objective, the dimensionality or the predictor's response to interior inputs.
It is a prediction about the real run, not a substitute for it.
